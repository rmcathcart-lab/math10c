Solving Right Triangles Completely

Every side and every angle. Besides the right angle, two known measurements are enough. Pick the tool that matches what is given: the Pythagorean Theorem, SOH CAH TOA, an inverse ratio or the 90° angle sum. Take each unknown from the original given data, and round only at the end.

I can…
  • choose the tool that uses exactly what is given: two sides → a side (Pythagoras), a side and an angle → a side (SOH CAH TOA), two sides → an angle (\sin^{-1}, \cos^{-1}, \tan^{-1}), one acute angle → the other (90^\circ- the angle)
  • find one length two ways and explain why a rounded value fed into one route makes the answers disagree
  • explain what it means to solve a right triangle (all three sides and all three angles) and why exactly two more pieces of information are needed
  • solve a right triangle from one side and one acute angle, or from two sides, in a diagram or a real situation, and check it with the Pythagorean Theorem and the angle sum
M4 Develop and apply the primary trigonometric ratios (sine, cosine, tangent) to solve problems that involve right triangles.
Warm-up

The Leaning Tower

Pisa’s famous tower is about 56 m long and leans about 4° from upright.

Solve the tower’s triangle
Tower (the slanted side)56 m
Lean from upright4^\circ
Angle with the ground86^\circ
How far the top is shifted3.9 m
Height of the top55.9 m
1
If it stood upright, how far would the top be shifted?
0 m: no lean, no shift.
2
In the right triangle, which side is the tower?
The hypotenuse. The right angle is where the plumb line meets the horizontal through the top, so the tower is opposite it.
3
What angle does the tower make with the ground?
\boldsymbol{86^\circ}. The plumb line meets the ground at 90^\circ and the lean takes 4^\circ of it: 90^\circ-4^\circ=86^\circ. (The two acute angles of a right triangle add to 90^\circ.)
!
The big one. It’s “only” 4^\circ. About how far sideways is the top shifted? Guess first.
A about 40 cm
B about 2.5 m
C about 3.9 m
C: about 3.9 m. The shift is opposite the 4^\circ angle and the tower is the hypotenuse: \text{shift}=56\sin4^\circ\approx56(0.0698)\approx3.9 m. Roughly the length of a car.
A is the gut feeling: “4^\circ is tiny, so the shift must be tiny.” Over 56 m a small angle adds up. B is \tfrac{4}{90}\times56: treating the shift as growing in step with the angle.
+
Early finishers. Before repair work that finished in 2001, the lean was about 5.5^\circ. How big was the shift then?
56\sin5.5^\circ\approx5.4 m.

Challenge a neighbour: tilt a pencil 4^\circ. Can they even see the lean? (At pencil size, about 18 cm, the tip moves only about 1.3 cm.)

Choosing the Right Tool

Two known measurements determine a third. Two sides with no angle involved call for the Pythagorean Theorem; a side paired with an acute angle, or two sides with the angle unknown, call for SOH, CAH or TOA. Name the given sides relative to the angle in use (opposite, adjacent, hypotenuse), then pick the tool that uses exactly what is given.

Which tool? Ask two questions

First: is the missing piece an angle or a side? Then: what do I already have? Name the sides from the angle in use before you write a ratio. If the unknown is on top of the fraction, multiply; on the bottom, divide; if the angle is unknown, run the ratio backwards with \sin^{-1}, \cos^{-1} or \tan^{-1}.

Inverse ratios on the TI-30XIIS. 2ndsin is \sin^{-1} (and the same for cos and tan). The calculator must be in DEG mode. Like sin, it opens a bracket: 2ndtan5.8÷11.4)ENTER

One unknown: match the tool to the given pair

In each case, calculate the indicated measure. Give angles to the nearest degree and sides to the nearest tenth.

(a)
Both given sides are legs: from x^\circ, 5.8 is opposite and 11.4 is adjacent \Rightarrow TOA.
\tan x^\circ=\dfrac{5.8}{11.4}=0.50877\ldots
x^\circ=\tan^{-1}(0.50877)=26.9657^\circ\approx\boldsymbol{27^\circ}
(b)

(c)
From 62^\circ: x is opposite and 33.5 is the hypotenuse \Rightarrow SOH.
\sin 62^\circ=\dfrac{x}{33.5}
x=33.5\sin 62^\circ=33.5(0.88295)=29.5787\ldots\approx\boldsymbol{29.6}
(d)

\cos x^\circ=\dfrac{8.1}{13.6}=0.59559\ldots
x^\circ=\cos^{-1}(0.59559)=53.4454^\circ\approx\boldsymbol{53^\circ}

Two Routes to the Same Side

When a side can be reached either by the Pythagorean Theorem or by a trigonometric ratio, the two routes agree only if no rounded value is fed into them. The next example computes one length both ways and compares.

Example 2

The same length, two ways

A radio antenna is 14 m tall. It is supported by a guy wire 22 m long running from the top of the antenna to a stake in the ground.

(a) Calculate, to the nearest degree, the angle \theta^\circ between the wire and the ground.
From \theta^\circ: the antenna (14) is opposite and the wire (22) is the hypotenuse \Rightarrow SOH.
\sin\theta^\circ=\dfrac{14}{22}=0.63636\ldots \Rightarrow \theta^\circ=\sin^{-1}(0.63636)=39.5212^\circ\approx\boldsymbol{40^\circ}
(b) Calculate the distance, to the nearest tenth of a metre, between the stake and the foot of the antenna, using
  i) the Pythagorean Theorem
The stake-to-foot distance is the third side, and both other sides are given exactly:
\text{base}=\sqrt{22^2-14^2}=\sqrt{484-196} =\sqrt{288}=16.9706\ldots\approx\boldsymbol{17.0\text{ m}}

  ii) trigonometry, using your rounded angle from (a).
\text{base}=22\cos 40^\circ=22(0.76604)=16.8530\ldots\approx\boldsymbol{16.9\text{ m}}
Think The two answers disagree. Which one is right, and why does the other miss?
17.0 m is right. ii) is built on the rounded 40^\circ rather than on 39.5212^\circ. Carrying the angle unrounded gives 22\cos(39.5212^\circ)=16.9706, matching i) exactly. Work from the original given data and round only at the end.

What It Means to “Solve” a Triangle

Solving a right triangle means finding the length of every side and the measure of every angle. One angle, the right angle, is already known, so exactly two more independent pieces of information are needed: either one side and one acute angle, or two sides. From those, the Pythagorean Theorem, SOH CAH TOA, and the fact that the two acute angles of a right triangle add to 90^\circ deliver everything else, applying whichever tool fits the information available at each step.

Take every unknown from the given data

Take every unknown from the original given measurements wherever possible, not from a value you have just rounded. A rounded intermediate result carries its error into everything built on it, as Example 2 shows. Carry full calculator precision through the work and round only the final answer.

Solve it: a checklist of six

Three sides and three angles. Tick off what is given, then take the missing pieces one at a time: ask the flowchart which tool fits, work it from the given data, and fill it in. Finish with a consistency check: the Pythagorean Theorem and the angle sum.

Example 3

Solve from one angle and one side

Solve the given triangle. Give all angles to the nearest degree and all sides to the nearest tenth.

Solve triangle ABC.
Angle C=90^\circ-48^\circ=\boldsymbol{42^\circ}.
BC is opposite 48^\circ and AB=25 is adjacent \Rightarrow TOA:
\tan 48^\circ=\dfrac{BC}{25} \Rightarrow BC=25\tan 48^\circ =25(1.11061)=27.7653\ldots\approx\boldsymbol{27.8}
AC is the hypotenuse with AB=25 adjacent \Rightarrow CAH, taken from the given 25 and 48^\circ, never from the rounded BC:
\cos 48^\circ=\dfrac{25}{AC} \Rightarrow AC=\dfrac{25}{\cos 48^\circ} =\dfrac{25}{0.66913}=37.3619\ldots\approx\boldsymbol{37.4}
Check: 90^\circ+48^\circ+42^\circ=180^\circ ✓ and \sqrt{25^2+27.7653^2}=37.3619 ✓
Think AC is on the bottom of \cos 48^\circ=\dfrac{25}{AC}. Do you multiply or divide? How can you tell your answer is not 25\cos 48^\circ?
Divide: AC=25\div\cos 48^\circ. Multiplying gives 25\cos48^\circ\approx16.7, which is shorter than the leg AB=25. The hypotenuse is always the longest side, so 16.7 can’t be it.

Example 4

Solve from two sides

Solve triangle DEF. Give all sides to the nearest tenth and all angles to the nearest degree.

Solve triangle DEF.
DF is the hypotenuse, and both legs are given, so use the Pythagorean Theorem:
DF=\sqrt{DE^2+EF^2}=\sqrt{8.6^2+6.1^2} =\sqrt{73.96+37.21}=\sqrt{111.17}=10.5437\ldots\approx\boldsymbol{10.5}
Angle F: DE=8.6 is opposite and EF=6.1 is adjacent \Rightarrow TOA:
\tan F=\dfrac{8.6}{6.1}=1.40984\ldots \Rightarrow F=\tan^{-1}(1.40984)=54.6518^\circ\approx\boldsymbol{55^\circ}
Angle D=90^\circ-54.6518^\circ=35.3482^\circ\approx\boldsymbol{35^\circ} (from the unrounded F, not from 55^\circ).
Check: 90^\circ+54.6518^\circ+35.3482^\circ=180^\circ ✓ and 8.6^2+6.1^2=111.17=10.5437^2 ✓
Think Before calculating: which acute angle, D or F, should be the larger one? How do you know?
\angle F. The bigger angle faces the longer side: F faces DE=8.6, while D faces EF=6.1. And indeed 55^\circ>35^\circ.

From words to a triangle

An applied problem hides the triangle in a sentence. Find the right angle first (a wall meets the ground, a tower stands on level ground), name the vertices, and decide which side each measurement is. “The angle with the ground” and “the angle with the wall” are two different corners.

Example 5

Solve an applied triangle

A 6.5 m ladder leans against a vertical wall with its foot 2.1 m from the base of the wall. Let W be the point where the wall meets the ground, F the foot of the ladder, and T the point where the top of the ladder touches the wall. Solve triangle WFT: find the height WT the ladder reaches on the wall, the angle the ladder makes with the ground at F, and the angle the ladder makes with the wall at T. Give the side to the nearest tenth of a metre and both angles to the nearest degree.

Solve triangle WFT.
WT from the Pythagorean Theorem, using the two given lengths:
WT=\sqrt{FT^2-WF^2}=\sqrt{6.5^2-2.1^2} =\sqrt{42.25-4.41}=\sqrt{37.84}=6.1514\ldots\approx\boldsymbol{6.2\text{ m}}
Angle F (ladder to ground): WF=2.1 is adjacent and FT=6.5 is the hypotenuse \Rightarrow CAH:
\cos F=\dfrac{2.1}{6.5}=0.32308\ldots \Rightarrow F=\cos^{-1}(0.32308)=71.1509^\circ\approx\boldsymbol{71^\circ}
Angle T (ladder to wall) =90^\circ-71.1509^\circ=18.8491^\circ\approx\boldsymbol{19^\circ}, taken from the unrounded F; starting from 71^\circ would give 19^\circ here but will not always.
Check: 2.1^2+6.1514^2=42.25=6.5^2 ✓ and 90^\circ+71.1509^\circ+18.8491^\circ=180^\circ ✓

Checks catch mistakes

A solved triangle must hang together: the angles add to 180^\circ, the hypotenuse is the longest side, the sides fit a^2+b^2=c^2, and the bigger acute angle faces the longer leg. Each check catches a different slip, so use more than one.

!
The angle sum can’t catch everything. If the two acute angles are swapped, they still add to 90^\circ. Ask which angle faces the longer leg.
✓

Key ideas

Match the tool to the pair. Two sides → side: Pythagoras. Side + angle → side: SOH CAH TOA. Two sides → angle: \sin^{-1}, \cos^{-1}, \tan^{-1}. Angle → angle: 90^\circ- it.
Solve = all three sides and all three angles. The right angle is free, so two more pieces are needed: a side and an acute angle, or two sides.
Work from the given data and round only at the end. A rounded value fed into the next step carries its error (Example 2: 16.9 m instead of 17.0 m).
Check with unrounded values: a^2+b^2=c^2 and angles adding to 180^\circ. The hypotenuse is the longest side, and the bigger acute angle faces the longer leg.
