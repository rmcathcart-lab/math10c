Trig Ratios: Calculator Values and Angle Patterns

Evaluating the ratios in degree mode, and how each ratio behaves as the angle opens from 0^\circ to 90^\circ. Fix the hypotenuse at 1 and open the angle: the opposite side climbs, the adjacent side shrinks, and every pattern can be read straight off the picture. (Finding an angle from a ratio comes in Lesson 4.)

I can…
  • evaluate \sin, \cos and \tan of an angle on the TI-30XIIS in degree mode, round to four decimal places, and name the side ratio each value stands for
  • explain with SOH CAH TOA why sine increases and cosine decreases as the angle opens, and what the tangent does
  • compare two ratios without a calculator, using the 45^\circ benchmark
  • state \sin, \cos and \tan of 0^\circ and 90^\circ and describe the pattern in between
  • Extension describe \tan x^\circ near 90^\circ, and explain why \sin x^\circ=\cos(90-x)^\circ
M4 Develop and apply the primary trigonometric ratios (sine, cosine, tangent) to solve problems that involve right triangles.
Warm-up

The 4-to-1 Ladder Rule

Safety sticker: “For every 4 m up the wall, put the foot 1 m out.”

1
A ladder reaches 12 m up a wall. How far out should its foot be?
3 m. 12 m up is three groups of “4 up”, so three groups of “1 out”.
2
Which of ladders A–D follow the rule?
A (4:1) and C (8:2=4:1). B is 6:3=2:1, too shallow (it could slide out). D is 5:0.5=10:1, too steep (it could tip backward).
3
Two of the ladders are different sizes but both follow the rule. Do they lean at the same angle? Why?
Yes. Both have up \div out =4, so C is just A scaled by 2: similar triangles, same angles.
!
The big one. A ladder that follows the 4-to-1 rule makes what angle with the ground? Guess first.
A about 14^\circ
B 22.5^\circ
C 72^\circ
D about 76^\circ
D: about 76^\circ. Relative to the angle \theta at the foot, “up” is opposite and “out” is adjacent, so \tan\theta=\frac{4}{1}=4. Nobody can “see” that angle, so hunt for it with the calculator (degree mode!): \tan72^\circ\approx3.08, \tan76^\circ\approx4.01, \tan80^\circ\approx5.67. So \theta\approx76^\circ.
A (about 14^\circ) is out over up: the angle at the top, between the ladder and the wall (14^\circ+76^\circ=90^\circ). B is “\tfrac14 of 90^\circ” and C is “\tfrac45 of 90^\circ”: both assume the angle grows in step with the ratio. It doesn’t, and that is what this lesson is about.
+
Early finishers. Some safety guides measure the 4 along the ladder instead: foot out =\tfrac14 of the ladder’s length. Which ratio is that, and about what angle?
Out is adjacent and the ladder is the hypotenuse: \cos\theta=0.25. \cos75^\circ\approx0.26 and \cos76^\circ\approx0.24, so about 75.5^\circ. (The \cos^{-1} key does this directly in Lesson 4.)

Hands up if you’ve set up a ladder. Show the rule’s angle with your arm!

1

Evaluating Trigonometric Ratios with a Calculator

For a reference angle x^\circ in a right triangle, SOH CAH TOA gives
SOH\sin x^\circ=\dfrac{\textcolor{#7b4fc4}{\text{opp}}}{\textcolor{#1f5bd6}{\text{hyp}}}
CAH\cos x^\circ=\dfrac{\textcolor{#c27a0e}{\text{adj}}}{\textcolor{#1f5bd6}{\text{hyp}}}
TOA\tan x^\circ=\dfrac{\textcolor{#7b4fc4}{\text{opp}}}{\textcolor{#c27a0e}{\text{adj}}}

A calculator returns any of these ratios, for any angle between 0^\circ and 90^\circ, to as many decimal places as are wanted, but only once it is set to degree mode.

!
Degree mode first. Look for DEG on the screen. If it says RAD, the calculator reads 24 as 24 radians and \sin24 comes out as -0.9056: a negative “sine” is the giveaway. On the TI-30XIIS press DRG, use the arrows to underline DEG, then ENTER.

(b) With respect to the two diagrams above, which ratio of sides (\frac{OA}{OB}, \frac{AB}{OB}, \frac{AB}{OA}, etc.) is represented by each of the four ratios in (a)?
In each triangle the right angle is at A, so OB is the hypotenuse, AB is opposite \angle O, and OA is adjacent to \angle O. By SOH CAH TOA, \sin(\angle O)=\dfrac{AB}{OB} and \cos(\angle O)=\dfrac{OA}{OB}:
\sin 24^\circ=\dfrac{AB}{OB}, \cos 24^\circ=\dfrac{OA}{OB} (first triangle); \sin 67^\circ=\dfrac{AB}{OB}, \cos 67^\circ=\dfrac{OA}{OB} (second triangle), each pair using the sides of its own triangle.
Math Historyc. 190–120 BCE · Rhodes

The first trig table

Before calculators, people looked trig values up in tables. The first we know of was made by Hipparchus, a Greek astronomer on Rhodes often called the father of trigonometry. It is lost; later writers describe it.

It listed the chord (the line straight across a circle) for angles, probably every 7½°. Half a chord is our opposite side. With it he found the Moon is 59 to 67 Earth radii away; the real value is about 60.

His circle's radius was 3438. A 60° slice has a chord of 3438. Half of it, 1719, is opposite 30°. What is sin 30°?
sin 30° = opp ÷ hyp = 1719 ÷ 3438 = 0.5, the same as your calculator.
2

How the Ratios Change as the Angle Changes

Rescaling a right triangle so that its hypotenuse is exactly 1 unit long makes the sine and cosine ratios equal to the side lengths themselves: \sin x^\circ=\dfrac{\text{opp}}{1}=\text{opp} and \cos x^\circ=\dfrac{\text{adj}}{1}=\text{adj}. Drawing every such triangle inside one quarter circle of radius 1 lets the effect of opening the angle be read straight off the picture.

Open the dial: the hypotenuse stays 1, the legs trade places

Every triangle below has the same hypotenuse, a radius of the quarter circle. Watch the opposite and adjacent bars as the angle opens, and the tangent, which is not tied to the hypotenuse at all.

Example 2

Why sine grows and cosine shrinks as the angle opens

The diagram shows the two triangles of Example 1 rescaled so that each hypotenuse OB equals 1 unit. Both hypotenuses are radii of the same quarter circle, so the vertical legs (the sines) and the horizontal legs (the cosines) can be compared directly.

(a) As angle AOB increases from 24^\circ to 67^\circ, explain, using SOH CAH TOA and the diagrams above, why the sine ratio increases in value while the cosine ratio decreases in value.
With the hypotenuse fixed at 1, \sin(\angle AOB)=\dfrac{AB}{OB}=AB and \cos(\angle AOB)=\dfrac{OA}{OB}=OA.
As the angle opens from 24^\circ to 67^\circ the triangle gets taller and narrower: the opposite side AB grows longer, so the sine ratio increases, while the adjacent side OA grows shorter, so the cosine ratio decreases.
Numerically, \sin 24^\circ=0.4067<\sin 67^\circ=0.9205 and \cos 24^\circ=0.9135>\cos 67^\circ=0.3907.
(b) Determine, with reasoning, what happens to the tangent ratio as the angle increases from 24^\circ to 67^\circ.
\tan(\angle AOB)=\dfrac{AB}{OA}. The numerator AB is increasing while the denominator OA is decreasing, so the tangent ratio increases, and more steeply than the sine ratio does, since the growing numerator and the shrinking denominator both push the ratio upward.
\tan 24^\circ\approx 0.4452 while \tan 67^\circ\approx 2.3559.
Check (b) on the calculator. TAN24)ENTER gives 0.4452286853 and TAN67)ENTER gives 2.355852366: from less than 1 to more than 2.

Try any angle

Drag the slider (or tap an angle) and read the three ratios off the bars. Then press Swap to flip the triangle over the 45^\circ line.

3

Comparing Ratios Without a Calculator

Once it is clear why each ratio grows or shrinks with the angle, two ratios can be compared by reasoning alone.

The 45^\circ benchmark. At exactly 45^\circ the triangle is isosceles: opp = adj. So \sin 45^\circ=\cos 45^\circ and \tan 45^\circ=1. Below 45^\circ the adjacent leg is longer (\cos>\sin, \tan<1); above 45^\circ the opposite leg is longer (\sin>\cos, \tan>1).
Example 3

Ordering ratios by SOH CAH TOA reasoning

Without using a calculator, state which ratio in each part has the greater value. Justify each answer using SOH CAH TOA reasoning.

(a) \sin 18^\circ,\ \sin 71^\circ
With the hypotenuse fixed, a larger angle has a longer opposite side, so the sine ratio increases with the angle: \boldsymbol{\sin 71^\circ} is greater.
(\sin 18^\circ\approx 0.3090, \sin 71^\circ\approx 0.9455.)
(b) \cos 12^\circ,\ \cos 55^\circ
With the hypotenuse fixed, a larger angle has a shorter adjacent side, so the cosine ratio decreases as the angle increases: \boldsymbol{\cos 12^\circ} is greater.
(\cos 12^\circ\approx 0.9781, \cos 55^\circ\approx 0.5736.)
(c) \tan 22^\circ,\ \tan 80^\circ
As the angle grows the opposite side grows and the adjacent side shrinks, so \tan=\frac{\text{opp}}{\text{adj}} increases with the angle: \boldsymbol{\tan 80^\circ} is greater.
(\tan 22^\circ\approx 0.4040, \tan 80^\circ\approx 5.6713.)
(d) \sin 50^\circ,\ \cos 50^\circ,\ \tan 50^\circ
Since 50^\circ>45^\circ, the opposite side is longer than the adjacent side, so \tan 50^\circ=\frac{\text{opp}}{\text{adj}}>1, and \sin 50^\circ=\frac{\text{opp}}{\text{hyp}} exceeds \cos 50^\circ=\frac{\text{adj}}{\text{hyp}}.
Order: \cos 50^\circ<\sin 50^\circ<\tan 50^\circ (0.6428<0.7660<1.1918), so \boldsymbol{\tan 50^\circ} is the greatest.
Reason first, then check. The calculator is only the referee once the reasoning is written down.
4

Special Ratios at 0^\circ and 90^\circ, and the Pattern Across All Angles

A triangle cannot actually be formed when the angle is 0^\circ or 90^\circ, but the ratios still make sense if a rotation angle is pictured sweeping open. Below, a segment of length r is rotated counterclockwise about O through an angle of a^\circ, forming right triangle OAB with opposite side y, adjacent side x, and hypotenuse r.

Example 4

The two extremes, and the pattern in between

Given that \sin a^\circ=\dfrac{y}{r}, (a) write \cos a^\circ and \tan a^\circ in terms of x, y, and r.
By SOH CAH TOA, the adjacent side is x and the hypotenuse is r, so \cos a^\circ=\dfrac{x}{r}; the opposite side is y and the adjacent side is x, so \tan a^\circ=\dfrac{y}{x}.
(b) Explain what happens to x and y as a shrinks toward 0^\circ, and what happens to x and y as a grows toward 90^\circ. Use this to state the exact value of each of \sin 0^\circ, \cos 0^\circ, \tan 0^\circ, \sin 90^\circ, \cos 90^\circ, and \tan 90^\circ.
As a shrinks toward 0^\circ the triangle flattens: y shrinks toward 0 while x grows toward r. As a grows toward 90^\circ the triangle stands up straight: x shrinks toward 0 while y grows toward r. Substituting these limiting values,
\sin 0^\circ=\frac{0}{r}=\mathbf{0}, \cos 0^\circ=\frac{r}{r}=\mathbf{1}, \tan 0^\circ=\frac{0}{r}=\mathbf{0},
\sin 90^\circ=\frac{r}{r}=\mathbf{1}, \cos 90^\circ=\frac{0}{r}=\mathbf{0}, \tan 90^\circ=\frac{r}{0} is undefined.
The calculator agrees. SIN90)ENTER gives 1, COS90)ENTER gives 0, and TAN90)ENTER gives a DOMAIN error: it refuses to divide by 0.

5

Complementary Angles: the Cofunction Relationship

Extension

In any right triangle the two acute angles add to 90^\circ: they are complementary. This links the sine of one acute angle to the cosine of the other.

Example 5

\sin x^\circ=\cos(90-x)^\circ

Extension

Triangle DEF has a right angle at D, with \angle F=x^\circ, as shown.

(a) Write an expression for \angle E in terms of x.
The three angles of any triangle sum to 180^\circ, and \angle D=90^\circ, so \angle E=180^\circ-90^\circ-x^\circ=\boldsymbol{(90-x)^\circ}.
(b) Explain, using the side ratios in the diagram, why \sin x^\circ=\cos(90-x)^\circ.
The hypotenuse is EF. Relative to \angle F=x^\circ the opposite side is DE, so \sin x^\circ=\dfrac{DE}{EF}. Relative to \angle E=(90-x)^\circ the adjacent side is DE, so \cos(90-x)^\circ=\dfrac{DE}{EF}. Both ratios equal \dfrac{DE}{EF}, hence \sin x^\circ=\cos(90-x)^\circ.

(c) Complete the following, using an angle between 0^\circ and 90^\circ.

(i) \sin 63^\circ=\cos 27^\circ
\sin 63^\circ=\cos(90-63)^\circ=\boldsymbol{\cos 27^\circ}.
Check: \sin 63^\circ\approx 0.8910 and \cos 27^\circ\approx 0.8910 ✓
(ii) \sin 9^\circ=\cos 81^\circ
\sin 9^\circ=\cos(90-9)^\circ=\boldsymbol{\cos 81^\circ}.
Check: \sin 9^\circ\approx 0.1564 and \cos 81^\circ\approx 0.1564 ✓
Check the pairs. SIN63)ENTER and COS27)ENTER both show 0.8910065242.
✓

Key ideas

Calculator: check DEG mode, then SIN 24 ) ENTER. The value is a side ratio: \sin 24^\circ=0.4067 means opp is about 0.41 of hyp.
Hypotenuse 1: \sin=\text{opp}, \cos=\text{adj}. Opening the angle, sine increases from 0 to 1, cosine decreases from 1 to 0, tangent increases from 0, passing 1 at 45^\circ.
Benchmark 45^\circ: opp = adj, so \sin45^\circ=\cos45^\circ, \tan45^\circ=1. Below: \cos>\sin. Above: \sin>\cos, \tan>1.
Extremes: \sin0^\circ=0, \cos0^\circ=1, \tan0^\circ=0; \sin90^\circ=1, \cos90^\circ=0, \tan90^\circ undefined. Extension \sin x^\circ=\cos(90-x)^\circ.
