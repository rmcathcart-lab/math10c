Entire and Mixed Radicals: Index 3 and Higher

In Lesson 7 we pulled the largest perfect square out of a square root. The same move works for cube roots and beyond: pull out the largest perfect cube, perfect fourth power, … perfect nth power.

I can…
  • write a cube root (or higher root) as a mixed radical by pulling out the largest perfect nth power
  • write a mixed radical with index 3 or higher as an entire radical
  • decide when a negative sign can move into or out of a radical (odd index vs. even index)
  • Extension: simplify radicals with variables by dividing each exponent by the index
AN2 Demonstrate an understanding of irrational numbers by representing, identifying and simplifying irrational numbers, and by ordering irrational numbers.

The big one. A cube-shaped box with 10 cm edges holds 1 litre. A cube-shaped box that holds 2 litres needs edges about how many times as long? Guess first.
A 2 times
B 1.4 times
C 1.26 times
D 8 times

C — about 1.26 times. The volume doubles, so the edge is multiplied by a number k with k\times k\times k=2. That's k=\sqrt[3]{2}\approx1.26: the 2-litre box has edges of about 12.6 cm, not 20 cm.

A (2 times) doubles every edge: 2\times2\times2=8, an 8-litre box!
B (1.4 times) is \sqrt{2}\approx1.41: that doubles the area of a square face, not the volume.
D (8 times) mixes up the edge and the volume.

An ancient Greek legend: an oracle told the people of Delos to double their cube-shaped altar. Doubling each edge made it 8 times as big. They needed edges \sqrt[3]{2} times as long. And \sqrt[3]{2\,000}=\sqrt[3]{1\,000\times2}=10\sqrt[3]{2}\approx12.6 cm, which is today's lesson in one line.

Settle it with the \sqrt[x]{\ } key. The index goes first: 32nd^2ENTER gives \sqrt[3]{2}\approx1.2599.

Entire to Mixed Radicals (Index 3 and Higher)

In Lesson 7, \sqrt{200}=\sqrt{100\times2}=10\sqrt{2}: the largest perfect square factor came out in front of the root. For a cube root, pull out the largest perfect cube factor; for a fourth root, the largest perfect fourth power; and so on.

An entire radical with index n becomes a mixed radical once the largest perfect nth-power factor is pulled out of the radicand. For index 3, that's the largest perfect cube dividing it.

Perfect powers to know

Tap a row to highlight it.

\sqrt[3]{250}=\sqrt[3]{125\times2}=\sqrt[3]{125}\times\sqrt[3]{2}=5\sqrt[3]{2} choose 125=5^3, the largest perfect cube dividing 250 · any index works the same way

!
Watch out: use the largest. \sqrt[3]{1\,080}=\sqrt[3]{8\times135}=2\sqrt[3]{135} is true, but not simplest form: 135 = 27 × 5 still hides a perfect cube. Keep going (or start with 216) to get 6\sqrt[3]{5}.

Or use prime factors: groups of three

Break the radicand into prime factors and sort them into groups of three identical factors. Each full group sends one factor outside the cube root; leftovers stay inside.

Check on the calculator. Both sides must give the same decimal: 32nd^54ENTER and 3×32nd^2ENTER both give 3.7798, so \sqrt[3]{54}=3\sqrt[3]{2}.

Higher index: groups of 4, 5, …

The index is the group size. A fourth root needs four identical factors to bring one out; a fifth root needs five.

Example 1

Higher-index entire → mixed

Convert to mixed radicals in simplest form.

(a) \sqrt[3]{1\,080}
Largest cube dividing 1 080 is 216=6^3 (1 080 = 216 × 5)
=\sqrt[3]{216}\times\sqrt[3]{5}= \boldsymbol{6\sqrt[3]{5}}
(b) \sqrt[5]{3\,072}
Largest fifth power dividing 3 072 is 1\,024=4^5 (3 072 = 1 024 × 3)
= \boldsymbol{4\sqrt[5]{3}}
(c) \sqrt[3]{-135}
Odd index, so the − can go inside: -135=-27\times5
=\sqrt[3]{-27}\times\sqrt[3]{5}= \boldsymbol{-3\sqrt[3]{5}}
Think Without a calculator: \sqrt[3]{1\,080} is between which two whole numbers?
10^3=1\,000 and 11^3=1\,331, so \sqrt[3]{1\,080} is between 10 and 11. Check: 6\sqrt[3]{5}\approx6\times1.71=10.26 ✓
Check (b) and (c). 52nd^3072ENTER and 4×52nd^3ENTER both give 4.9829. For a negative radicand use (−), not −: \sqrt[3]{-135}\approx-5.1299.
2

Mixed to Entire Radicals (Index 3 and Higher)

To reverse the process, turn the coefficient into a radical of the same index by raising it to the power n, then multiply under one root.

\tfrac13\sqrt[3]{54}=\sqrt[3]{\left(\tfrac13\right)^3}\times\sqrt[3]{54}=\sqrt[3]{\tfrac{1}{27}}\times\sqrt[3]{54}=\sqrt[3]{\tfrac{54}{27}}=\sqrt[3]{2}

Watch the sign

Odd index (3, 5, 7, …): a negative sign travels freely in or out of the radical, since an odd power of a negative number is still negative.
-2\sqrt[3]{5}=\sqrt[3]{(-2)^3\times5}=\sqrt[3]{-40}
Even index (2, 4, 6, …): it can't. An even root of a negative number is not real, so a negative coefficient must stay outside.
-2\sqrt[4]{3}=-\sqrt[4]{2^4\times3}=-\sqrt[4]{48}
The calculator agrees. 32nd^(−)8ENTER gives −2, but 42nd^(−)16ENTER gives an error: \sqrt[4]{-16} is not a real number.
Example 2

Higher-index mixed → entire

Convert to entire radicals.

(a) 3\sqrt[4]{2}
3^4=81
=\sqrt[4]{81}\times\sqrt[4]{2}
= \boldsymbol{\sqrt[4]{162}}
(b) -3\sqrt[3]{11}
Odd index: (-3)^3=-27
=\sqrt[3]{-27}\times\sqrt[3]{11}
= \boldsymbol{\sqrt[3]{-297}}
(c) \dfrac{3}{4}\sqrt[3]{32}
\left(\dfrac34\right)^3=\dfrac{27}{64}
\dfrac{27}{64}\times32=\dfrac{27}{2}
= \boldsymbol{\sqrt[3]{\dfrac{27}{2}}}
(d) -2\sqrt[5]{5}
Odd index: (-2)^5=-32
-32\times5=-160
= \boldsymbol{\sqrt[5]{-160}}
Think Can -3\sqrt[4]{2} be written as \sqrt[4]{-162}?
No. The index is even: (-3)^4=+81, and \sqrt[4]{-162} isn't a real number. The − stays outside: \boldsymbol{-3\sqrt[4]{2}=-\sqrt[4]{162}}.
Check (a). 3×42nd^2ENTER and 42nd^162ENTER both give 3.5676.

Extension: Radicals With Variables

The same two conversions work when the radicand contains variables: track exponents instead of values. Assume every variable is non-negative, so no absolute-value signs are needed.

Divide the exponent by the index. The quotient is the exponent that comes out; the remainder stays inside.

Tap each one to check.

When the exponent does not divide evenly by the index, split it into the largest multiple of the index plus a remainder:

\sqrt{x^7}=\sqrt{x^6\times x}=\sqrt{x^6}\times\sqrt{x}=x^3\sqrt{x}

The exponent machine

Pick an exponent and an index. Every full group of x's comes out as one x.

Numbers and variables together

Do each factor on its own: the number by its largest perfect nth power, each variable by dividing its exponent by the index. Then put the outside parts together and the inside parts together.

Example 3

Variable entire → mixed

Convert the following entire radicals to mixed radicals in simplest form.

(a) \sqrt{a^9}
a^9=a^8\times a, \sqrt{a^8}=a^4
= \boldsymbol{a^4\sqrt{a}}
(b) \sqrt{t^{11}}
t^{11}=t^{10}\times t, \sqrt{t^{10}}=t^5
= \boldsymbol{t^5\sqrt{t}}
(c) \sqrt[3]{x^8}
x^8=x^6\times x^2, \sqrt[3]{x^6}=x^2
= \boldsymbol{x^2\sqrt[3]{x^2}}
(d) \sqrt[3]{x^{10}}
x^{10}=x^9\times x, \sqrt[3]{x^9}=x^3
= \boldsymbol{x^3\sqrt[3]{x}}
(e) \sqrt{x^8y^7}
x^8 is a perfect square; y^7=y^6\times y
=\sqrt{x^8y^6\times y}
= \boldsymbol{x^4y^3\sqrt{y}}
(f) \sqrt{50x^5}
50=25\times2, x^5=x^4\times x
=\sqrt{25x^4\times2x}
= \boldsymbol{5x^2\sqrt{2x}}
(g) \sqrt{48y^5z^6}
48=16\times3, y^5=y^4\times y, z^6 is a perfect square
=\sqrt{16y^4z^6\times3y}
= \boldsymbol{4y^2z^3\sqrt{3y}}
(h) \sqrt[3]{54x^7y^{10}}
54=27\times2, x^7=x^6\times x, y^{10}=y^9\times y
=\sqrt[3]{27x^6y^9\times2xy}
= \boldsymbol{3x^2y^3\sqrt[3]{2xy}}

Variable mixed → entire

Same as Section 2: the whole coefficient goes in as its nth power. Raise each exponent: (b^3)^2=b^6, (2pq^2)^3=8p^3q^6. Then multiply under one root by adding exponents of matching variables.

Example 4

Variable mixed → entire

Convert the following mixed radicals to entire radicals.

(a) 3\sqrt{x^5}
3^2=9
= \boldsymbol{\sqrt{9x^5}}
(b) b^3\sqrt{b}
(b^3)^2=b^6
=\sqrt{b^6\times b}= \boldsymbol{\sqrt{b^7}}
(c) x^4\sqrt{xy}
(x^4)^2=x^8
=\sqrt{x^8\times xy}= \boldsymbol{\sqrt{x^9y}}
(d) 2pq^2\sqrt[3]{3r^5}
(2pq^2)^3=8p^3q^6
8p^3q^6\times3r^5
= \boldsymbol{\sqrt[3]{24p^3q^6r^5}}
✓

Key ideas

Entire → mixed: pull out the largest perfect nth power. \sqrt[3]{54}=\sqrt[3]{27\times2}=3\sqrt[3]{2}
Mixed → entire: the coefficient goes back in as its nth power. 3\sqrt[3]{2}=\sqrt[3]{3^3\times2}=\sqrt[3]{54}
Signs: with an odd index the − moves freely in and out. With an even index it must stay outside.
Variables: exponent ÷ index. The quotient comes out, the remainder stays in. \sqrt[3]{x^7}=x^2\sqrt[3]{x}
