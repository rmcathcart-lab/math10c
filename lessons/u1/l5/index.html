Roots of Real Numbers

A square root undoes squaring and a cube root undoes cubing. Today every power gets an undo button — fourth roots, fifth roots, seventh roots… We’ll name the parts of a radical, use the calculator’s \sqrt[x]{\phantom{0}} key, and find out which ways of combining radicals work — and which famous one doesn’t.

I can…
  • evaluate square roots, cube roots and roots of any index, in my head or with prime factors
  • tell when a root is not a real number (an even index with a negative radicand)
  • use the TI-30XIIS \sqrt[x]{\phantom{0}} key to evaluate a root exactly or to the nearest hundredth
  • name the index, the radicand and the radical sign of a radical
  • multiply and divide radicals with the product and quotient rules — and avoid the \sqrt{a+b} trap

Screen A: can any number times itself make −9? Try 3, then try −3.
No. 3\times3=9 and (-3)(-3)=9. Same signs always multiply to a positive, so \sqrt{-9} is ERROR (not a real number).

B — two screens: A and C. \sqrt{-9} and \sqrt[4]{-16} are ERROR: any real number to the 2nd or 4th power is ≥ 0. The other four work: \sqrt[3]{-8}=-2, \sqrt[5]{-32}=-2 since (-2)^5=-32, \sqrt{0}=0, \sqrt[3]{-\tfrac{1}{8}}=-\tfrac{1}{2}.

Can’t see it? Use prime factors

Write the radicand as a product of primes (Lesson 1!). Make groups the size of the index — groups of 4 for a fourth root. One prime from each group comes out. A prime left over means the radicand is not a perfect power.

Negative radicands: odd index or even index?

Odd index (3, 5, 7, …) — a negative radicand is fine. The root is negative.
\sqrt[5]{-32}=-2 because (-2)^5=-32
Even index (2, 4, 6, …) — a negative radicand has no real root: it’s not possible in the real numbers.
\sqrt[4]{-16}: not a real number

Mentally evaluate, where possible, using the real number system.

(a) \sqrt{121}
11\times11=121
= 11
(b) \sqrt[3]{-125}
(-5)^3=-125

(c) \sqrt[4]{6\,561}
9^2=81 and 81^2=6\,561, so 9^4=6\,561

3^5=243, so \left(\tfrac{1}{3}\right)^5=\tfrac{1}{243}

Evaluating Radicals with a Calculator

The \sqrt[x]{\phantom{0}} (xth root) key evaluates a radical of any index. On the TI-30XIIS it is the 2nd function of ^. Key the index first, then 2nd^, then the radicand, then ENTER.

\sqrt[5]{161\,051}: 52nd^161051ENTER shows 11. (TI-83/84: index, then MATH → 5: \sqrt[x]{\phantom{0}}, then the radicand.)
!
Index first! Keyed the other way round, 1610512nd^5 finds the 161 051st root of 5 ≈ 1.00001.
!
Negative radicand? Use the (−) key, not −: 72nd^(−)128.

Radical Vocabulary

Any expression \sqrt[n]{x}, where n\in N and n\ge2, is a radical: \sqrt{19}, \sqrt[3]{54}, \sqrt[5]{7}, \sqrt[6]{2\,000\,000}.
\sqrt{\phantom{x}} is the radical sign, x is the radicand, and n is the index: how many times the root is used as a factor to make x.
If the index is even, the radicand must be non-negative, or the radical is not a real number.
No index written? It’s 2: \sqrt{85}=\sqrt[2]{85}. A number in front, like the 4 in 4\sqrt[5]{7}, is a coefficient that multiplies.

Combining Radicals: Products and Quotients

Using 16 and 9 — which of these are true? Tap one to check it.

(i) Products and quotients — YES. The root of a product (or quotient) is the product (or quotient) of the roots.
\sqrt{a}\times\sqrt{b}=\sqrt{ab},  a,b\ge0
\dfrac{\sqrt{a}}{\sqrt{b}}=\sqrt{\dfrac{a}{b}},  a\ge0,\ b>0
(ii) Sums and differences — NO. The sum (or difference) of the roots is not the root of the sum (or difference).
\sqrt{a}+\sqrt{b}\ne\sqrt{a+b}
\sqrt{a}-\sqrt{b}\ne\sqrt{a-b}

Why sums don’t work

Watch out. “Square root of each piece” only works when the pieces are multiplied or divided. \sqrt{9+16} is \sqrt{25}=5, not 3+4=7. Add (or subtract) inside first.

Example 6

Splitting a radical into a product

Express as a product of two radicals.

(a) \sqrt{85}
85=5\times17 (no perfect square)
= \sqrt{5}\times\sqrt{17}
(b) \sqrt{98}
98=49\times2, and 49 is a perfect square
= \sqrt{49}\times\sqrt{2} (which is 7\sqrt{2})
(c) \sqrt{32}
32=16\times2, and 16 is a perfect square
= \sqrt{16}\times\sqrt{2} (which is 4\sqrt{2})
Think \sqrt{98}=\sqrt{7}\times\sqrt{14} is also correct. Why is \sqrt{49}\times\sqrt{2} the most useful split?
\sqrt{49}=7 is a whole number, so \sqrt{98}=7\sqrt{2} — a simpler way to write it. That’s Lesson 6!

Key ideas

\sqrt[n]{x} asks: what number, used as a factor n times, makes x? \sqrt[5]{32}=2 since 2^5=32.
Even index: the radicand must be ≥ 0, and the answer is the positive (principal) root. Odd index: negatives are fine — the root is negative.
TI-30XIIS: index, 2nd^, radicand, ENTER. Use (−) for a negative. Round only at the end.
\sqrt{a}\times\sqrt{b}=\sqrt{ab} and \tfrac{\sqrt{a}}{\sqrt{b}}=\sqrt{\tfrac{a}{b}} — but \sqrt{a+b}\ne\sqrt{a}+\sqrt{b}.
