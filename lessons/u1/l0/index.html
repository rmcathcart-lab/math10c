Alberta Education’s rules — the same standard all year

  • A scientific calculator with SIN, COS and TAN keys, like the TI-30XIIS — around $15–20.

10

What Counts

Outcomes are what I assess

Each unit test covers a set of Math 10C outcomes. Your mark reflects the outcomes you can show me.

Exit slips along the way check one or two outcomes from the lesson.

Everything else still counts

What I see you doing during work time matters. In a judgment call on an outcome, I’ll make the call based on how I’ve seen you perform in class.

I can only assess class work

If I didn’t watch you do it, I can’t know the thinking is yours. That’s why outcome evidence is gathered here, in this room — not at home. Practice at home is still great for learning.

Earn your medals

Every outcome has its own medal. It shows the best level you’ve reached — from practice in class or a unit test — and it sits beside your name on the website. Tap one to spin it.

Academic Honesty

Practice

Copying someone’s assignment helps neither of you — it costs you the practice.

AI and photo-solver apps: fine for checking your work, not for producing it.

Tests & exit slips

Talking, notes you weren’t allowed, another device, or using AI for any part of a test is cheating.

It can mean a call home and a 0 on that test (or a different test, at my discretion). Serious cases go further.

Leaving the test window locks your test.

Emergency Protocols

Boring until it isn’t. Know these four before you need them.
Fire drill
Leave your stuff, take your body. Out the nearest exit and across to the back field. I count you on the way out and again outside.
Lockdown
Threat inside the building. Lights off, out of sight, silent — including phones. Nobody leaves until an administrator opens the door.
On alert
Something happening nearby. Class continues, door locked, nobody in the hallways.
Shelter in place
Hazard outside — weather, air quality. We stay put, windows and doors closed.
Look around Where is the nearest exit, the pull station and the fire extinguisher? What’s the fastest route from this room to the back field?

Numbers About Me

No marks, no wrong answers — just a warm-up so nobody leaves day one having done zero math.

1Write four numbers that describe you. Anything: jersey number, siblings, hours of sleep last night, kilometres from here to your favourite place.
2Trade with the person beside you. No explanations yet — just the four numbers.
3Guess what each number means. Then check. You’ll be wrong about at least one, and that’s the fun part.
