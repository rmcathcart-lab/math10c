Prime Building Blocks of Numbers

Every whole number bigger than 1 is built by multiplying primes together. Today we find factors, sort primes from composites, and take numbers apart into their prime building blocks.

I can…
  • list all the factors of a whole number, in pairs, without missing any
  • decide whether a number is prime or composite
  • tell the difference between the prime factors of a number and its product of prime factors
  • find a prime factorization with a division ladder or a factor tree
AN1 Demonstrate an understanding of factors of whole numbers by determining the prime factors, greatest common factor, least common multiple, square root and cube root.

Factors

Factors of a whole number are the whole numbers that divide into it evenly (no remainder).

Only 1, 2, 7 and 14 divide evenly into 14, so 14 has four factors. Factors come in pairs that multiply to the number:

14 = 1 × 14 = 2 × 7

To list factors without missing any, test 1, 2, 3, … in order. Each number that works brings its partner. Stop when the pairs would start repeating — that happens once you pass the square root.

!
Watch out. Don’t stop too early: 40 ÷ 5 = 8 is easy to miss. And a square number’s middle factor is listed once: 16 = 4 × 4 gives just one 4.

Prime and Composite Numbers

Prime number — has exactly two factors, 1 and itself.
2 = 1 × 2    13 = 1 × 13
Composite number — has more than two factors.
12: 1, 2, 3, 4, 6, 12    28: 1, 2, 4, 7, 14, 28
1 and 0 are neither. 1 has only one factor. Every whole number divides into 0 (0 = 5 × 0), so 0 has infinitely many factors — and no product of primes can ever make 0.

Testing a bigger number

Divide by each prime up to the square root. If none of them divides evenly, the number is prime. The first three tests have shortcuts:

÷ 2last digit is even (0, 2, 4, 6, 8)
÷ 3digits add up to a multiple of 3 (51: 5 + 1 = 6 ✓)
÷ 5last digit is 0 or 5
÷ 7, 11, 13…use the calculator: is the answer a whole number?

Math Historyc. 300 BCE · Euclid’s Elements, Book IX

Euclid’s proof: the primes never run out

Around 300 BCE, the Greek mathematician Euclid wrote the Elements, a math book that was still used in schools 2 000 years later. In it he proved there is no largest prime.

His idea: take any list of primes, multiply them and add 1. None of the primes on the list divides the new number (each leaves a remainder of 1). So the new number is a new prime, or its prime factors are new primes.

2 × 3 × 5 × 7 × 11 × 13 + 1 = 30 031. Is 30 031 prime? (Hint: try 59.)
30 031 ÷ 59 = 509, so it is composite: 30 031 = 59 × 509. And 509 is prime (no prime up to √509 ≈ 22.6 divides it). So 59 and 509 are new primes, just as Euclid said.

Prime Factors

The prime factors of a whole number are the factors that are themselves prime.

The factors of 18 are 1, 2, 3, 6, 9 and 18. Only 2 and 3 are prime, so the prime factors of 18 are 2 and 3.

Prime factors of 18
2, 3
a list — each prime once
18 as a product of prime factors
18 = 2 × 3 × 3 = 2 × 32
an equation — repeats included, so it multiplies back to 18

Prime Factorization

Prime factorization breaks a composite number down until only primes remain. Every composite number has exactly one set of prime factors (that’s the warm-up!).
Division ladder
Divide by the smallest prime that fits. Repeat on the answer until you reach 1. The primes are down the left side.
Very organized — great for big numbers.
or
Factor tree
Split the number into any factor pair. Keep splitting every composite until each branch ends in a prime (circle it).
Quick when you spot a friendly pair like 10.

90 = 2 × 3 × 3 × 5 = 2 × 32 × 5 primes from smallest to largest, exponents for repeats

Key ideas

Factors divide evenly and come in pairs. Test 1, 2, 3, … up to the square root.
Prime: exactly two factors. Composite: more than two. 0 and 1 are neither.
Prime factors are a list (2, 3). A prime factorization is an equation (18 = 2 × 32).
Ladder or tree, every composite number has one prime factorization.
