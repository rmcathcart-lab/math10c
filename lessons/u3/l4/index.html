Multiplying Polynomials II — Binomial × Trinomial and Three Binomials

FOIL handles two binomials, but it runs out of hands the moment a factor has three terms. Today every term shakes hands with every term: six partial products at a time, organized in a grid or by distribution. Then three binomials at once, to find the volume of a box.

I can…
  • explain why an m-term polynomial times an n-term polynomial makes m\times n partial products
  • multiply a binomial by a trinomial with a grid (area model) and with the distributive property, then collect like terms
  • multiply three binomials in two steps, and explain why the pair I multiply first doesn’t matter
  • write and expand the volume of a rectangular prism whose edges are binomials
  • check an expansion by substituting a number into both forms
AN4 Demonstrate an understanding of the multiplication of polynomial expressions (limited to monomials, binomials and trinomials), concretely, pictorially and symbolically.
Warm-up

The Painted Cube

A 3 by 3 by 3 cube is dipped in paint, then cut into 27 small cubes. Some small cubes got paint on 3 faces, some on 2, some on 1… and some on none. Tap a kind of cube to find them all.

1
How many small cubes have paint on 3 faces? Where are they?
8, one at each corner of the big cube.
2
How many have exactly 2 painted faces? Exactly 1?
Two faces: 12, the middle of each of the 12 edges. One face: 6, the centre of each of the 6 faces.
3
How many have no paint? What if the big cube were 4 by 4 by 4?
1, the hidden centre cube. Check: 8+12+6+1=27. For 4 by 4 by 4 the core is 2\times2\times2=\boldsymbol{8}.
!
The big one. A 10 by 10 by 10 cube is painted, then cut into 1000 small cubes. How many small cubes have no paint? Guess first.
A 100
B 400
C 512
D 729
C, 512: more than half! Peel one layer off every side: 10-2=8 each way, so the unpainted core is 8\times8\times8=512.
B takes away 6\times100=600 outside cubes, but edge and corner cubes sit on 2 or 3 faces and get taken away more than once (only 488 are painted). D is 9^3: a layer peeled off only one side each way. A is the “the paint must get most of them” gut feeling.
+
Challenge your neighbour. A 3 × 4 × 5 box is painted and cut into 60 cubes. How many have no paint? (It’s on the cube above too.)
(3-2)(4-2)(5-2)=1\times2\times3=\boldsymbol{6}. For an n\times n\times n cube it’s (n-2)(n-2)(n-2): three binomials multiplied together. That’s today.

Multiplying a Binomial by a Trinomial

When a binomial (2 terms) is multiplied by a trinomial (3 terms), every term of the binomial must “shake hands” with every term of the trinomial: 2\times3=6 partial products before any like terms are collected.

General rule for multiplying polynomials. Multiply every term of the first factor by every term of the second, then collect like terms. An m-term polynomial times an n-term polynomial gives m\times n partial products before collecting.
binomial × binomial2\times2=4this is FOIL
binomial × trinomial2\times3=6today
trinomial × trinomial3\times3=9same rule
!
FOIL is only the 2 × 2 case. First, Outer, Inner, Last makes 4 products. With a trinomial, FOIL never touches its middle term, so two partial products go missing.

Method 1: the grid (area model)

A small grid keeps track of all six. One factor labels the rows, the other labels the columns, and each cell holds the product of its row and column labels. Add every cell and combine like terms.

↘
Write both factors in descending powers and the like terms land on the same diagonal of the grid. That makes them easy to spot.

Method 2: the distributive property

Each term of the first factor multiplies the whole second factor. It’s the same six products as the grid, written in a line.

!
Subtracting a product? Keep it in brackets. Expand it first, then the minus sign flips every term inside: -(25b^2-9)=-25b^2+9.
Example 1

The same product two ways: grid, then distribution

A rectangle has length (3x+4) cm and width \left(x^2+3x+2\right) cm. Determine the area of the rectangle, in cm², using

(i) an area model (grid)
\begin{array}{c|ccc}\times&x^2&3x&2\\\hline3x&3x^3&9x^2&6x\\4&4x^2&12x&8\end{array}
Add all six cells, then collect: 3x^3+(9x^2+4x^2)+(6x+12x)+8
=\boldsymbol{3x^3+13x^2+18x+8} cm²
(ii) the distributive property
=3x(x^2+3x+2)+4(x^2+3x+2)
=(3x^3+9x^2+6x)+(4x^2+12x+8)
=\boldsymbol{3x^3+13x^2+18x+8}
Both methods agree: the area is (3x^3+13x^2+18x+8) cm².
Example 2

Expanding a binomial times a trinomial

Expand and simplify.

(a) (x^2-9)(3x^3+2x-7)
=x^2(3x^3+2x-7)-9(3x^3+2x-7)
=(3x^5+2x^3-7x^2)-(27x^3+18x-63)
Only the x^3 terms are alike: 2x^3-27x^3=-25x^3
=\boldsymbol{3x^5-25x^3-7x^2-18x+63}
(b) (3y^2-2y-5)(y-4)
=3y^2(y-4)-2y(y-4)-5(y-4)
=(3y^3-12y^2)-(2y^2-8y)-(5y-20)
-12y^2-2y^2=-14y^2 and 8y-5y=3y
=\boldsymbol{3y^3-14y^2+3y+20}
(c) 3(b-5)(b^2-2b-4)-(5b-3)(5b+3)
Binomial × trinomial first (six partial products): (b-5)(b^2-2b-4)=b^3-2b^2-4b-5b^2+10b+20=b^3-7b^2+6b+20, so 3(b-5)(b^2-2b-4)=3b^3-21b^2+18b+60.
A sum times a difference: (5b-3)(5b+3)=25b^2-9.
Subtracting: (3b^3-21b^2+18b+60)-(25b^2-9)=\boldsymbol{3b^3-46b^2+18b+69}
Think In (a), how many partial products are there before collecting? Why does the answer have only five terms?
2\times3=6 partial products. Only one pair is alike (2x^3 and -27x^3), so they combine into one term: 6-1=5 terms.

Multiplying Three Binomials

The same idea extends to three binomials: multiply the first two, then multiply that result by the third, then collect like terms. Because multiplication is commutative and associative, neither the choice of which pair to multiply first nor the order of the factors changes the final expansion.

Two steps
Step 1: binomial × binomial gives a trinomial (FOIL, 4 products).
Step 2: trinomial × binomial (6 products), then collect.
Volume of a rectangular prism
V=\text{length}\times\text{width}\times\text{height}. When the edges are binomials, the volume is a product of three binomials.

Back to the warm-up: the unpainted core of an n\times n\times n cube is (n-2)(n-2)(n-2).

A box with binomial edges

Step 1 finds the area of the base. Step 2 multiplies by the height, which slices the box into layers.

!
Not just firsts and lasts. (x+3)(x+4)(x+6)\neq x^3+72. Those are only 2 of the 8 blocks of the box. Every term of each factor has to meet every term of the others.
Example 3

Volume of a rectangular prism

Consider the rectangular prism shown, with edge lengths (x+3) cm, (x+4) cm, and (x+6) cm.

(a) Write an expression for the volume of the prism, in cm³.
V=\text{length}\times\text{width}\times\text{height}, so
V=\boldsymbol{(x+3)(x+4)(x+6)} cm³
(b) Expand the expression in (a) to write the volume in the form V=ax^3+bx^2+cx+d.
First two: (x+3)(x+4)=x^2+7x+12. Then (x^2+7x+12)(x+6)=x^2(x+6)+7x(x+6)+12(x+6)
=(x^3+6x^2)+(7x^2+42x)+(12x+72)=\boldsymbol{x^3+13x^2+54x+72}
So V=x^3+13x^2+54x+72 cm³.
Think If you multiplied all three binomials at once, without collecting in between, how many partial products would there be?
2\times2\times2=\boldsymbol{8}: one for each block of the box. x^3+3x^2+4x^2+12x+6x^2+18x+24x+72 collects to the same answer.

Does it matter which pair goes first?

Three binomials can be paired up three ways. Try all three and compare.

Example 4

The order of the three factors does not matter

Expand and simplify.

(a) (x-2)(x+5)(3x-1)
First two: (x-2)(x+5)=x^2+3x-10
(x^2+3x-10)(3x-1)
=(3x^3-x^2)+(9x^2-3x)+(-30x+10)
=\boldsymbol{3x^3+8x^2-33x+10}
(b) (3x-1)(x-2)(x+5) (multiply (3x-1)(x-2) first)
A different pair first: (3x-1)(x-2)=3x^2-7x+2
(3x^2-7x+2)(x+5)
=(3x^3+15x^2)+(-7x^2-35x)+(2x+10)
=\boldsymbol{3x^3+8x^2-33x+10}
(c) Comment on the results to (a) and (b).
Both give the same polynomial, 3x^3+8x^2-33x+10, even though (a) multiplied (x-2)(x+5) first and (b) multiplied (3x-1)(x-2) first. Multiplication is commutative and associative, so the three binomials can be grouped or reordered any way: choose the easiest pair first.
✓

Is My Expansion Right? Check It with a Number

An expression and its expansion are equal for every value of the variable. Substitute the same number into both: if the values don’t match, there’s a mistake somewhere.

Check Example 1 at x=2. The original: (3×2+4)×(2x²+3×2+2)ENTER. The expansion: 3×2^3+13×2x²+18×2+8ENTER. Both give 120 ✓.
Check the box of Example 3 at x=2. The edges are 5, 6 and 8: 5×6×8ENTER gives 240. The expansion: 2^3+13×2x²+54×2+72ENTER also gives 240 ✓.
!
0 and 1 make a weak test. At x=1 every power of x is 1, so a wrong exponent slips right past. Use 2 or 3. A match is strong evidence; a mismatch proves there’s a mistake.
✓

Key ideas

Every term × every term, then collect like terms. An m-term times an n-term polynomial makes m\times n partial products. FOIL is only the 2\times2 case.
Grid or distribution, same six products: (3x+4)(x^2+3x+2)=3x^3+13x^2+18x+8. In a grid, like terms line up along the diagonals.
Three binomials, two steps: multiply a pair, then multiply by the third. Any pair first gives the same answer, so pick the easiest.
Volume of a box with binomial edges: V=(x+3)(x+4)(x+6)=x^3+13x^2+54x+72. Check any expansion by substituting 2 or 3.
