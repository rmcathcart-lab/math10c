Math 10C · Unit 8 Systems of Linear Equations · Lesson 3

Combining Equations: The Elimination Method

Each equation says that two amounts are equal. Add equal things to equal things and they stay equal, so two equations can be added (or subtracted) to make a new true equation. Choose the combination well and one variable vanishes: directly, after scaling one equation, or after scaling both.

I can…
  • explain why two equations can be added or subtracted, and which variable is eliminated
  • add equations with opposite coefficients and subtract equations with matching coefficients, changing the sign of every term I subtract
  • multiply one or both equations (every term, the constant too) to create matching or opposite coefficients, using the least common multiple
  • rearrange equations into the form Ax+By=C and clear fractions before eliminating, and read 0=0 or 0=-15
  • choose between graphing, substitution and elimination, and verify my solution in both equations
RF9 Solve problems that involve systems of linear equations in two variables, graphically and algebraically.
Warm-up

Weigh the Pets

The vet’s scale only works with two pets on it at a time.

1
Scales B and C both have the rabbit. Who is heavier, the dog or the cat?
The dog. Scales B and C share the rabbit, so the difference between them (12 kg vs 6 kg) is all dog versus cat.
2
By how many kilograms?
12-6=6: the dog is 6 kg heavier.
3
Add all three readings. How many cats, dogs and rabbits did that weigh?
14+12+6=32 kg, and every pet was weighed exactly twice: 2 cats, 2 dogs and 2 rabbits.
!
The big one. All three pets on one big scale: what would it read? Guess first.
A 32 kg
B 16 kg
C Can’t tell
B: 16 kg. Two of each pet weigh 32 kg, so one of each weighs 32\div2=16 kg. Then each pet: take away one scale at a time. Rabbit =16-14=2 kg (Scale A has no rabbit), cat =16-12=4 kg, dog =16-6=10 kg. Check: 4+10=14,\ 10+2=12,\ 4+2=6 ✓
A adds the readings and stops, forgetting that every pet stood on a scale twice. C feels safe (“three unknowns, never weighed alone”), but three different readings are exactly enough.

The pets, solved by combining scales

Let c, d and r be the masses in kilograms. Subtracting two scales, or adding them, made one pet disappear. That move has a name: elimination.

1

The Idea Behind Elimination

Adding equal amounts to equal amounts keeps them equal. Each equation states that its left side and its right side are the same number, so the two left sides may be added and the two right sides added, and the result is still true. If one variable has opposite coefficients, adding makes it vanish; if it has matching coefficients, subtracting does. Either way one equation in one variable is left.

Think of each equation as a level balance. Put everything from both balances onto one big balance, left pan to left pan and right pan to right pan, and it is still level.

Steps of the Elimination Method

Step 1
If necessary, multiply one or both equations by a constant so that one variable has matching or opposite coefficients in the two equations.
Step 2
Add the equations (opposite coefficients) or subtract them (matching coefficients) to eliminate that variable.
Step 3
Solve the resulting one-variable equation.
Step 4
Substitute that value back into either original equation to find the other variable, then verify the solution in both original equations.
!
Multiplying an equation by a constant means multiplying every term, the constant term included.
!
Subtracting an equation reverses the sign of every term being subtracted, the constant too. Write it as a bracketed difference, (\;\cdots)-(\;\cdots), to keep the signs straight.
2

Eliminating by Adding or Subtracting Directly

When a system already has matching or opposite coefficients, Step 1 needs no work at all.

Opposite coefficients → add
+3y and -3y
They sum to 0.
Matching coefficients → subtract
3x and 3x
Their difference is 0.
Example 1

Opposite coefficients: add the equations

Consider the system Eq. 14x+3y=18 Eq. 22x-3y=0

(a) Add the two equations together. Which variable is eliminated, and why?
Add left sides and right sides:
\textcolor{#1f5bd6}{(4x+3y)}+\textcolor{#c27a0e}{(2x-3y)}=\textcolor{#1f5bd6}{18}+\textcolor{#c27a0e}{0}
The y-terms 3y and -3y are opposites, so they sum to 0: y is eliminated.
6x=18
(b) Solve the equation from part (a) for x, then substitute back into either original equation to find y.
6x=18\ \Rightarrow\ x=3
Into Eq. 2: 2(3)-3y=0
6-3y=0\ \Rightarrow\ -3y=-6
y=2
(c) Verify that your solution satisfies both original equations.
Eq. 1: 4(3)+3(2)=12+6=18 ✓
Eq. 2: 2(3)-3(2)=6-6=0 ✓
Solution: x=3,\ y=2
Example 2

Matching coefficients: subtract the equations

Consider the system Eq. 13x+4y=7 Eq. 23x-2y=19

(a) Subtract the second equation from the first (Eq. 1 - Eq. 2). Which variable is eliminated, and why?
Subtracting reverses the sign of every term of Eq. 2:
\textcolor{#1f5bd6}{(3x+4y)}-\textcolor{#c27a0e}{(3x-2y)}=\textcolor{#1f5bd6}{7}-\textcolor{#c27a0e}{19}
3x+4y-3x+2y=-12
The x-terms match (3x in both), so x is eliminated: 6y=-12
(b) Solve the equation from part (a) for y, then substitute back into either original equation to find x.
6y=-12\ \Rightarrow\ y=-2
Into Eq. 1: 3x+4(-2)=7
3x-8=7\ \Rightarrow\ 3x=15
x=5
(c) Verify that your solution satisfies both original equations.
Eq. 1: 3(5)+4(-2)=15-8=7 ✓
Eq. 2: 3(5)-2(-2)=15+4=19 ✓
Solution: x=5,\ y=-2

Subtracting an equation: every sign flips

Subtracting Eq. 2 is the same as adding its opposite. Write the whole of Eq. 2 in brackets, flip the sign of every term inside (the constant too), then add down the columns.

Choosing which variable to eliminate. When either would do, pick the one with the simpler coefficients (already equal or opposite, or small numbers), since that keeps the arithmetic in the later steps simple.
Think In Example 2, could you eliminate y instead of x? What would you have to do first?
Yes. Multiply every term of Eq. 2 by 2: 6x-4y=38. Now +4y and -4y are opposites, so add to Eq. 1: 9x=45, x=5. Same answer, one extra step, so eliminating x (already matching) is simpler.

What elimination does on the graph

Adding the equations makes a new line. Every point on both lines also fits the sum, so the new line passes through the crossing too. Elimination picks the combination that is a vertical line (x=\ldots) or a horizontal line (y=\ldots) through that crossing.

3

When One Equation Needs to Be Scaled

If neither variable has matching or opposite coefficients as the system stands, multiplying just one of the two equations by a constant may create a matching pair.

Multiplying an equation by 2 is like putting a second copy of the whole balance on top: every block on both pans doubles. The equation looks different, but its line and its solutions are exactly the same.
Example 3

Scaling one equation

Consider the system Eq. 13x+2y=20 Eq. 25x-y=29

(a) As written, does adding or subtracting the equations eliminate either variable? Explain.
No. The x-coefficients are 3 and 5 and the y-coefficients are 2 and -1; neither pair is equal or opposite.
Adding gives 8x+y=49 and subtracting gives -2x+3y=-9: both still contain two variables.
(b) Multiply Eq. 2 by a constant so that adding it to Eq. 1 eliminates y. Add, and solve for x.
Multiply every term of Eq. 2 by 2:
2(5x)-2(y)=2(29)\ \Rightarrow\ 10x-2y=58
Now +2y and -2y are opposites, so add:
(3x+2y)+(10x-2y)=20+58
13x=78\ \Rightarrow\ \boldsymbol{x=6}
(c) Substitute your x into either original equation to find y, then verify in both.
Into 5x-y=29: 5(6)-y=29, 30-y=29\ \Rightarrow\ -y=-1\ \Rightarrow\ y=1
Check Eq. 1: 3(6)+2(1)=18+2=20 ✓   Check Eq. 2: 5(6)-1=30-1=29 ✓
Solution: x=6,\ y=1
4

When Both Equations Need to Be Scaled

If no single equation can be scaled to match the other, multiply both equations, by possibly different constants, so that one variable ends up with matching or opposite coefficients.

The smallest such pair comes from the least common multiple (LCM) of the two coefficients. Then look at the signs: same sign → subtract; opposite signs → add.
Example 4

When scaling one equation is not enough

Return to the system of Example 3: Eq. 13x+2y=20 Eq. 25x-y=29

(a) Can x be eliminated by multiplying only one equation by a whole number? Explain.
No. The x-coefficients are 3 and 5. Neither is a whole-number multiple of the other: Eq. 1 would need \times\tfrac{5}{3} (or Eq. 2 \times\tfrac{3}{5}), which brings in fractions.
(b) Scale both equations so that x is eliminated, and solve for y.
The least common multiple of 3 and 5 is 15.
Eq. 1 \times5: 15x+10y=100
Eq. 2 \times3: 15x-3y=87
The x-coefficients now match, so subtract:
(15x+10y)-(15x-3y)=100-87\ \Rightarrow\ 13y=13\ \Rightarrow\ \boldsymbol{y=1}, matching Example 3 (c).
Example 5

Scaling both equations

Consider the system Eq. 14x+5y=1 Eq. 23x-2y=18

(a) Choose whole numbers to multiply each equation by so that adding the results eliminates y.
The y-coefficients are 5 and -2; the least common multiple of 5 and 2 is 10.
Eq. 1 \times2: 8x+10y=2
Eq. 2 \times5: 15x-10y=90
+10y and -10y are opposites.
(b) Add the equations from (a) and solve for x; substitute back for y and verify.
Adding: (8x+10y)+(15x-10y)=2+90
23x=92\ \Rightarrow\ x=4
Into 4x+5y=1: 16+5y=1, 5y=-15\ \Rightarrow\ y=-3
Check: 4(4)+5(-3)=16-15=1 ✓  3(4)-2(-3)=12+6=18 ✓
Solution: x=4,\ y=-3
(c) Back to the original system: choose whole numbers so that x is eliminated instead.
The x-coefficients are 4 and 3; their least common multiple is 12.
Eq. 1 \times3: 12x+15y=3
Eq. 2 \times4: 12x-8y=72
(d) Combine the equations from (c) and solve for y.
The x-coefficients match, so subtract:
(12x+15y)-(12x-8y)=3-72
15y+8y=-69\ \Rightarrow\ 23y=-69
\boldsymbol{y=-3}, matching part (b).

5

Preparing a System Before Eliminating

Not every system arrives ready to eliminate.

Rearrange each equation into the form Ax+By=C: the x-term, then the y-term on the left, the constant on the right. (General form, Ax+By+C=0, just needs its constant term moved to the other side.)
Clear fractions by multiplying every term of the equation by a common denominator (the LCD).
Example 6

Rearranging into the form Ax+By=C; clearing fractions

Neither system below is ready to eliminate as it stands.

(a) Rearrange 5x-2y-7=0, 2x=3y-6 into the form Ax+By=C, solve by elimination, and verify in both originals.
In the form Ax+By=C: 5x-2y=7 and 2x-3y=-6.
To eliminate y, multiply every term: Eq. 1 \times3, Eq. 2 \times2: 15x-6y=21, 4x-6y=-12.
Matching -6y, so subtract, and subtract the constant too, 21-(-12)=33:
(15x-6y)-(4x-6y)=21-(-12)\Rightarrow 11x=33\Rightarrow x=3
Into 2x-3y=-6: 6-3y=-6\Rightarrow -3y=-12\Rightarrow y=4.
Check 5(3)-2(4)-7=0 ✓; 2(3)=6 and 3(4)-6=6 ✓
Solution: x=3,\ y=4
(b) Clear the fractions in \dfrac{3x-5y}{4}=\dfrac{1}{2}, \dfrac{x}{3}+\dfrac{y}{2}=\dfrac{7}{3} and solve by elimination.
Clear fractions: Eq. 1 \times4: 3x-5y=2. Eq. 2 \times6: 2x+3y=14.
To eliminate y: \times3 and \times5: 9x-15y=6, 10x+15y=70.
Opposite coefficients, so add: 19x=76\Rightarrow x=4.
Into 2x+3y=14: 8+3y=14\Rightarrow 3y=6\Rightarrow y=2.
Check \dfrac{12-10}{4}=\dfrac{2}{4}=\dfrac12 ✓; \dfrac43+\dfrac22=\dfrac43+1=\dfrac73 ✓
Solution: x=4,\ y=2
Subtracting a negative constant. In (a), 21-(-12): key it with the negative key, 21−(−)12=, and you get 33, not 9. To check (b) in the first original: (3×4−5×2)÷4= gives 0.5 ✓.

Special cases: when both variables disappear

If eliminating one variable removes both (as in Lesson 2’s box), read the result the same way: a true statement such as 0=0 means infinitely many solutions (the same line); a false statement such as 0=-15 means no solution (parallel lines).

Choosing a Method

Method
Use it when …
Example
Graphingby hand or calculator
you need a picture or an estimate, or a graphing calculator is allowed. By hand it is exact only when the solution has integer coordinates.
y=2x-1

Substitution
a variable already has coefficient 1 or -1, or is already isolated (y=\ldots or x=\ldots).

the coefficients of one variable already match or are opposites, or are easy multiples of each other.

✓

Key ideas

The idea. Equal + equal = equal: add the two left sides and the two right sides and the result is still true. Choose the combination so one variable cancels, leaving one equation in one variable.
Add or subtract? Opposite coefficients (+3y,\ -3y): add. Matching coefficients (3x,\ 3x): subtract, flipping the sign of every term subtracted, constant included: (\;\cdots)-(\;\cdots).
Scaling. Multiply every term, the constant too: same line, same solutions. Use the LCM of the coefficients: one equation when one coefficient divides the other, otherwise both.
Before and after. First get Ax+By=C with no fractions. 0=0: infinitely many solutions; 0=-15: none. Choose the easiest method, and verify in both equations.
