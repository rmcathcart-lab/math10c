Balance the Mobile

A level bar means both sides weigh the same. Bars and strings weigh nothing.

1
Mobile A: which weighs more, a star or a moon? How do you know?
A star. The bar is level, so 1 star balances 2 moons: a star weighs as much as 2 moons.
2
Swap the star for moons. The whole mobile now weighs as much as how many moons?
4 moons: 2 in place of the star, plus the 2 already hanging.
3
How heavy is one moon? One star?
Moon =20\div4=\mathbf{5} g; star =2\times5=\mathbf{10} g. Check: 10=5+5 and 10+10=20 ✓
!
The big one. Mobile B weighs 48 g in total. How heavy is the square? Guess first. Then find the triangle and the circle too.
A 8 g
B 12 g
C 18 g
D 24 g
C: the square is 18 g (triangle 6 g, circle 12 g). The top bar is level, so each side is 48\div2=24 g. The small bar is level, so circle = 2 triangles and the left side is 4 triangles =24 g: triangle 6 g, circle 12 g. Right side: square + triangle =24, so square =24-6=18 g. Check: 6+6+12+18+6=48 ✓
D (24) is half the total: it forgets the triangle hanging under the square. A (8) spreads 48 g evenly over 6 shapes, ignoring the balance clues. B (12) guesses the square matches the circle because they hang at the same height.
Today’s lesson: every level bar is an equation. Solving the mobile used substitution (swap the circle for 2 triangles) — the whole unit is about tools like that.

Think Elimination ends with 0=0. A classmate writes “x=0”. What does 0=0 really tell you?
Both variables vanished and what is left is always true, whatever x and y are. So every point that satisfies one equation satisfies the other: the two equations are the same line, with infinitely many solutions. It says nothing about x being 0. (A false leftover such as 0=5 means no point works: parallel lines.)

Three methods, one crossing point

Graphing Lesson 1A

By hand: rearrange to y=mx+b, plot b, use the slope; read the crossing point.
Graphing calculator TI-83/84: Y= both equations, choose a WINDOW, then 2nd TRACE 5:intersect.
Verify in both equations.

Substitution Lesson 2

  1. Solve one equation for one variable.
  2. Substitute into the other equation.
  3. Solve for the remaining variable.
  4. Back-substitute; verify in both.

Elimination Lesson 3

  1. Scale one or both equations (every term) so one variable has matching or opposite coefficients.
  2. Subtract (matching) or add (opposite).
  3. Solve; back-substitute; verify in both.
!
Verify in BOTH equations. A point that works in only one equation is on only one line. The solution is the one point on both.

Example 3

A trip out and a trip back

(Multiple Choice) Sam walks at 6 km/h and runs at 10 km/h. Going from his house to school he walks part of the way and runs the rest, and the trip takes 42 minutes. Coming home he runs twice as far as he ran going to school (and walks the remainder); this trip takes 34 minutes. The distance between Sam’s house and school is
4 km
5 km
6 km
7 km
Let w be the distance walked going to school and r the distance run going to school, both in km. The distance between house and school is then w+r, and the times must be written in hours.
Going: \dfrac{w}{6}+\dfrac{r}{10}=\dfrac{42}{60}=0.7. Multiply every term by 30: \textcolor{#1f5bd6}{\boldsymbol{5w+3r=21}}.
Coming home: he runs 2r, so he walks the rest of the same total distance, (w+r)-2r=w-r. \dfrac{w-r}{6}+\dfrac{2r}{10}=\dfrac{34}{60}. Multiply every term by 30: 5(w-r)+6r=17\Rightarrow\textcolor{#b06a07}{\boldsymbol{5w+r=17}}.
The w-coefficients match, so subtract: (5w+3r)-(5w+r)=21-17\Rightarrow 2r=4\Rightarrow r=2. Then 5w+2=17\Rightarrow 5w=15\Rightarrow w=3.
Check against the words: going \frac{3}{6}+\frac{2}{10}=0.5+0.2=0.7 h =42 min ✓; home \frac{3-2}{6}+\frac{4}{10}=0.1\overline{6}+0.4=0.5\overline{6} h =34 min ✓
Distance =w+r=3+2=\mathbf{5} km. Answer: B
!
Minutes or hours? The speeds are in km/h, so the times must be in hours: 42\text{ min}=\frac{42}{60}\text{ h}=0.7\text{ h}. Writing \frac{w}{6}+\frac{r}{10}=42 mixes units.
Minutes to hours. 42÷60ENTER gives 0.7 h; 34÷60ENTER gives 0.5\overline{6} h, which is why multiplying by 30 (to clear the 6, the 10 and the 60) is easier.

Example 4

Comparing three plans

A phone carrier offers three data plans. Plan 1 costs $25 per month plus $0.50 per GB. Plan 2 costs $18 per month plus $0.90 per GB. Plan 3 costs $70 per month for unlimited data. Let y= total cost per month in dollars and x= number of GB of data used per month.

(a) What factor determines which plan is most economical for a given customer?
The number of GB of data used per month, x. Each plan charges a different rate per GB, so which plan costs least depends entirely on how much data is used.
(b) Write a linear equation for each of the three plans.
Plan 1: \textcolor{#1f5bd6}{\boldsymbol{y=25+0.5x}}   Plan 2: \textcolor{#b06a07}{\boldsymbol{y=18+0.9x}}
Plan 3: \textcolor{#12805a}{\boldsymbol{y=70}} (a horizontal line: the cost does not depend on x)
(c) Determine algebraically the number of GB at which Plans 1 and 2 cost the same amount, and state that common cost.
Set the two costs equal: 25+0.5x=18+0.9x\Rightarrow 7=0.4x\Rightarrow x=17.5 GB
y=25+0.5(17.5)=\$33.75; check Plan 2: 18+0.9(17.5)=\$33.75 ✓
The plans cost the same, $33.75, at 17.5 GB.
(d) For each of Plans 1 and 2, determine the number of GB that could be purchased for $70.
Plan 1: 70=25+0.5x\Rightarrow 0.5x=45\Rightarrow \boldsymbol{x=90} GB
Plan 2: 70=18+0.9x\Rightarrow 0.9x=52\Rightarrow x\approx\boldsymbol{57.8} GB
So $70, the price of unlimited data, buys 90 GB on Plan 1 but only about 57.8 GB on Plan 2.
(f) Graph all three plans on the grid and mark the two crossover points where the cheapest plan changes.
Crossovers \textcolor{#7b4fc4}{(17.5,\ 33.75)} (Plans 1 and 2) and \textcolor{#7b4fc4}{(90,\ 70)} (Plans 1 and 3).
(e) Devise a simple rule that determines which plan is most economical, depending on the expected number of GB of data used per month. Use the graph in (f).
On the graph the cheapest plan is the lowest line. Plan 2 has the lower monthly fee, so it is lowest while usage is small; Plan 1’s cheaper per-GB rate takes over at the crossover (17.5,\ 33.75) (part (c)); Plan 1 in turn reaches $70 at 90 GB (part (d)), beyond which the flat $70 of Plan 3 is lowest.
Rule: Plan 2 for 0–17.5 GB, Plan 1 for 17.5–90 GB, Plan 3 for more than 90 GB.
Part (d), Plan 2. 52÷.9ENTER gives 57.777\ldots, about 57.8 GB.

Key ideas

How many solutions? Different slopes: one. Equal slopes, different intercepts: none (algebra gives a false statement like 0=5). Same line: infinitely many (0=0).
Choose the method from the system. Decimals or a picture: graph. A variable isolated or with coefficient \pm1: substitution. Matching, opposite or easy-multiple coefficients: elimination.
Always verify in both original equations, then check the answer against the words: units (hours, not minutes), whole counts, positive lengths.
Crossing points decide. Where two cost lines cross, the cheaper option changes. The best plan is the lowest line between crossings.
