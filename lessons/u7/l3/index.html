The General Form of a Linear Equation

Writing Ax+By+C=0, reading slope and intercepts back out of it, and using it to test parallel and perpendicular lines.

I can…
  • rewrite an equation in general form Ax+By+C=0: integers, A\ge0, no common factor
  • find the slope and y-intercept from general form by solving for y, and check with m=-\tfrac{A}{B}
  • find both intercepts straight from general form by covering x, then covering y
  • decide whether lines in general form are parallel or perpendicular
  • build a line from a perpendicular (or parallel) slope and a matching y-intercept, and judge claims about a line

1

Rewriting an Equation in General Form

A linear equation is in general form once every term has been gathered onto one side, leaving the other side equal to zero: Ax+By+C=0.
1
A, B and C are integers (clear any fractions first).
2
A\ge0: the x-term is positive. A horizontal line has A=0; then make B positive instead: write y-4=0, not -y+4=0.
3
A, B and C have no common factor other than 1: divide it out. 10x-4y+20=0 is written 5x-2y+10=0.
General Form of the Equation of a Line → Ax+By+C=0

Unlike y=mx+b, general form can represent every line in the plane, horizontal and vertical lines included. To convert from slope–intercept form, move every term to the left side. If A comes out negative, multiply every term by -1 (or move the terms to the right side instead). If the slope or y-intercept is a fraction, first multiply the entire equation by the common denominator so that A, B and C all come out as integers.

!
Multiply every term, on both sides. y=\tfrac35x+2 times 5 is 5y=3x+10, not 5y=3x+2. The constant needs the 5 too.
i
Shortcut for stations 2 and 3. Move terms to whichever side keeps the x-term positive: y=4x-9 becomes 0=4x-y-9, then read it right to left: 4x-y-9=0. Same answer, no sign fix needed.
Example 1

Slope–intercept form to general form

Convert each equation from slope–intercept form, y=mx+b, to general form Ax+By+C=0, where A, B and C are integers with no common factor.

(a) y=4x-9
Move every term to one side: -4x+y+9=0, then make A positive (or move y to the right instead):
\boldsymbol{4x-y-9=0}
(b) y=\dfrac{3}{5}x+2
Multiply every term by 5 to clear the fraction:
5y=3x+10
\boldsymbol{3x-5y+10=0}
(c) y=-\dfrac{1}{3}x+\dfrac{5}{6}
Denominators 3 and 6; multiply every term by 6:
6y=-2x+5
\boldsymbol{2x+6y-5=0}
(d) Jordan converted y=2x-6 to general form and wrote 4x-2y-12=0. Is Jordan’s equation the same line? Is it written in general form? Explain, and correct it if needed.
Dividing 4x-2y-12=0 by 2 gives 2x-y-6=0, i.e. y=2x-6, so it is the same line. But 4, -2 and -12 share the common factor 2, so it is not in general form. Divide it out: \boxed{2x-y-6=0} (moving the terms of y=2x-6 to the right side, 0=2x-y-6, gives this directly).

Why the rules? One line, many equations

Multiplying every term by the same number never changes the line, so every line has endless equations. The three rules pick out exactly one of them, so everyone’s answer looks the same.

2

Extracting Slope and Intercepts from General Form

Once an equation is in general form, the slope and y-intercept are recovered by solving for y: exactly the reverse of Example 1. Isolate the By term, then divide every term by B; the result is in slope–intercept form.

Either intercept can also be read straight off the general form without solving for y: substitute x=0 for the y-intercept, and y=0 for the x-intercept.

Example 2

Slope and y-intercept from general form

(a) & (b) Determine the slope and y-intercept of the graph of each line.

(a) 3x-4y+20=0
3x-4y+20=0
-4y=-3x-20
y=\tfrac{3}{4}x+5
slope =\boldsymbol{\tfrac{3}{4}}, y-int =\boldsymbol{5}
(b) 8x+3y-21=0
8x+3y-21=0
3y=-8x+21
y=-\tfrac{8}{3}x+7
slope =\boldsymbol{-\tfrac{8}{3}}, y-int =\boldsymbol{7}
(c) The lines 4x-5y+15=0 and 6x-ky-9=0 have the same y-intercept. Determine the value of k.
4x-5y+15=0\Rightarrow -5y=-4x-15\Rightarrow y=\tfrac{4}{5}x+3, so its y-intercept is 3.
6x-ky-9=0\Rightarrow -ky=-6x+9\Rightarrow y=\tfrac{6}{k}x-\tfrac{9}{k}, so its y-intercept is -\tfrac{9}{k}.
Set them equal: -\tfrac{9}{k}=3\Rightarrow -9=3k\Rightarrow \boldsymbol{k=-3}.
Check: k=-3 gives 6x+3y-9=0\Rightarrow y=-2x+3, which has y-intercept 3 ✓.
Check (a) with the rule. Slope -\tfrac{A}{B}: (−)3÷(−)4ENTER gives 0.75=\tfrac34. y-intercept -\tfrac{C}{B}: (−)20÷(−)4ENTER gives 5.

Example 2(c): a whole family of lines

A letter in the equation makes a different line for every value. Find the one value that gives the right y-intercept.

General rule. For the line Ax+By+C=0 (with B\ne0), solving for y gives
By=-Ax-C\quad\Longrightarrow\quad y=-\dfrac{A}{B}\,x-\dfrac{C}{B},\qquad\text{so}\qquad m=-\dfrac{A}{B},\qquad y\text{-intercept}=-\dfrac{C}{B}.
Setting y=0 gives Ax+C=0, so the x-intercept is -\dfrac{C}{A} (when A\ne0). Use the rule as a quick check on your rearranging: in Example 2(a), 3x-4y+20=0 gives m=-\tfrac{3}{-4}=\tfrac{3}{4} and y-intercept -\tfrac{20}{-4}=5.
3

Perpendicular Lines in General Form

Parallel: two non-vertical lines are parallel exactly when their slopes are equal. All vertical lines are parallel.
Perpendicular: two non-vertical lines are perpendicular exactly when their slopes are negative reciprocals, that is, when their slopes multiply to -1. A horizontal line and a vertical line are always perpendicular.

Since the slope can be pulled out of any general-form equation, lines given in general form are tested by comparing those slopes.

Example 3

Testing three lines for perpendicularity

Which of the following lines is/are perpendicular to the line 5x-3y+6=0?

(i) 3x+5y-8=0
Given line: 5x-3y+6=0\Rightarrow y=\tfrac{5}{3}x+2, slope \tfrac53, so a perpendicular line needs slope -\tfrac35.
5y=-3x+8\Rightarrow y=-\tfrac{3}{5}x+\tfrac{8}{5}
slope =-\tfrac35: perpendicular
(ii) x+3y-9=0
3y=-x+9\Rightarrow y=-\tfrac{1}{3}x+3
slope =-\tfrac13\ne-\tfrac35: not perpendicular
(iii) 2x-5y+10=0
-5y=-2x-10\Rightarrow y=\tfrac{2}{5}x+2
slope =\tfrac25\ne-\tfrac35: not perpendicular.
Only (i) is perpendicular to 5x-3y+6=0.
Do the slopes multiply to -1? Use the fraction key: 5a b/c3×(−)3a b/c5ENTER gives -1 for line (i). For line (ii), \tfrac53\times\left(-\tfrac13\right) gives -\tfrac59, not -1.
4

Writing an Equation from Given Conditions

A line whose slope is described only indirectly (through a parallel or perpendicular relationship to another line) and whose y-intercept must match that of a third line is built in four steps:

1determine the target slope
2determine the target y-intercept
3assemble y=mx+b
4convert to general form if required
Example 4

Building a line from a perpendicular and an intercept

Write the equation of a line which is perpendicular to 3x+4y-8=0 and has the same y-intercept as 5x+y-10=0. Answer in slope–intercept form and in general form.
Target slope: 3x+4y-8=0\Rightarrow 4y=-3x+8\Rightarrow y=-\tfrac{3}{4}x+2, slope -\tfrac34; the perpendicular slope is the negative reciprocal, \tfrac43.
Target y-intercept: 5x+y-10=0\Rightarrow y=-5x+10, so y-intercept =10.
Assemble: \boldsymbol{y=\tfrac{4}{3}x+10} (slope–intercept form)
Multiply by 3, then gather every term on the right so the x-term stays positive: 3y=4x+30\Rightarrow 0=4x-3y+30, so \boldsymbol{4x-3y+30=0} (general form)
Example 5

Judging three statements about one line

General form is also useful for quickly checking claims about a line (its slope, its intercepts, or whether it passes through a given point) without first solving for y.

A student made the following statements about the line with equation 4x-3y+18=0.
Statement 1: The line has a slope of 4.
Statement 2: The line is parallel to 8x-6y+15=0.
Statement 3: The line passes through (3,\ 10).
Which of the above statement(s) is/are true?
1 and 2 only
1 and 3 only
2 and 3 only
some other combination
Use the general rule, m=-\tfrac{A}{B} and y-int =-\tfrac{C}{B}, straight from general form. 4x-3y+18=0: m=-\tfrac{4}{-3}=\tfrac43, y-int =-\tfrac{18}{-3}=6.
Statement 1 claims a slope of 4 (the coefficient of x), but the slope is \tfrac43. False.
Statement 2: 8x-6y+15=0: m=-\tfrac{8}{-6}=\tfrac43, y-int =-\tfrac{15}{-6}=\tfrac52. Same slope, different y-intercept (\tfrac52 vs. 6), so the lines are parallel. True.
Statement 3: substitute (3,\ 10): 4(3)-3(10)+18=12-30+18=0 ✓, so the point is on the line. True.
Statements 2 and 3 only: C.
Statement 3 on the calculator. Substitute and look for 0: 4×3−3×10+18ENTER gives 0, so (3,\ 10) is on the line.
✓

Key ideas

General form Ax+By+C=0. Integers, A\ge0 (if A=0, B>0), no common factor. Tidy up: clear fractions (× LCD, every term), move everything to one side, fix the sign, divide out a common factor.
Slope and y-intercept. Isolate By, divide every term by B: y=-\tfrac{A}{B}x-\tfrac{C}{B}. So m=-\tfrac{A}{B} and y-int =-\tfrac{C}{B}: a quick check.
Intercepts. Cover x (x=0) for the y-intercept; cover y (y=0) for the x-intercept, -\tfrac{C}{A}. A point is on the line when substituting gives 0.
Parallel and perpendicular. Equal slopes: parallel. Slopes multiplying to -1: perpendicular. To build a line: target slope, target y-intercept, assemble y=mx+b, convert.
