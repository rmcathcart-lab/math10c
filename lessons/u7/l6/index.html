Slope in Context: Interpreting Rates of Change

Slope as a rate of change with units: read it off a graph, give the intercept a meaning, find average rates between two points, and model base pay plus commission.

I can…
  • read a rate of change off a graph as \dfrac{\text{rise}}{\text{run}} with units (vertical units per horizontal unit) and say it in a sentence: increasing or decreasing
  • write the equation of a rate-of-change graph in the form y=mx+b and explain the slope (the rate) and the vertical intercept (the starting value) in context
  • calculate the average rate of change between two points, such as the average speed over each leg of a trip
  • model base pay plus commission as E=mS+b and use the equation forwards and backwards
RF3 Demonstrate an understanding of slope with respect to: rise and run; line segments and lines; rate of change; parallel lines; perpendicular lines.
RF7 Determine the equation of a linear relation, given: a graph; a point and the slope; two points; a point and the equation of a parallel or perpendicular line, to solve problems.
Warm-up

How Fast Does It Grow?

Too slow to watch, but every one of these has a speed.

1
How much longer is your hair after 6 months?
6\times1\text{ cm}: about 6 cm.
2
How much does a fingernail grow in 1 year? Answer in cm.
12\times3\text{ mm}=36\text{ mm}: about 3.6 cm per year.
3
Nail, hair or lawn: which grows fastest? (Careful: the units are all different!)
The lawn, by far. In cm per month: nail \approx0.3, hair \approx1, lawn \approx5\times4=20. The lawn’s “5” looks small, but its time unit is a week. Rates only compare in the same units.
!
The big one. North America and Europe are slowly drifting apart, so the Atlantic gets a little wider every year. Their speed is closest to… Guess first.
A a snail crawling
B your hair growing
C your nails growing
D the lawn growing
C: your fingernails. The Atlantic widens by about 2.5 cm per year; nails grow about 3.6 cm per year (question 2).
A a snail covers about 1 mm per second: tens of kilometres a year. B hair is about 12 cm per year, roughly 5 times the drift. D the lawn would be over 2 m a year: about 100 times too fast (question 3’s trap).
Over 100 years that is only about 2.5 m, but over 100 million years it is about 2\,500 km: enough to open an ocean.
+
Early finishers. At 2.5 cm per year, how many years does it take the Atlantic to widen by 1 m?
100\div2.5=40 years.

Could you ever actually watch one of these grow?

1

Reading a Rate of Change from a Graph

For a straight-line graph the slope is the same no matter which two points on the line are used. When the axes measure real quantities, this constant ratio is the rate of change: how much the vertical quantity changes for each one-unit increase in the horizontal quantity.
m=\dfrac{\color{#7b4fc4}\text{rise}}{\color{#c27a0e}\text{run}}=\dfrac{y_2-y_1}{x_2-x_1}
Positive slope
the vertical quantity is increasing
Negative slope
the vertical quantity is decreasing
Units
straight from the axes: vertical units per horizontal unit

Watch a story and its graph run together: as time ticks along the horizontal axis, the quantity on the vertical axis changes, and the slope says how fast.

!
Answer with a sentence carrying units. “The distance is increasing at 25 km/h”, not just “25”. A bare number does not say what is changing, in which direction, or how fast.
Example 1

Reading a rate of change off a graph

Each graph below shows a linear relationship between two real-world quantities.

(a) The distance travelled by a cyclist as a function of time.
Use m=\dfrac{\text{rise}}{\text{run}}=\dfrac{y_2-y_1}{x_2-x_1} to calculate the slope of the line, then complete:
“The distance is increasing at the rate of 25 km/h.”
m=\dfrac{100-50}{4-2}=\dfrac{50}{2}=25
The slope is positive, so the distance is increasing at the rate of 25 km/h.
(Units: km on the vertical axis, hours on the horizontal axis, so km/h.)
(b) The temperature inside an oven as a function of time.
Calculate the slope of the line. What units should be used for this rate of change? Then complete:
“The temperature is increasing at the rate of 55 °F per minute.”
m=\dfrac{340-65}{5-0}=\dfrac{275}{5}=55
The vertical axis is in °F and the horizontal axis in minutes, so the units are °F per minute.
The temperature is increasing at the rate of 55 °F per minute.
Brackets keep the subtractions together. (100−50)÷(4−2)ENTER gives 25. Without the brackets the calculator divides first and gets it wrong.

Say it as a sentence

Every rate sentence has four blanks, and the graph fills all of them: the sign of the slope gives the direction, its size gives the number, and the two axis titles give the units.

2

Writing the Equation of a Rate-of-Change Graph

Given the rate of change (the slope) and the coordinates of one point on the line, substitute the point into y=mx+b and solve for b, or use slope–point form, y-y_1=m(x-x_1), from Lesson 4 and rearrange. Either works whether the rate is positive or negative.

Slope m = the rate of change, with units: how much y changes for each 1 unit of x.
Vertical intercept b = the value of the vertical quantity when the horizontal quantity is 0: a starting amount, a full tank, a fixed fee. State it in context, with units.
Example 2

Equation and interpretation from a fuel-consumption graph

The graph shows the fuel remaining in the tank of a delivery van, N litres, as a function of the distance driven, D kilometres.

(a) Calculate the slope of the line. What units apply here? Then complete:
“The amount of fuel in the tank is decreasing at the rate of 0.4 L per km.”
m=\dfrac{32-80}{150-30}=\dfrac{-48}{120}=-0.4
Litres on the vertical axis, kilometres on the horizontal axis, so the units are L/km.
The slope is negative, so the fuel is decreasing at the rate of 0.4 L per km.
(b) Determine the equation of the line in the form N=mD+b, and state what the value of b means for the van.
N=-0.4D+b. Substitute the point (30,\ 80):
80=-0.4(30)+b=-12+b\ \Rightarrow\ b=92
\boldsymbol{N=-0.4D+92}
Check with (150,\ 32): -0.4(150)+92=-60+92=32 ✓
b=92 is the value of N when D=0: the van set out with 92 L of fuel in the tank.
The slope, then b. (32−80)÷(150−30)ENTER gives -0.4. Then b=80-(-0.4)(30): use (−) for the negative slope.

Two ways to the equation

Substituting into y=mx+b and starting from slope–point form are two roads to the same line. Use whichever you like; the check with the second point works either way.

Every part of the equation tells the story

Change the story and the graph changes with it: a different starting value slides the line up or down (same rate, parallel lines); a different rate tilts it about the starting value.

Think Two vans use fuel at the same rate, but one starts with more fuel. How do their graphs compare? Which one runs dry farther along the road?
Same rate means the same slope, so the lines are parallel. The van with more fuel has the higher vertical intercept, so its line meets the horizontal axis farther right: it runs dry later.
3

Average Rate of Change Between Two Points

Not every situation runs at one steady rate: a car may travel quickly on the highway for a while and slowly through towns the next while. Join any two data points with a straight segment: the slope of that segment is the average rate of change between the two points, the constant rate that would have produced the same overall change even though the actual rate varied in between. Different pairs of points on the same graph give different average rates of change.

Key idea. On a graph of distance as a function of time, the slope of the segment joining two points is the average speed between those two points.
Example 3

Average speed over each leg of a trip

The graph records a road trip by car at four points: O(0,0), P(2,80), Q(5,260) and R(7,320), where distance is in kilometres and time is in hours. Calculate the average speed of the car over each interval below.

(a) between O and P
m=\dfrac{80-0}{2-0}=\dfrac{80}{2}=\boldsymbol{40} km/h
(b) between P and Q
m=\dfrac{260-80}{5-2}=\dfrac{180}{3}=\boldsymbol{60} km/h
(c) between Q and R
m=\dfrac{320-260}{7-5}=\dfrac{60}{2}=\boldsymbol{30} km/h
(d) for the whole trip, O to R
m=\dfrac{320-0}{7-0}=\dfrac{320}{7}=45\tfrac{5}{7}\approx\boldsymbol{45.7} km/h
The car was fastest from P to Q and slowest from Q to R; the whole-trip average lies between the fastest and slowest legs.
(d) as a decimal and as a mixed number. 320÷7ENTER gives 45.714…; 320a b/c7ENTER shows the fraction 320┘7; then 2ndd/e turns it into 45┘5┘7, which means 45\tfrac{5}{7}.

Average rate of change on a curve

When the graph is a curve, there is no single slope: the rate keeps changing. The segment joining two points (a secant) still has a slope, and that slope is the average rate of change between those two points.

4

Modelling Base Pay Plus Commission

Many jobs pay a fixed base amount plus a variable amount that depends on performance, such as commission on sales. If earnings E depend on sales S linearly, E=mS+b, then:

the slope m is the commission rate (a decimal, usually quoted as a percent): dollars earned per dollar of sales
the vertical intercept b is the fixed base pay: the amount earned on zero sales
Example 4

Base salary plus commission

Maria is paid a weekly base salary plus commission for selling kitchen appliances. One week her sales totalled $2 800 and she earned $560. The next week her sales totalled $4 600 and she earned $740.

(a) On the grid, plot the two ordered pairs (Sales, Earnings) described above and join them.
Tap the grid to plot a point (drag to adjust, tap it again to remove it).
Plot (2\,800,\ 560) and (4\,600,\ 740), then join them with a ruler.
(b) Calculate the slope of the line segment joining the two points, and explain what it represents in this context.
m=\dfrac{740-560}{4\,600-2\,800}=\dfrac{180}{1\,800}=0.1
Maria’s earnings rise by $0.10 for every $1 of sales. The slope is her commission rate.
(c) State, as a percent, Maria’s commission rate. Then calculate her fixed weekly base salary and write the equation of the line in the form E=mS+b.
Commission rate =0.1=\boldsymbol{10\%}.
E=0.1S+b; substitute (2\,800,\ 560):
560=0.1(2\,800)+b=280+b\ \Rightarrow\ b=280
Her fixed weekly base salary is $280: what she earns on $0 of sales.
\boldsymbol{E=0.10S+280}
Check: 0.1(4\,600)+280=460+280=740 ✓
(d) Three weeks ago Maria earned $410. Use your equation from part (c) to calculate her sales that week.
410=0.1S+280
130=0.1S
S=1\,300
Her sales that week were $1 300.
Slope, base pay, then (d). The slope: (740−560)÷(4600−2800)ENTER. Base pay: 560-0.1\times2\,800. Part (d) undoes the equation: subtract 280, then divide by 0.1.

Use the equation forwards and backwards

Forwards, the equation turns an input into an output: sales into earnings. Backwards, it answers “what input gave this output?”: undo the steps in reverse order.

✓

Key ideas

Slope is a rate of change. m=\dfrac{\text{rise}}{\text{run}} in vertical units per horizontal unit. Positive: increasing; negative: decreasing. Answer with a sentence: “The distance is increasing at 25 km/h.”
The equation tells the story. In y=mx+b, m is the rate and b is the starting value (the value when x=0): N=-0.4D+92 means 92 L at the start, using 0.4 L per km.
Average rate of change between two points is the slope of the segment joining them. On a distance–time graph it is the average speed: \tfrac{320\text{ km}}{7\text{ h}}\approx45.7 km/h for the whole trip.
Base pay plus commission: E=mS+b, with m the commission rate (0.10=10\%) and b the base pay ($280). Use it forwards (sales → earnings) and backwards (earnings → sales).
