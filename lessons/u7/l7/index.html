Reading Slope and Intercepts From an Equation

An equation in general form Ax+By+C=0 becomes slope–intercept form y=mx+b once y is isolated: the coefficient of x is then the slope and the constant is the y-intercept.
Either intercept can also be read straight from general form without solving for y: substitute x=0 and solve for y to get the y-intercept, or y=0 and solve for x to get the x-intercept.
!
Only once y is alone. In 5y=4x-20 the slope is not 4: divide by 5 first. The number in front of x is the slope only when the y has coefficient 1.
Think Why does “covering up” the x-term give the y-intercept?
The y-intercept is the point on the line where x=0. Putting x=0 makes the Ax term zero, so it disappears: covering it up is substituting x=0.

Writing the Equation of a Line From a Graph

A line drawn through lattice points supplies two exact coordinates. Compute the slope between them, then substitute one point into y=mx+b or into y-y_1=m(x-x_1) and rearrange into whichever form the question asks for.
General form, tidied (Lesson 3): A, B and C are integers (clear any fractions first); A\ge0, the x-term is positive (a horizontal line has A=0: then make B positive); and A, B, C have no common factor other than 1.
!
Check with both points. Substitute each lattice point into your general form: the left side must come out to exactly 0. One wrong sign shows up immediately.

Parallel and Perpendicular Lines

Two non-vertical lines are parallel exactly when their slopes are equal; all vertical lines are parallel.
Two non-vertical lines are perpendicular exactly when their slopes are negative reciprocals of one another; equivalently, when the two slopes multiply to -1 (the turned slope triangle in Lesson 2, Section 3 shows why). A vertical line x=k has an undefined slope, so it is excluded from that test; it is perpendicular to every horizontal line y=k, whose slope is 0.
!
Flip and change the sign: both. From -\frac14, flipping alone gives -4 and changing the sign alone gives \frac14; only 4 multiplies to -1. Those two half-mistakes are the usual wrong answers (traps B and C in the warm-up).
Think To test general-form lines quickly, is there a shortcut for the slope of Ax+By+C=0?
Isolating y gives By=-Ax-C, so y=-\frac{A}{B}x-\frac{C}{B} and m=-\frac{A}{B}. For 4y+x+8=0: A=1, B=4, m=-\frac14. (Isolating y every time is just as good, and harder to get wrong.)

Applying Perpendicularity: Altitudes of a Triangle

The altitude from a vertex of a triangle is the segment drawn from that vertex perpendicular to the opposite side. Its equation is built like any other perpendicular: take the negative reciprocal of the side’s slope, then substitute the vertex into slope–point form.
!
Use the side’s slope, through the vertex. The altitude from F uses the slope of the opposite side DE, turned 90°, and the point F. Using DE’s own slope gives a line parallel to the side (trap A in Example 4(a)).

Rates of Change in Applied Contexts

The slope of a linear model is a rate of change: the change in the output quantity per unit change in the input quantity. It carries the units of output per unit of input, and two data points are enough to calculate it even when no equation has been given.
!
Match the units the question asks for. Per km means the run must be in km: convert 2\,400 m to 2.4 km before dividing, or you get -0.006 °C per metre.
Example 5

A rate of change from two measurements

The temperature at the base of a hiking trail is 15.4\,^\circ\text{C}. At an elevation of 2\,400 m above the base, the temperature is 1.0\,^\circ\text{C}. Determine the rate of temperature change, in ^\circ\text{C} per km, to the nearest tenth.
2\,400\text{ m}=2.4\text{ km}.
rate =\dfrac{\Delta\text{temperature}}{\Delta\text{elevation}}=\dfrac{1.0-15.4}{2.4}=\dfrac{-14.4}{2.4}=-6.0
The rate of temperature change is \boldsymbol{-6.0\,^\circ\text{C/km}}: the temperature drops 6.0\,^\circ\text{C} for every kilometre of elevation gained.
Brackets on top. (1−15.4)÷2.4= shows -6. Without the brackets the calculator divides only the 15.4.

Key ideas

Read it: in y=mx+b, m is the slope and b the y-intercept, but only once y is alone. From Ax+By+C=0: isolate y, or cover up a term for an intercept.
Build it: find the slope (two points, a graph, or a parallel/perpendicular line), put a point into y-y_1=m(x-x_1), then tidy into the form asked: integers, A\ge0, no common factor.
Perpendicular: slopes multiply to -1 (flip and change the sign). An altitude is the perpendicular from a vertex to the opposite side. Horizontal ⊥ vertical.
Rate of change = slope with units, output per input: \frac{\Delta\,\text{output}}{\Delta\,\text{input}}. Match the units asked for before dividing; the intercept is the starting value.
