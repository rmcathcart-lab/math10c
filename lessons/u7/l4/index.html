Using Slope–Point Form to Determine the Equation of a Line

Building y-y_1=m(x-x_1) from the slope formula, then using it to turn indirect descriptions of a line into general form.

I can…
  • explain where slope–point form y-y_1=m(x-x_1) comes from: the slope formula with one point fixed and (x,y) free to move along the line
  • write the equation of a line in slope–point form from a point and a slope, and rearrange it to y=mx+b or to general form Ax+By+C=0
  • read the slope and the point back out of a slope–point equation, without falling for the sign trap
  • find the equation of a line from a graph, from two points, from a slope and an intercept, or from a perpendicular line
RF6 Relate linear relations expressed in: slope–intercept form (y=mx+b); general form (Ax+By+C=0); slope–point form (y-y_1=m(x-x_1)) to their graphs.
RF7 Determine the equation of a linear relation, given: a graph; a point and the slope; two points; a point and the equation of a parallel or perpendicular line, to solve problems.

1

Building the Equation from the Slope Formula

Let (x_1,y_1) be a fixed point on a line and let P(x,y) be any other point on that same line, free to move along it. From (x_1,y_1) to P the rise is y-y_1 and the run is x-x_1, so the slope between them is

m=\dfrac{\color{#7b4fc4}y-y_1}{\color{#c27a0e}x-x_1}

Multiplying both sides by (x-x_1) clears the fraction and gives an equation that is also true for (x_1,y_1) itself (both sides become 0), so it is the equation of the entire line:

y-y_1=m(x-x_1)

Walkthrough

A line has slope 4 and passes through A(2,-1). With P(x,y) any point on the line,

\dfrac{y-(-1)}{x-2}=4\quad\Longrightarrow\quad y+1=4(x-2)\quad\Longrightarrow\quad y+1=4x-8\quad\Longrightarrow\quad y=4x-9.

The middle equation, y+1=4(x-2), is exactly the pattern y-y_1=m(x-x_1) with m=4 and (x_1,y_1)=(2,-1) substituted in directly: no cross-multiplying or solving required.

Slope–Point Form (also called point-slope form)
y-\color{#13807a}y_1\color{#1c2230}=\color{#1f5bd6}m\color{#1c2230}(x-\color{#13807a}x_1\color{#1c2230})
where m is the slope and (x₁, y₁) is any known point on the line.
  • Use it whenever a slope and a point are given: it needs no solving, only substitution.
  • A final answer is usually expected in general form, Ax+By+C=0, or in slope–intercept form, y=mx+b, so slope–point form is often just the first step.
  • Any point on the line may be used for (x_1,y_1); different choices give different-looking slope–point equations that all simplify to the same line.
2

Practice with the Slope–Point Form

Substitute the slope for m and the point for (x_1,y_1). Keep the minus signs of the pattern and put each coordinate in brackets first: a negative coordinate turns -(-3) into +3.

Example 1

Writing slope–point form from a point and a slope

State the equation, in slope–point form, of the line through the given point with the given slope.

(a) (4,7), slope 5
\boldsymbol{y-7=5(x-4)}
(b) (2,-3), slope -6
y-(-3)=-6(x-2)
i.e. \boldsymbol{y+3=-6(x-2)}
(c) (-8,-2), slope \dfrac{1}{3}
y-(-2)=\tfrac{1}{3}\bigl(x-(-8)\bigr)
i.e. \boldsymbol{y+2=\tfrac{1}{3}(x+8)}
Example 2

Converting a point and a slope to y=mx+b

In each case the slope of a line and a point on the line are given. Determine the equation of the line in slope–intercept form, y=mx+b.

(a) m=6, point (-4,3)
y-3=6\bigl(x-(-4)\bigr)=6(x+4)
y-3=6x+24
\boldsymbol{y=6x+27}
(b) m=-8, point (-1,5)
y-5=-8\bigl(x-(-1)\bigr)=-8(x+1)
y-5=-8x-8
\boldsymbol{y=-8x-3}
Example 3

Two methods compared: y=mx+b against y-y_1=m(x-x_1)

Priya and Deshawn were each asked: “Determine the equation, in general form, of the line with slope -5 passing through the point (4,-2).” Priya could only remember y=mx+b; Deshawn remembered y-y_1=m(x-x_1). Complete each student’s method, then compare.

(a) Priya’s method. Start from y=mx+b, substitute the slope, then substitute the point to solve for b.
y=-5x+b. Substitute (4,-2):
-2=-5(4)+b=-20+b
b=18
\boldsymbol{y=-5x+18}
(b) Deshawn’s method. Start from y-y_1=m(x-x_1) and substitute the slope and the point directly.
y-(-2)=-5(x-4)
y+2=-5x+20
\boldsymbol{y=-5x+18}
(c) Convert both final answers to general form, Ax+By+C=0. What do you notice?
Both reach y=-5x+18. Adding 5x-18 to both sides gives \boldsymbol{5x+y-18=0}.
The two methods always agree: substituting the point into y-y_1=m(x-x_1) builds the point into the equation, so the separate step of solving for b is not needed.
Example 4

From a grid to general form; reading m and (x_1,y_1)

(a) The line on the grid passes through at least two points with integer coordinates. Determine the equation of the line in general form.

The line passes through (-3,-4) and (1,4).
m=\dfrac{4-(-4)}{1-(-3)}=\dfrac{8}{4}=2
Using (1,4): y-4=2(x-1), so y-4=2x-2 and y=2x+2.
\boldsymbol{2x-y+2=0}. Check (-3,-4): 2(-3)-(-4)+2=-6+4+2=0 ✓
The slope in one go. Bracket the top and the bottom: (4−(−)4)÷(1−(−)3)ENTER gives 2.

(b) Each equation below is written in slope–point form. State the slope of the line and the coordinates of the point used to write the equation.

(i) y-5=-\dfrac{2}{3}(x+9)
y-5=-\tfrac{2}{3}\bigl(x-(-9)\bigr)
\boldsymbol{m=-\tfrac{2}{3}}, point \boldsymbol{(-9,5)}
(ii) y+7=4x
y-(-7)=4(x-0)
\boldsymbol{m=4}, point \boldsymbol{(0,-7)}
(iii) y=-6(x-10)
y-0=-6(x-10)
\boldsymbol{m=-6}, point \boldsymbol{(10,0)}
!
The sign trap. The pattern has minus signs built in: y-y_1 and x-x_1. So the numbers you see in the equation have the opposite signs of the point’s coordinates: x+9 means x-(-9), so x_1=-9. A missing term means a coordinate of 0.
3

Using Slope–Point Form for Indirect Descriptions of a Line

Three forms are now available:

Ax+By+C=0General form
y=mx+bSlope–intercept form
y-y_1=m(x-x_1)Slope–point form

Slope–intercept form is the natural choice when the slope m and the y-intercept b are both handed to you. Slope–point form is more flexible: it needs the slope and any single point (x_1,y_1), which need not be the y-intercept.

Most questions do not state a slope and a point outright. Instead the line is described indirectly: through two points on it, through its relationship (parallel or perpendicular) to another line, or through an intercept it shares with another line. The work is turning those clues into a slope and a point; slope–point form then produces the equation, and clearing fractions and collecting terms converts it to general form.

General form, tidied up (Lesson 3): A, B and C are integers (clear fractions first), A\ge0 (the x-term is positive), and they have no common factor other than 1.
Example 5

General form of the line through two points

Given the points P(4,-3) and Q(-2,9), determine the equation, in general form, of the line passing through P and Q.

m=\dfrac{9-(-3)}{-2-4}=\dfrac{12}{-6}=-2
Using P(4,-3): y-(-3)=-2(x-4), so y+3=-2x+8
\boldsymbol{2x+y-5=0}. Check Q(-2,9): 2(-2)+9-5=-4+9-5=0 ✓
The slope on the calculator. (9−(−)3)÷((−)2−4)ENTER gives -2. Without the brackets the calculator divides first and gives 3.5: wrong.
Example 6

General form from a slope and an x-intercept

Determine the equation, in general form, of the line with slope \dfrac{3}{4} and an x-intercept of 8.

An x-intercept of 8 means the line passes through (8,0).
y-0=\tfrac{3}{4}(x-8). Multiply by 4: 4y=3(x-8)=3x-24
\boldsymbol{3x-4y-24=0}. Check (8,0): 3(8)-4(0)-24=0 ✓
Example 7

General form from a point and a perpendicular line

Determine the equation, in general form, of the line that passes through the point (-4,2) and is perpendicular to the line with equation 2x-3y+12=0.

2x-3y+12=0\ \Rightarrow\ y=\dfrac{2x+12}{3}=\tfrac{2}{3}x+4, so m_{\text{given}}=\tfrac{2}{3}.
Negative reciprocal: m_{\perp}=-\tfrac{3}{2}.
Using (-4,2): y-2=-\tfrac{3}{2}(x+4). Multiply by 2: 2y-4=-3x-12
\boldsymbol{3x+2y+8=0}. Check: 3(-4)+2(2)+8=-12+4+8=0 ✓
Example 8

A perpendicular slope and a shared x-intercept

Determine the equation, in general form, of the line that is perpendicular to the line 4x-9y-27=0 and has the same x-intercept as the line 3x-5y-15=0.

x-intercept of 3x-5y-15=0: set y=0, 3x-15=0, x=5, so the line passes through (5,0).
4x-9y-27=0\ \Rightarrow\ y=\dfrac{4x-27}{9}=\tfrac{4}{9}x-3, so m_{\text{given}}=\tfrac{4}{9} and m_{\perp}=-\tfrac{9}{4}.
Using (5,0): y-0=-\tfrac{9}{4}(x-5). Multiply by 4: 4y=-9x+45
\boldsymbol{9x+4y-45=0}. Check: 9(5)+4(0)-45=0 ✓
✓

Key ideas

Where it comes from. Fix one point (x_1,y_1), let (x,y) move along the line: \dfrac{y-y_1}{x-x_1}=m always. Clear the fraction: \boldsymbol{y-y_1=m(x-x_1)}.
Substitute, then tidy. Slope for m, point for (x_1,y_1), in brackets: y-(-3) becomes y+3. Expand and isolate y for y=mx+b; clear fractions and collect for Ax+By+C=0.
Reading it back. The signs in the brackets are the opposite of the point’s: y-5=-\tfrac23(x+9) has m=-\tfrac23 and point (-9,5). Any point on the line works, so one line has many slope–point equations.
Indirect clues. Turn them into a slope (slope formula, parallel: same, perpendicular: negative reciprocal) and a point (given, either of two points, an intercept (a,0) or (0,b)). Then slope–point form, then general form, then check a point.
