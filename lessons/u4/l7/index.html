Extension — Solving Polynomial Equations by Factoring

So far we have factored expressions. Put one in an equation with a 0 on the other side and factoring does something new: the Zero Product Law turns one hard equation into two easy ones. Then we use it on area, geometry and number problems.

I can…
  • explain the Zero Product Law: if A\cdot B=0, then A=0 or B=0
  • rearrange an equation so one side is 0, factor the other side, and solve each little equation
  • check every solution by substituting it back into the original equation
  • build an equation from a word problem, solve it, and reject a root that doesn’t fit the context (like a negative length)
AN5 Demonstrate an understanding of common factors and trinomial factoring, concretely, pictorially and symbolically. Extension This lesson uses AN5’s factoring; solving equations itself is Math 20-1 and is not part of AN5.

-5(9)+60=\boldsymbol{15} m again. At 1 s it is on the way up; at 3 s it passes the same height on the way down.

The big one. When does the ball land (back at h=0)? Guess first.
A 2 s
B 4 s
C 5 s
D 20 s
B, 4 s. Landing means h=0:  -5t(t-4)=0, so -5t=0 or t-4=0, giving t=0 or t=4. t=0 is the launch, so it lands at 4 s.
A (2 s) is the top of the flight, not the landing. C is read off the -5 and D off the 20. Turning “when is it 0?” into “which factor is 0?” is today’s whole lesson.
+
Bonus: how high does it get?
20 m, at t=2 s. The flight is symmetric (question 2), so the top is halfway between t=0 and t=4: -5(4)+40=20.

Try it: toss an eraser gently straight up. Is the trip down as long as the trip up?

1

The Zero Product Law

Extension

If two or more factors are multiplied together and the result is 0, then at least one of those factors must itself be 0.

Zero Product Law\text{if } A\cdot B=0,\ \text{ then } A=0\ \text{ or }\ B=0\ \text{(or both)}.

For example: x-6=0 only when x=6, and x+2=0 only when x=-2. So (x-6)(x+2)=0 whenever either factor is zero — that is, when x=6 or x=-2. A nonzero constant out front, as in 3(x-6)(x+2)=0, changes nothing, since 3 can never be zero.

A value that makes both sides equal satisfies the equation. The full list of such values is the solution.
Factoring x^2-4x-12=0 gives (x-6)(x+2)=0, so the solutions are x=6 and x=-2, written x=-2,\ 6.
!
Two answers, both needed. “x=-2,\ 6” means x can be -2 or 6: two separate values, not a range from -2 to 6. Stopping after one is only half the solution.
Example 1

The Zero Product Law on a simple trinomial

Extension

Solve the equation x^2-5x-14=0 by factoring the left side and applying the Zero Product Law.

Two numbers with product -14 and sum -5: -7 and 2.
(x-7)(x+2)=0, so x-7=0 or x+2=0
Solutions: x=-2,\ 7   Check: 7^2-5(7)-14=49-35-14=0 ✓; (-2)^2-5(-2)-14=4+10-14=0 ✓
Think Can a quadratic equation have only one solution? Try x^2-6x+9=0.
Yes. It’s a perfect square trinomial (Lesson 5): (x-3)(x-3)=0. Both factors give the same little equation, x-3=0, so the only solution is \boldsymbol{x=3}.

Solving Equations by Factoring

Extension

To solve a polynomial equation by factoring:

i) Rearrange the equation, if necessary, so that one side is exactly 0.
ii) Factor the nonzero side completely (common factor, difference of squares, or trinomial factoring).
iii) Set each factor equal to 0 and solve the resulting simple equations.
iv) State all the solutions.
Step i) is not optional. The Zero Product Law says nothing about factors whose product is 6 — only about factors whose product is 0. So x^2+5x=6 must first be rearranged to x^2+5x-6=0.

One equation becomes two little ones

Here are all four steps at once. Watch the factored form fork: each factor becomes its own little equation, and each little equation gives one solution. Finish by putting each solution back into the original equation.

Example 2

Common factors and differences of squares

Extension

Solve each equation.

(a) x^2-64=0
Difference of squares: (x-8)(x+8)=0
x-8=0 or x+8=0
\boldsymbol{x=\pm 8}
(b) 9x^2-25=0
Difference of squares: (3x-5)(3x+5)=0
3x=5 or 3x=-5
\boldsymbol{x=\tfrac{5}{3},\ -\tfrac{5}{3}}
(c) 6x^2-42x=0
Common factor 6x: 6x(x-7)=0
6x=0 gives x=0; x-7=0 gives x=7
\boldsymbol{x=0,\ 7}
(d) 6x^2-54=0
Common factor 6, then a difference of squares: 6(x-3)(x+3)=0
The constant 6 can never be 0, so only the brackets matter.
\boldsymbol{x=\pm 3}
Think x^2-64=0 has two solutions. How many does x^2+64=0 have?
None. A sum of squares never factors (Lesson 3), and no real number works: x^2 is never negative, so x^2+64 is always at least 64.
!
Don’t divide by x. Dividing 6x^2=42x by 6x gives x=7 and silently loses x=0. Factor the x out instead.
!
Squares have two roots. x^2=64 means x=8 or x=-8, since (-8)^2=64 too. Factoring finds both automatically.

When one side is not yet zero

Solve each equation. (Hint: rearrange first so one side is 0.)

(a) 4x^2-4x=15

Problem Solving with Polynomial Equations

Extension

Word problems follow this procedure.

i) Introduce a variable to represent an unknown quantity.
ii) Use the given information to build a polynomial equation.
iii) Solve the equation using the factoring methods from this lesson.
iv) Interpret the solution(s) in the context of the problem, rejecting any answer that does not make sense (for example, a negative length).
Step iv) matters as much as the algebra. A quadratic almost always has two roots, and in a measurement problem one is usually impossible. Reject it explicitly, and say why.
Example 4

Area of a banner

Extension

The area of a rectangular banner is 208\text{ cm}^2. The length of the banner is 3 cm more than its width. Form a polynomial equation and solve it to determine the perimeter of the banner.

Let w = the width in cm; the length is w+3.  w(w+3)=208\ \Rightarrow\ w^2+3w-208=0
Product -208, sum 3: 16 and -13.  (w+16)(w-13)=0\ \Rightarrow\ w=-16 or w=13
Reject w=-16: a width cannot be negative. So w=13 cm and the length is 16 cm.
Perimeter =2(13+16)=58.  The perimeter is 58 cm. (Check: 13\times16=208 ✓)
Hunting for the pair? The width and length are close together, so both are near \sqrt{208}: 2ndx²208ENTER gives about 14.4. Try factor pairs of 208 near there: 13\times16 differs by 3 ✓
Example 5

Cross-section of a water trough

Extension

The diagram shows the cross-section of a water trough whose sloping sides AD and BC make an angle of 45^\circ with the horizontal. The length DC=40 cm.

(a) Show that the area of the cross-section is x(40-x)\text{ cm}^2.
Each end triangle is right-angled with a 45^\circ angle, so it is isosceles: its horizontal leg is x, like its depth. So AB=40-2x.
Trapezoid: A=\tfrac12(40+40-2x)(x)=\tfrac12(80-2x)x=\boldsymbol{x(40-x)} ■
(b) If the area of the cross-section is 204\text{ cm}^2, determine the value of x.
x(40-x)=204\Rightarrow 40x-x^2=204\Rightarrow x^2-40x+204=0
Product 204, sum -40: -34 and -6.  (x-34)(x-6)=0
Reject x=34: AB=40-2x must be positive, so x<20; x=34 gives AB=-28 cm.
\boldsymbol{x=6} cm (Check: 6(40-6)=6(34)=204 ✓)
Example 6

Consecutive even integers

Extension

The product of two consecutive positive even integers is 168. Form a polynomial equation and solve it to find the two integers.

Let n = the smaller even integer; the next even integer is n+2.  n(n+2)=168\ \Rightarrow\ n^2+2n-168=0
Product -168, sum 2: 14 and -12.  (n+14)(n-12)=0\ \Rightarrow\ n=-14 or n=12
Reject n=-14: the integers must be positive. So n=12, and the next even integer is 14.
The two integers are 12 and 14. (Check: 12\times14=168 ✓)
!
“Consecutive even” means +2, not +1. n and n+1 would be one even and one odd number.
Estimate first. Two close numbers multiplying to 168 are each near \sqrt{168}\approx13.0. So try 12 and 14.
✓

Key ideas

Extension · Math 20-1 preview
Zero Product Law: if A\cdot B=0, then A=0 or B=0. It works only for 0: a product of 6 tells you nothing about the factors.
Four steps: i) make one side 0, ii) factor completely, iii) set each factor =0 and solve, iv) state all solutions. A constant factor like 6 is never 0.
Check each solution by substituting it into the original equation. Never divide both sides by x: it throws away x=0.
Word problems: variable → equation → solve → interpret. Two roots, often one answer: reject a negative length (or a root that breaks the story) and say why.
