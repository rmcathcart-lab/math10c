Common Factors: The GCF of Monomials and Polynomials

In Unit 3 we multiplied: 3x(x+4)=3x^2+12x. Factoring is expanding run backwards: start with 3x^2+12x and find the factors that made it. Today’s first tool is the greatest common factor: find the biggest thing every term shares, and pull it out front.

I can…
  • explain why expanding and factoring are opposite processes
  • find the GCF of a set of monomials, from prime factorizations and by inspection (lowest power of each common letter)
  • factor a polynomial by removing the GCF, dividing every term by it, and check by expanding
  • take out a negative GCF when the leading coefficient is negative
  • (Extension) remove a fractional common factor so the bracket has whole-number coefficients
AN5 Demonstrate an understanding of common factors and trinomial factoring, concretely, pictorially and symbolically.
Warm-up

Tile the Floor

Cover a 240 cm by 180 cm floor with identical square tiles. No cutting allowed! Tap a tile size to try it.

1
Using 20 cm tiles: how many tiles?
240\div20=12 across, 180\div20=9 up: 12\times9=\boldsymbol{108} tiles.
2
Why won’t 50 cm tiles work? Will 40 cm tiles work?
240\div50=4.8: the last tile in each row would be cut. 40 cm fails too: 240\div40=6 is fine, but 180\div40=4.5. A size must fit both sides.
3
A size works if it divides exactly into and into .
240 and 180: it must be a common factor. Sizes that work: 1, 2, 3, 4, 5, 6, 10, 12, 15, 20, 30 and 60 cm.

The big one. What is the biggest square tile that fits with no cutting? And how many of them do you need? Guess first.
A 30 cm
B 60 cm
C 90 cm
D 120 cm
B, 60 cm: only 12 tiles (240\div60=4 across, 180\div60=3 up). 60 is the greatest common factor of 240 and 180.
A works, but 30 is only a common factor, not the greatest: 8\times6=48 tiles. C fits the 180 side, but 240\div90\approx2.67. D fits across, but 180\div120=1.5.
Today, the same idea with algebra: 240x+180y=60(4x+3y). The 4 and 3 left in the bracket are the tiles across and up.

Challenge your neighbour. A hallway is 210 cm by 150 cm. What is the biggest square tile, and how many does it take?
30 cm, 35 tiles. 210=2\times3\times5\times7 and 150=2\times3\times5\times5, so the GCF is 2\times3\times5=30; 7\times5=35 tiles.

Expanding and Factoring Are Opposite Processes

Multiplying polynomials out is called expanding: it turns a product into a sum or difference of terms.

productsum of terms
monomial × polynomial3x(x+4)3x^2+12x
binomial × binomial → trinomial(x+2)(x+6)x^2+8x+12
(3x+2)(x+5)3x^2+17x+10
binomial × binomial → binomial(x-7)(x+7)x^2-49
Factoring is the reverse: rewriting a sum or difference of terms as a product of factors. Every factoring can be checked by expanding it back.

The Greatest Common Factor (GCF)

The GCF of two whole numbers comes from their prime factorizations. Multiply the prime factors common to both lists:

60=\underline{2}\times\underline{2}\times\underline{3}\times584=\underline{2}\times\underline{2}\times\underline{3}\times7
GCF =2\times2\times3=\boldsymbol{12}

The same idea works for monomials: letters are factors too.

12a^4=\underline{2}\times2\times\underline{3}\times\underline{a}\times\underline{a}\times a\times a18a^2b=\underline{2}\times\underline{3}\times3\times\underline{a}\times\underline{a}\times b
GCF =2\times3\times a\times a=\boldsymbol{6a^2}

The shortcut: by inspection

Take the GCF of the numerical coefficients, then multiply by each common letter raised to its lowest exponent.
The GCF of 14p^2q^3 and 21pq^4 is 7pq^3: GCF(14, 21) = 7, lowest powers p^1 and q^3.
A letter missing from even one monomial is not common, so it doesn’t appear in the GCF. The GCF of 12a^4 and 18a^2b has no b.
Signs. For a set of monomials, the GCF is positive unless every monomial is negative; then take the GCF negative too.
!
Lowest, not highest. It’s tempting to grab the biggest power, but x^4 isn’t a factor of x^3. The GCF can only use what every term has.
Example 1

The GCF of a set of monomials

(a) Write the prime factorization of 18x^3y and 24xy^3, and use it to determine the greatest common factor of 18x^3y and 24xy^3.
18x^3y=2\times3\times3\times x\times x\times x\times y
24xy^3=2\times2\times2\times3\times x\times y\times y\times y
Common: 2,\ 3,\ x,\ y, so GCF =2\times3\times x\times y=\boldsymbol{6xy}

(b) State the greatest common factor of each set of monomials by inspection.

(i) 16ab,\ 20a^2b^3
GCF(16, 20) = 4; lowest a^1,b^1
GCF =\boldsymbol{4ab}
(ii) 27x^4y^2,\ -36x^3y^5
GCF(27, 36) = 9; lowest x^3,y^2. Mixed signs, so positive.
GCF =\boldsymbol{9x^3y^2}
(iii) a^2bc^3,\ 3abc
GCF(1, 3) = 1; lowest a^1,b^1,c^1
GCF =\boldsymbol{abc}
(iv) -30a^3b,\ -18a^2b^2, -24a^2b
All negative, so negative. GCF(30, 18, 24) = 6; lowest a^2,b^1
GCF =\boldsymbol{-6a^2b}

Factoring by Removing the Greatest Common Factor

Think of a polynomial as the area of a rectangle. Factoring it means finding the rectangle’s side lengths. With algebra tiles you can do it by hand: arrange the tiles into equal rows, and the tallest side you can make is the GCF.

Without tiles: divide every term by the GCF

Since 6a^2b(3a+4b)=18a^3b+24a^2b^2, factoring 18a^3b+24a^2b^2 gives 6a^2b(3a+4b): the GCF has been removed from every term, leaving a polynomial factor behind.

18a^3b+24a^2b^2factorexpand6a^2b(3a+4b)
Negative leading coefficient. Remove a negative common factor so the bracket starts with a positive term: -4x^2+10x=-2x(2x-5). Every sign inside the bracket flips. (This is the convention in every answer key in this unit.)
!
A term that equals the GCF leaves a 1. 21x^2y^2-35xy+7x=7x(3xy^2-5y+\boldsymbol{1}), not 7x(3xy^2-5y).
!
Dividing by a negative flips every sign, not just the first: -5mn-15m^2=-5m(n\boldsymbol{+}3m).
Example 2

Filling in a missing factor

Complete the factoring in each case.

(a) 15x+25y=\underline{\qquad}(3x+5y)
5(3x+5y)=15x+25y
Blank =\boldsymbol{5}
(b) 8x^3+20x^2+4x=\underline{\qquad}(2x^2+5x+1)
4x(2x^2+5x+1)=8x^3+20x^2+4x
Blank =\boldsymbol{4x}
(c) 6a^2+30a=6a(a+\underline{\qquad})
6a(a+5)=6a^2+30a
Blank =\boldsymbol{5}
(d) 24p-16q=8(\underline{\qquad}-2q)
8(3p-2q)=24p-16q
Blank =\boldsymbol{3p}
(e) -5mn-15m^2=-5m(\underline{\qquad\quad})
The GCF is negative, so both signs flip: -5m(n+3m)=-5mn-15m^2
Blank =\boldsymbol{n+3m}

Factor completely by removing the GCF

Factor each polynomial completely by removing the greatest common factor.

(a) 18x-8
GCF(18, 8) = 2; no letter is in both terms.
18x-8=\boldsymbol{2(9x-4)}
(b) 20x^4+5x^2
GCF(20, 5) = 5; lowest power x^2.
=\boldsymbol{5x^2(4x^2+1)}
(c) 12a^3b^2+9ab^3+3ab^4
GCF(12, 9, 3) = 3; lowest a^1,b^2.
=\boldsymbol{3ab^2(4a^2+3b+b^2)}
(d) 14p^3-7p^2+21p
GCF(14, 7, 21) = 7; lowest power p^1.
=\boldsymbol{7p(2p^2-p+3)}
(e) 35xy^2z^3-28x^2y^4z^2+42x^3y^2z^5
GCF(35, 28, 42) = 7; lowest x^1,y^2,z^2: GCF =7xy^2z^2.
Quotients 5z, -4xy^2, 6x^2z^3
=\boldsymbol{7xy^2z^2(5z-4xy^2+6x^2z^3)}
Think Lee factored (b) as 5x(4x^3+x). It expands back correctly. Is Lee finished?
No. 4x^3 and x still share an x, so 5x isn’t the greatest common factor. Take out the lowest power, x^2: 5x^2(4x^2+1). Expanding can’t catch this one: always look inside the bracket too.
Example 4

A formula in factored form

The surface area of a cone is given by A=\pi r^2+\pi rs, where r is the radius of the base and s is the slant height.

(i) Determine the surface area of a cone, to the nearest 0.01\ \text{cm}^2, with slant height 8.20 cm and base radius 3.10 cm.
\pi r^2=\pi(3.10)^2=9.61\pi\approx30.1907
\pi rs=\pi(3.10)(8.20)=25.42\pi\approx79.8593
A\approx\boldsymbol{110.05\ \textbf{cm}^2}
(ii) Write the formula for A in factored form.
The GCF of \pi r^2 and \pi rs is \pi r.
A=\boldsymbol{\pi r(r+s)}
(iii) Calculate the surface area, to the nearest 0.01\ \text{cm}^2, using the factored form of A.
A=\pi(3.10)(3.10+8.20)=\pi(3.10)(11.30)=35.03\pi

Extension — Monomial Common Factors Involving Fractions

Extension

When a polynomial has fractional coefficients, let the common factor itself be a fraction. Ask: “what do I multiply this fraction by to rebuild each original term?” Chosen well, it leaves a bracket with whole-number coefficients (an integral polynomial).

\tfrac14x^2-2x=\tfrac14x(x-8)because \tfrac14x\cdot x=\tfrac14x^2 and \tfrac14x\cdot(-8)=-2x
!
Only the numbers become fractions. In \tfrac13\pi r^2-2\pi r=\tfrac13\pi r(r-6), the \pi comes out with the GCF. Leaving a \pi in the bracket means it was taken out twice.

Key ideas

Factoring is expanding run backwards: {3x^2+12x}\rightleftarrows{3x(x+4)}. A polynomial is a rectangle’s area; the factors are its sides. Check any factoring by expanding.
GCF of monomials: the GCF of the numbers × each common letter to its lowest power. A letter missing from one term isn’t common. Negative only if every monomial is.
Remove the GCF: divide every term by it; GCF out front, quotients in the bracket (a term equal to the GCF leaves 1). Negative leading coefficient → take out a negative GCF; every sign flips.
Factored completely? Nothing common left in the bracket. (Extension) A fraction can come out front too: \tfrac14x^2-2x=\tfrac14x(x-8), leaving whole numbers.
