Unit 4 Review: Factoring (AN5)

Laid out like the unit test: Beginning, Progressing and Mastery questions for outcome AN5, then a short Extension part on solving by factoring. First, every factoring tool on one card — and the checklist that tells you which one to reach for.

I can…
  • take out the greatest common factor — a negative one when the leading coefficient is negative
  • factor x^2+bx+c by inspection and ax^2+bx+c by decomposition or an area box, and read an algebra-tile rectangle
  • spot a difference of squares, a perfect square trinomial and a trinomial in disguise — and know that a sum of squares does not factor
  • factor completely (check every bracket again), then verify by expanding
  • reason backwards: find a missing coefficient, explain an error, and (Extension) solve an equation by factoring
AN5 Demonstrate an understanding of common factors and trinomial factoring, concretely, pictorially and symbolically.

Which tool first? The Lesson 6 checklist

Work down the checklist in order, every time — and loop back for every bracket that might factor again.

!
Stopping one step too early. 2(x^2-9) and (9a^2-4)(9a^2+4) are not finished — a bracket is still a difference of squares.
!
Signs in the wrong brackets. (x-3)(x+2) has the right numbers for x^2+x-6 but gives x^2-x-6. Expanding catches it.

Recap: arrange the tiles into a rectangle

x^2x1 white with a minus = negative

The tiles for a trinomial form a rectangle; its side lengths are the factors. When the x-tiles won’t fill the gaps, add zero pairs.

Recap: decomposition and the area box for ax^2+bx+c

Find two integers with product ac and sum b, split the middle term into a 2 × 2 box, and read the factors off the edges.

Check by substitution. At x=2, 6x^2-x-2 gives 24-2-2=20…
…and (2x+1)(3x-2) must give 20 too: 5\times4=20 ✓

Recap: which middle terms work?

x^2+bx+c factors over the integers only when b is the sum of a factor pair of c. List every pair and you have every b that works.

MAS · 19

A rectangle in four regions

19 Rectangle ABCD has been subdivided into four regions, as shown. The areas of two of the regions are b^2 and 81, and the combined area of the other two regions is 18b. The region with area b^2 is a square. Determine the perimeter of rectangle ABCD, in terms of b.
Read the b^2 region as a b-by-b square. Right column width w, bottom row height h: hw=81 and bw+bh=18b, so w+h=18.
P=2[(b+w)+(b+h)]=4b+2(w+h)=\boldsymbol{4b+36}
(In fact w=h=9: the area is b^2+18b+81=(b+9)^2.)
MAS · 20

Find the error

20 Identify and explain the error in each factorization. Then write the correct factored form.
(a) 6x^2-x-2=(3x+2)(2x-1)
Expanding gives 6x^2-3x+4x-2=6x^2+x-2: the signs are in the wrong brackets. Pair for ac=-12, sum -1: -4,\ 3.
Correct: \boldsymbol{(3x-2)(2x+1)}
(b) x^4-81=(x^2-9)^2
(x^2-9)^2=x^4-18x^2+81 has a middle term. A difference of squares gives one − and one + bracket — and x^2-9 factors again.
Correct: \boldsymbol{(x-3)(x+3)(x^2+9)}
MAS · 21

Written response: x^2+bx+20

21 Written response Students are investigating polynomials of the form x^2+bx+c, where b and c are integers, with c=20.
(a) State a polynomial with c=20 which can be factored over the integers.
Any factor pair of 20 works, e.g. 4 and 5:
\boldsymbol{x^2+9x+20=(x+4)(x+5)}
(b) State one which cannot be factored over the integers. Explain why.
E.g. \boldsymbol{x^2+3x+20}: the pairs 1 & 20, 2 & 10, 4 & 5 add to 21, 12, 9 (or their negatives). None adds to 3.
(c) How many polynomials x^2+bx+20 (b a nonzero integer) can be factored over the integers?
c>0, so the pair has the same sign: b=\pm21,\pm12,\pm9.
6 polynomials.

Lesson 7, a Math 20-1 preview · Questions 22–24
Extension
Zero Product Law: if a product is 0, one of the factors must be 0 — if AB=0, then A=0 or B=0. So: get 0 on one side, factor, and set each factor equal to 0.
EXT · 22

(Extension) Solve by factoring

22 Solve by factoring.
(a) x^2-7x+12=0
Pair -3,-4: (x-3)(x-4)=0
x=\boldsymbol{3} or x=\boldsymbol{4}
(b) 2x^2+5x=12
0 on one side first: 2x^2+5x-12=0
ac=-24: 8,-3; (2x-3)(x+4)=0
x=\boldsymbol{\tfrac32} or x=\boldsymbol{-4}
(c) x^2=6x
x^2-6x=0, x(x-6)=0
x=\boldsymbol{0} or x=\boldsymbol{6} — do not divide by x.
EXT · 23

(Extension) Kiran’s shortcut

23 Kiran solves x^2=6x by dividing both sides by x to get x=6. Explain the error, and state the complete solution.
Dividing by x is only allowed when x\neq0 — but x=0 is a solution (0^2=6(0)), so that step throws a root away.
x^2-6x=0,\ x(x-6)=0, so x=\boldsymbol{0} or x=\boldsymbol{6}.
EXT · 24

(Extension) A rectangular array of chairs

24 A rectangular array of chairs has 5 more chairs in each row than there are rows, and 84 chairs in total.
(a) Write and solve an equation for the number of rows.
r rows of r+5: r(r+5)=84
r^2+5r-84=0,\ (r-7)(r+12)=0
r=\boldsymbol{7} or r=\boldsymbol{-12}
(b) State the number of rows and the number of chairs per row, and explain why one root of the equation must be rejected.
7 rows of 12 chairs (7\times12=84 ✓).
Reject r=-12: a number of rows must be a positive whole number.
✓

Key ideas

Factoring is expanding backwards. Every factorization can be checked by expanding it — or by putting x=2 into both forms.
The checklist: GCF first (a negative one if the lead is negative) → 2 terms: A^2-B^2 → 3 terms: inspection or decomposition, watch for perfect squares and degrees 4, 2, 0 → 4 terms: grouping.
Factor completely: check every bracket again. 2(x^2-9) isn’t finished, and a sum of squares A^2+B^2 never factors.
Reason backwards: b must be a factor-pair sum of c; a perfect square needs the middle term 2AB; a known factor fixes the missing number.
