Understanding Powers and the Exponent Laws

6\times6\times6\times6 is a lot to write, so we write 6^{4}. Today we name the parts of a power, see exactly what an exponent reaches, and rebuild all five exponent laws from plain repeated multiplication.

  • evaluate powers with negative bases, and explain why -7^{2} and (-7)^{2} are different

How Long to Crack It?

A thief has your phone and tries every possible code, one by one. Tap a phone to count its codes.

1
How many 2-digit codes are there, from 00 to 99?
100 codes: 10 choices for each digit, so 10\times10=10^{2}=100.
2
How many 4-digit PINs? Write the answer as a power of 10.
10\times10\times10\times10=10^{4} = 10 000 PINs (0000 to 9999).
3
Switching to a 6-digit PIN adds 2 more digits. Fill in: (tap a blank)
The 2 new digits give 10\times10=10^{2} choices for each old PIN: 10^{4}\times10^{2}=10^{6} = 1 000 000. The exponents simply add: 4 + 2 = 6.
!
The big one. Which lock has the most possible codes to try? Guess first.
A an 8-digit PIN
B a 6-letter password (a to z)
C a 4-character password using capitals, lowercase and digits
B, the 6-letter password, with about 3 times as many codes as A. Longer isn’t automatically stronger: 8 slots with only 10 choices lose to 6 slots with 26 choices. And C’s 62 choices per slot is a big base, but only 4 slots. The base and the exponent both matter.

1

What Does an Exponent Mean?

An exponent is shorthand for repeated multiplication of the same factor:

6\times6\times6\times6=6^{4}\qquad\qquad p\times p\times p\times p\times p=p^{5}

Exponential form: a repeated multiplication written the compact way, like 6^{4}.
A power is the whole expression a^{n}, not just the little number.
Base: the factor being repeated. Exponent: how many times the base is used as a factor.
A number multiplying a power is its coefficient. If none is written, it is understood to be 1.
Powers on the calculator. The ^ key raises to any exponent: 6^4ENTER gives 1296. For squares there is a shortcut: 6x²ENTER gives 36.

Coefficients vs. Powers of a Product

Grouping matters. In 5m^{3} only m is repeated three times; the 5 sits out front as a coefficient. In (5m)^{3} the entire quantity 5m is the base:

(5m)^{3}=(5m)\times(5m)\times(5m)=(5\times5\times5)\times(m\times m\times m)=125m^{3}

Brackets act like a fence. The exponent repeats only what is inside the fence. Anything outside is used once. No brackets? Then the fence holds only the single symbol right under the exponent.
!
Watch out. 5m^{3} and (5m)^{3} look alike but are not equal. Try m=2: 5\times2^{3}=40, but (5\times2)^{3}=1000.
Example 2

Where the fence sits

Write each of the following as a repeated multiplication.

(a) 5m^{3}n
Only m is cubed; the 5 and the n stay outside the fence.
5m^{3}n=\mathbf{5\times m\times m\times m\times n}
(b) 5mn^{3}
Only n is cubed this time.
5mn^{3}=\mathbf{5\times m\times n\times n\times n}
(c) 5(mn)^{3}
The fence holds mn, repeated three times; the 5 is still outside.
5\times(mn)\times(mn)\times(mn)=\mathbf{5\times m\times n\times m\times n\times m\times n}
(d) (5mn)^{3}
The fence holds 5mn itself.
(5mn)(5mn)(5mn)=\mathbf{5\times m\times n\times5\times m\times n\times5\times m\times n}
=125m^{3}n^{3}
Think Which two of (a)–(d) have the same number of m’s and n’s but are still not equal? What’s different?
(c) and (d): both have three m’s and three n’s. But in (c) the 5 is outside the fence (used once), and in (d) it is inside (used three times): 5m^{3}n^{3} vs 125m^{3}n^{3}.
3

Evaluating Powers, Including Negative Bases

The fence also settles a subtle question about negative numbers.

-7^{2}: no brackets, so the fence holds only the 7. The minus sign sits outside the power.
-7^{2}=-(7\times7)=-49
(-7)^{2}: the fence holds −7 itself, so −7 is the base that gets multiplied.
(-7)^{2}=(-7)\times(-7)=49

When does the difference disappear?

Each extra factor of a negative number flips the sign. Watch the two kinds of power side by side as the exponent grows.

!
Even exponent: (-a)^{n} is positive but -a^{n} is negative. Read the brackets before you calculate.
!
Odd exponent: they agree. A lone minus out front acts like an odd number of negative factors.
(−) is not −. The TI-30XIIS does powers before negation, just like the fence: (−)7x² gives −49. To square −7, put it in brackets: ((−)7)x² gives 49.
Example 3

-7^{2} is not (-7)^{2}

Evaluate each of the following without a calculator.

(a) -7^{2}
No brackets, so the fence holds only the 7:
-7^{2}=-(7\times7)=\mathbf{-49}
(b) (-7)^{2}
The fence holds −7:
(-7)\times(-7)=\mathbf{49}
(c) -7^{3}
-7^{3}=-(7\times7\times7)
=\mathbf{-343}
(d) (-7)^{3}
(-7)(-7)(-7)=\mathbf{-343}
Odd exponent: (c) and (d) agree.
Think Without multiplying anything out: is (-1)^{100} positive or negative? What about -1^{100}?
(-1)^{100}=\mathbf{1}: 100 negative factors pair up into 50 positives. -1^{100}=-(1^{100})=\mathbf{-1}: the minus is outside the fence.
4

The Zero Exponent

What could 6^{0} mean — “zero factors of 6”? Follow the pattern: lowering the exponent by 1 takes away one factor of 6, so it divides the value by 6.

6^{4}=1296,\quad 6^{3}=216,\quad 6^{2}=36,\quad 6^{1}=6,\quad 6^{0}=\ ?

Zero Exponent Law  a^{0}=1,\quad a\neq0
Watch the fence: in -a^{0} the exponent applies only to a, so -a^{0}=-(a^{0})=-1.

Inside or outside the fence?

The exponent 0 turns whatever is inside its fence into 1. Everything outside the fence is left alone.

Example 4

Zero exponents, inside and outside the fence

Evaluate each of the following.

(a) 11^{0}
Any nonzero base to the power 0:
11^{0}=\mathbf{1}
(b) (-8)^{0}
The base inside the fence is −8:
(-8)^{0}=\mathbf{1}
(c) -8^{0}
The exponent applies only to 8:
-8^{0}=-(8^{0})=\mathbf{-1}
(d) 3\left(5^{2}\right)^{0}
\left(5^{2}\right)^{0}=1 (base 25\neq0), so
3\times1=\mathbf{3}
Think Why does the law say a\neq0? What goes wrong if you try the pattern with a base of 0?
The pattern gets each value by dividing by the base. For 0^{0} that would be 0^{1}\div0=0\div0, and we can’t divide by 0. So 0^{0} is undefined.
Check (b) and (c). ((−)8)^0ENTER gives 1, but (−)8^0ENTER gives −1: the calculator only raises the 8 to the power 0.

The Five Exponent Laws

There’s nothing new to memorize here: every law comes from writing the powers out as repeated multiplication and counting the factors. Pick a law and watch the factors move.

The five laws, side by side (tap a law to reveal it)

Expanded (numerical base)
Variable base
Exponent Law
6^{3}\times6^{2}=(6\cdot6\cdot6)(6\cdot6)=6^{5}
c^{3}\times c^{2}=c^{5}
6^{5}\div6^{2}=\dfrac{6\cdot6\cdot6\cdot\cancel{6}\cdot\cancel{6}}{\cancel{6}\cdot\cancel{6}}=6^{3}
c^{5}\div c^{2}=c^{3}
(6\cdot5)^{3}=(6\cdot5)(6\cdot5)(6\cdot5)=6^{3}\cdot5^{3}
(cd)^{3}=c^{3}d^{3}
\left(\dfrac{6}{5}\right)^{3}=\dfrac{6}{5}\cdot\dfrac{6}{5}\cdot\dfrac{6}{5}=\dfrac{6^{3}}{5^{3}}
\left(\dfrac{c}{d}\right)^{3}=\dfrac{c^{3}}{d^{3}}
\left(6^{3}\right)^{2}=(6\cdot6\cdot6)(6\cdot6\cdot6)=6^{6}
\left(c^{3}\right)^{2}=c^{6}
!
Same base only. 2^{3}\times5^{2} can’t be combined into one power: the factors are different numbers.
!
Add or multiply? c^{3}\cdot c^{2}=c^{5} (product: add), but \left(c^{3}\right)^{2}=c^{6} (power of a power: multiply).
!
Only × and ÷ inside the brackets. (cd)^{2}=c^{2}d^{2}, but (c+d)^{2}\neq c^{2}+d^{2}. Check with c=1,\ d=2: (1+2)^{2}=9, but 1^{2}+2^{2}=5.

Which law? Read the clues

same base ×add the exponents
same base ÷subtract the exponents
( × )n or ( ÷ )ngive the exponent to every factor inside
( power )nmultiply the exponents
Example 5

Simplify with a law, then evaluate

Use the exponent laws to simplify, then evaluate.

(a) 4^{3}\cdot4^{2}
Product Law: add the exponents.
4^{3+2}=4^{5}=\mathbf{1024}
(b) \dfrac{(-3)^{6}}{(-3)^{2}}
Quotient Law (the base is −3, fence included):
(-3)^{6-2}=(-3)^{4}=\mathbf{81}
(c) \left(7^{2}\right)^{2}
Power of a Power Law: multiply the exponents.
7^{2\times2}=7^{4}=\mathbf{2401}
Think Jordan wrote 3^{2}\times3^{4}=9^{6}. What went wrong?
Jordan multiplied the bases too. The base stays 3: 3^{2}\times3^{4}=\mathbf{3^{6}}=729, while 9^{6}=531\,441. Only the exponents change.
Check a law with numbers. If the law is right, both sides give the same value: 4^3×4^2ENTER and 4^5ENTER both give 1024. Keep the brackets for a negative base.

✓

Key ideas

In 9m^{4}: coefficient 9, base m, exponent 4. The power is m^{4}=m\times m\times m\times m.
Brackets are a fence: the exponent repeats only what is inside. -7^{2}=-49 but (-7)^{2}=49; 5m^{3}\neq(5m)^{3}=125m^{3}.
Zero exponent: a^{0}=1 for a\neq0, from the divide-by-the-base pattern. But -a^{0}=-1.
Same base: × adds exponents, ÷ subtracts. Power of a power multiplies. A power of a product or quotient goes to every factor.
