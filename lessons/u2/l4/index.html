Scientific Notation Application / Extension

The Sun is about 150 000 000 000 m away, and a red blood cell is 0.000008 m across. Numbers like these are hard to read and easy to get wrong. Scientific notation writes them as a\times10^{n}, and then the exponent laws make multiplying and dividing them quick.

I can…
  • move between standard notation, product form and scientific notation
  • rewrite a number whose coefficient is out of range (renormalize it)
  • multiply and divide numbers in scientific notation with the product and quotient laws
  • solve problems with very large and very small numbers, and check them with the EE key
+
Application / Extension. Scientific notation is not a Math 10C outcome on its own: converting numbers is review from Grade 9 and Science 10. It appears here as a context for using the exponent laws.

Scientific Notation (Recap)

A positive number is in scientific notation when it is written as a\times10^{n}, where the coefficient a satisfies 1\le a\lt10 and n is an integer. Every positive number has exactly one scientific-notation form.

Product form shows where the power of ten comes from:

A big number: multiply by tens
52\,000=5.2\times10\times10\times10\times10=5.2\times10^{4}
A small number: divide by tens
0.00047=\dfrac{4.7}{10\times10\times10\times10}=4.7\times10^{-4}

To change a\times10^{n} to standard notation, move the decimal point of a by n places: to the right if n is positive, to the left if n is negative. ○ = where the point starts   ● = where it ends

Which sign does the exponent get?

Number ≥ 10
n is positive
84\,200=8.42\times10^{4}
Number from 1 to 10
n=0
9.1=9.1\times10^{0}
Number less than 1
n is negative
0.00072=7.2\times10^{-4}
!
A negative exponent does not make a negative number. 7.2\times10^{-4}=0.00072 is small, but still positive. The sign of n only says which way the point moves.
The EE key means “× 10 to the”. It is the 2nd function of x⁻¹, so press 2nd first. To type 4.1\times10^{-3}: 4.12ndEE(−)3ENTER. The screen shows 4.1E-3 and the answer 0.0041. Use (−), not −, for a negative exponent.

How big? How small? A powers-of-ten scale

On this scale every tick is 10 times the one to its left. The exponent tells you where a measurement sits: positive exponents are bigger than 1 m, negative exponents are smaller.

When the coefficient is out of range

Renormalize: if the coefficient is not between 1 and 10, move its decimal point until it is, and change the exponent by the same number of places the other way, so the value doesn’t change.
34.5\times10^{4}point left 1
exponent up 13.45\times10^{5}
0.35\times10^{-6}point right 1
exponent down 13.5\times10^{-7}
!
Coefficient smaller ⇒ exponent bigger (and the other way round). A common slip: 34.5\times10^{4}=3.45\times10^{3} ✗. That changes the value from 345 000 to 3 450.
Example 2

A coefficient that is out of range

Each number is not in scientific notation. Explain why, and rewrite it correctly.

(a) 0.35\times10^{-6}
The coefficient 0.35 is less than 1. Point 1 place right (0.35=3.5\times10^{-1}), so the exponent goes down 1:
0.35\times10^{-6}=3.5\times10^{-1}\times10^{-6}=\mathbf{3.5\times10^{-7}}
(b) 34.5\times10^{4}
The coefficient 34.5 is 10 or more. Point 1 place left (34.5=3.45\times10^{1}), so the exponent goes up 1:
34.5\times10^{4}=3.45\times10^{1}\times10^{4}=\mathbf{3.45\times10^{5}}

Multiplying and Dividing with the Exponent Laws

The exponent laws do this work by hand. Deal with the coefficients and the powers of 10 separately, then renormalize if the new coefficient is not between 1 and 10.

Multiply: multiply the coefficients, add the exponents (Product Law).
(a\times10^{m})(b\times10^{n})=(a\times b)\times10^{m+n}
Divide: divide the coefficients, subtract the exponents (Quotient Law).
(a\times10^{m})\div(b\times10^{n})=(a\div b)\times10^{m-n}
!
Coefficients multiply; only exponents add. (2.5\times10^{5})(3\times10^{4}) is not 5.5\times10^{9} and not 7.5\times10^{20}.
!
Watch the signs. -3-5=-8 and 4-(-2)=6. Then renormalize the right way: 20\times10^{-9}=2\times10^{-8}.
Example 3

Products and quotients, with renormalizing

Evaluate each expression and write the result in scientific notation.

(a) (2.5\times10^{5})(3\times10^{4})
2.5\times3=7.5;\ \ 5+4=9
\mathbf{7.5\times10^{9}} (already in range)
(b) (6.4\times10^{8})\div(1.6\times10^{3})
6.4\div1.6=4;\ \ 8-3=5
\mathbf{4\times10^{5}}
(c) (4\times10^{-3})(5\times10^{-6})
4\times5=20;\ \ -3+(-6)=-9
20\times10^{-9}=2\times10^{1}\times10^{-9}=\mathbf{2\times10^{-8}}
(d) (1.2\times10^{-3})\div(4\times10^{5})
1.2\div4=0.3;\ \ -3-5=-8
0.3\times10^{-8}=3\times10^{-1}\times10^{-8}=\mathbf{3\times10^{-9}}
Think How could you tell, before calculating, that the answers to (c) and (d) are both less than 1?
In (c) both numbers are less than 1, and multiplying two numbers less than 1 gives something even smaller. In (d) a tiny number is divided by a huge one. Both answers get negative exponents.
Check (c) with EE. 42ndEE(−)3×52ndEE(−)6ENTER. The answer shows as 2 ×10−8: read it as 2\times10^{-8}.
!
Typing “× 10 ^” instead of EE? Then each number needs its own brackets. 6.4×10^8÷1.6×10^3 works left to right, so it multiplies by 10^{3} instead of dividing: you get 4\times10^{11}, not 4\times10^{5}. EE keeps each number in one piece.

Problems with huge distances: \text{time}=\dfrac{\text{distance}}{\text{speed}}

Light travels about 3\times10^{8} m every second: that’s 300 000 km, about 7½ times around the Earth. Even so, space is so big that light takes a while to cross it.

Very big answers. How far does light go in a year (about 3.15\times10^{7} s)? 32ndEE8×3.152ndEE7ENTER. The calculator can’t fit all the digits, so it shows 9.45 ×1015. That means 9.45\times10^{15} m (a light-year). Never copy it down as 9.45^{15}.
Example 4

How long does sunlight take to reach us?

The Sun is about 1.5\times10^{11} m from Earth, and light travels about 3\times10^{8} m/s.

(a) About how many seconds does sunlight take to reach Earth?
t=\dfrac{1.5\times10^{11}}{3\times10^{8}}=0.5\times10^{11-8}=0.5\times10^{3}
=5\times10^{-1}\times10^{3}=\mathbf{5\times10^{2}} s (500 s)
(b) About how many minutes is that?
500\div60\approx\mathbf{8.3} minutes
The sunlight you see left the Sun about 8 minutes ago.
Check it. 1.52ndEE11÷32ndEE8ENTER gives 500. Keep going ÷60 for minutes: 8.333…

Key ideas

Scientific notation is a\times10^{n} with 1\le a\lt10. Product form shows the tens: 52\,000=5.2\times10\times10\times10\times10.
Count the hops. Big numbers get a positive exponent, numbers less than 1 a negative one: 0.00047=4.7\times10^{-4}.
Renormalize: point left ⇒ exponent up; point right ⇒ exponent down. 34.5\times10^{4}=3.45\times10^{5}
Multiply: coefficients × , exponents +. Divide: coefficients ÷ , exponents −. Then renormalize. Check with EE.
