Warm-up

Power Rankings

Eight powers enter. In every match, the bigger number moves on. No calculator — they’re far too big!

1
Which is bigger: 2^3 or 3^2?
3^2=9 beats 2^3=8.
A bigger exponent doesn’t always win!
2
Write 8^{33} and 16^{24} as powers of 2. Who wins?
8^{33}=(2^3)^{33}=2^{99}
16^{24}=(2^4)^{24}=2^{96}
Same base: 8^{33} wins.
3
Write 3^{60} and 2^{90} as “something^{30}”. Who wins?
3^{60}=(3^2)^{30}=9^{30}
2^{90}=(2^3)^{30}=8^{30}
Same exponent: 3^{60} wins.
!
The big one. Predict the result of the whole tournament. Guess first!
A 10^{30} wins
B 2^{100} wins
C 3^{60} wins
D the final is a tie!
D — a tie. 4^{50}=(2^2)^{50}=2^{100}: the finalists are the same number, about 1.27\times10^{30}. Base 10 feels huge, but 10^{30}=1000^{10} loses to 2^{100}=1024^{10} in round 1.
Challenge your neighbour: invent a new player that would tie with 2^{100}. Tap for ideas:
Careful: what is 2^{100}+2^{100}? Not 2^{200} or 4^{100}… Two of the same thing: 2\times2^{100}=2^{1}\times2^{100}=2^{101}. Exponent laws need × or ÷, never +.

Laws at a Glance

Every law comes from repeated multiplication. Tap a law to see why it works.

!
Only × and ÷. The Product and Quotient Laws combine exponents only when bases are multiplied or divided. b^6+b^2 is not a single power.
!
Rational exponents. Write \tfrac{m}{n} in lowest terms. If n is even, the base can’t be negative: \sqrt{-4} isn’t a real number.

Vocabulary: Base, Exponent, and Coefficient

Base — the factor being repeated.
In a^n, the base is a.
Exponent — how many times the base is used as a factor.
In a^n, the exponent is n.
Coefficient — the number multiplying the power.
-\dfrac{4y^6}{9}=-\dfrac{4}{9}y^6: it’s -\tfrac49.
Negative signs need care. In -a^0 the exponent applies only to a, so -a^0=-(a^0)=-1. In (-a)^0=1 the brackets make the whole -a the base.
The calculator knows the fence. (−)3x² gives −9: it squares first, then negates.
Brackets make -3 the base: ((−)3)x² gives 9.
Example 1

Base, exponent, coefficient, and the -a^0 trap

(a) State the base and exponent in the power (-3)^5.
The brackets make the whole -3 the repeated factor.
Base = -3, exponent = 5
(b) State the coefficient in -\dfrac{4y^6}{9}.
-\dfrac{4y^6}{9}=-\dfrac{4}{9}\,y^6
Coefficient = -\dfrac{4}{9}
(c) Evaluate -a^0 and (-a)^0, assuming a\neq0.
-a^0=-(a^0)=-(1)=\boldsymbol{-1}
Brackets make -a the base: (-a)^0=\boldsymbol{1}

Expanding Powers as Repeated Multiplication

Expanding a power as repeated multiplication is a good check on a simplification. Brackets act like a fence: a coefficient sitting outside a bracket is not raised to the power; only what is inside is.
Coefficient outside the fence
5m^3=5\times m\times m\times m
the 5 appears once
Coefficient inside the fence
(5m)^3=5m\times5m\times5m=125m^3
the 5 is part of the base, so it repeats
Example 2

True or false: two expansions

Consider the following two statements. Determine whether each is true or false, and explain your reasoning.

Statement 1: 8p^3q^5=8\times p\times p\times p\times q\times q\times q\times q\times q
Statement 2: 4(mn)^3=4\times4\times4\times m\times m\times m\times n\times n\times n
Statement 1
True. p appears three times and q five times, with the coefficient 8 written once — it isn’t part of the repeated multiplication.
Statement 2
False. Only m and n are inside the brackets, so only they are cubed: 4(mn)^3=4\times m\times m\times m\times n\times n\times n. Writing 4\times4\times4 changes 4m^3n^3 into 64m^3n^3.
Think Change the left side of Statement 2 so that the statement becomes true.
Move the 4 inside the fence: (4mn)^3=4\times4\times4\times m\times m\times m\times n\times n\times n=64m^3n^3.

Product and Quotient Laws with Coefficients

With a coefficient attached, multiply or divide the coefficients separately from adding or subtracting the exponents.
!
Two different jobs. Coefficients are ordinary numbers: 6\times3=18. Exponents follow the law: 4+5=9. Writing 9b^9 (adding the coefficients) or 18b^{20} (multiplying the exponents) mixes the jobs up.
Example 4

The Product Law with coefficients

Simplify 6b^4\times3b^5.
6b^4\times3b^5=(6\times3)\,b^{4+5}=\boldsymbol{18b^9}
Example 5

The Quotient Law, including a negative exponent

Simplify each quotient.

(a) \dfrac{8x^{14}}{4x^6}
\dfrac{8x^{14}}{4x^6}=\left(\dfrac{8}{4}\right)x^{14-6}=\boldsymbol{2x^8}
(b) \dfrac{9y^2}{3y^{-5}}
Subtracting -5 adds 5:
\dfrac{9y^2}{3y^{-5}}=\left(\dfrac{9}{3}\right)y^{2-(-5)}=\boldsymbol{3y^7}
6

Negative Exponents

A negative exponent means take the reciprocal of the base raised to the matching positive exponent: a^{-n}=\dfrac{1}{a^n}. It does not make the value negative, and it does not affect a coefficient sitting outside the power.
See it’s positive. 2^(−)3ENTER gives 0.125, and 2ndF◂▸D turns it into \tfrac18 — not −8.
Example 6

Rewriting with positive exponents

Rewrite each expression using only positive exponents.

(a) y^{-4}
A negative exponent means “take the reciprocal”:
y^{-4}=\boldsymbol{\dfrac{1}{y^4}}
(b) 7x^{-3}
The -3 applies only to x, not to the 7:
7x^{-3}=7\times\dfrac{1}{x^3}=\boldsymbol{\dfrac{7}{x^3}}
Think Is 7x^{-3} the same as (7x)^{-3}?
No. In (7x)^{-3} the 7 is inside the fence: (7x)^{-3}=\dfrac{1}{(7x)^3}=\dfrac{1}{343x^3}, while 7x^{-3}=\dfrac{7}{x^3}.

Rational Exponents

A rational (fractional) exponent combines a root and a power: a^{m/n}=\sqrt[n]{a^m}=\left(\sqrt[n]{a}\right)^m. The denominator n is the index of the root; the numerator m is the power. A negative rational exponent still means “reciprocal”.
Brackets around the exponent! 9^(3÷2) gives 27. Without them, the calculator does 9^3\div2.
Check Example 7(b) in one line. The calculator won't take (-27)^{-2/3} directly (negative base), so do the cube root first and square it: \left((-27)^{-1/3}\right)^2.
Example 7

Radical form, and evaluating a rational exponent

(a) Express x^{2/7} in radical form.
Denominator → index, numerator → power:
x^{2/7}=\boldsymbol{\sqrt[7]{x^2}}
(b) If p=9 and q=-27, evaluate p^{3/2}-q^{-2/3} to the nearest hundredth.
p^{3/2}=(\sqrt{9})^3=3^3=27;  q^{2/3}=(\sqrt[3]{-27})^2=(-3)^2=9, so q^{-2/3}=\tfrac19
27-\tfrac19=26.888\ldots\approx\boldsymbol{26.89}

A rational exponent on a whole bracket

The Power of a Product and Power of a Power laws work with fractional exponents too: raise every factor inside the bracket, and multiply exponents.

!
The number is a power, too. 125^{-2/3} is not 125\times\left(-\tfrac23\right). Root, power, reciprocal: 125^{-2/3}=\dfrac{1}{(\sqrt[3]{125})^2}=\dfrac{1}{25}.
Check the number part of Example 8: 16^(3÷4) → 8.
Example 8

Simplifying with a rational exponent

Simplify \left(16x^8y^{-4}\right)^{3/4}. Write the answer with positive exponents.
Raise every factor to \tfrac34: 16^{3/4}=(\sqrt[4]{16})^3=2^3=8,  x^{8\cdot\frac34}=x^6,  y^{-4\cdot\frac34}=y^{-3}
8x^6y^{-3}=\boldsymbol{\dfrac{8x^6}{y^3}}

Scientific Notation

A number in scientific notation has the form a\times10^n with 1\le a<10. To multiply, multiply the coefficients and add the exponents of 10 — then check the coefficient is still between 1 and 10, adjusting the exponent if it isn’t.
The EE key means “× 10 to the”: 5.42ndEE(−)2 is 5.4\times10^{-2}. Example 9 gives 162 000.
!
Don’t type ×10^ with EE. EE already includes the “× 10”. And use (−), not −, for a negative exponent.
Example 9

Multiplying in scientific notation

Evaluate (5.4\times10^{-2})\times(3.0\times10^6), and write the answer in proper scientific notation.
(5.4\times3.0)\times10^{-2+6}=16.2\times10^4;  16.2 isn’t between 1 and 10:
16.2\times10^4=1.62\times10^1\times10^4=\boldsymbol{1.62\times10^5}

Dividing: how many thin sheets?

Check Example 10. 1.6÷6.42ndEE(−)5ENTER → 25 000. EE keeps the whole 6.4\times10^{-5} together as one number.
Example 10

A stack of thin sheets

A sheet of aluminum foil is 6.4\times10^{-5} m thick. Approximately how many sheets are needed to build a stack 1.6 m high?
Total height ÷ one sheet: \dfrac{1.6}{6.4\times10^{-5}}=\dfrac{1.6}{0.000\,064}=25\,000
About 25 000 sheets
✓

Key ideas

Same base: multiply → add exponents, divide → subtract, power of a power → multiply. Never combine across + or −.
The fence decides the base. -a^0=-1 but (-a)^0=1; a coefficient outside a bracket is not raised to the power.
Negative exponent = reciprocal, never a negative value. It moves only its own base across the fraction bar.
a^{m/n}: denominator = root, numerator = power. Take the root first. In scientific notation, multiply coefficients, add exponents, then renormalize.
