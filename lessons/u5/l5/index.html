Graphing Calculator Techniques for Relations

A graphing calculator draws a relation in a second, but it only shows the part of the plane inside its window, and its answers are only as good as the buttons you press. Today: the TI-83/84 tools for reading a graph (window, table, trace, intersect, zero, value, maximum and minimum), and the habit that goes with every one of them: check the answer algebraically.

I can…
  • set up the calculator, enter a relation in Y=, and choose a window that shows the features I need, written as x:[X_{\min},X_{\max},X_{scl}], y:[Y_{\min},Y_{\max},Y_{scl}]
  • find an output from an input with trace, value or the table, and an input from an output with intersect
  • find x-intercepts with zero and the y-intercept at x=0, and turn a decimal answer into an exact fraction
  • find a maximum or minimum point, and check every calculator answer by substituting into the equation
RF1 Interpret and explain the relationships among data, graphs and situations.
RF5 Determine the characteristics of the graphs of linear relations, including the intercepts, slope, domain and range.
Warm-up

Zoom and Enhance

Three graphing-calculator screens. Can you trust what you see?

1
Screen A shows y=x^2. What are the coordinates of its lowest point?
(0,\ 0): the vertex sits at the origin.
2
Screen B is the same graph, zoomed in near (1,\ 1). What shape do you see?
A straight line. Over 0.95\le x\le 1.05 the curve bends away from a straight line by at most 0.0025, about 1% of the screen’s height.
3
Name something real that looks flat up close but is really curved.
The Earth (a lake, the prairie horizon), a big ball, a highway curve seen from a car… Any smooth curve looks straight if you zoom in far enough.
!
The big one. On Screen C the graphs cross twice. How many times do y=2^x and y=x^2 really cross? Guess first.
A 1
B 2
C 3
D infinitely many
Screen C:
C: three times. At x\approx-0.77 (where 2^x\approx x^2\approx0.59) and at x=2 (2^2=4=2^2): both visible on Screen C. The third is hidden: x=4, where 2^4=16=4^2, and y=16 is above Y_{\max}=10. After x=4, 2^x pulls ahead forever (at x=10: 1024 vs 100).
B trusts the screen. D guesses the graphs keep weaving. A notices only the crossing near the origin.
Today’s lesson: the default window rarely shows every feature worth seeing, so learn to choose the window.
+
Early finishers. Find a window where y=2^x and y=x^2 look like they never cross.
For example x:[5,\ 10], y:[0,\ 1100]: there 2^x is above x^2 the whole way across.

1

Getting the Calculator Ready

Today’s steps are for the TI-83/84 graphing calculator. The keys look like Y=, 2nd TRACE; the screens in the animations look like a TI-84. The TI-30XIIS built into this page can’t graph, so we use it only for the algebraic checks.

Confirm three settings before graphing anything.

iMode. Press MODE and make sure Func (function) is highlighted: every relation is entered solved for y.
iiWindow. Press WINDOW. This screen controls what part of the Cartesian plane is visible.
iiiFormat. Press 2nd ZOOM and confirm RectGC, CoordOn, AxesOn and LabelOff are highlighted. Then press Y= and check that Plot1, Plot2, Plot3 are not highlighted (scroll to one and press ENTER to turn it off), or the calculator plots leftover statistical data along with your relation.

The WINDOW screen

Xmin, Xmax
smallest and largest x-value shown
Ymin, Ymax
smallest and largest y-value shown
Xscl
spacing between x-axis tick marks
Yscl
spacing between y-axis tick marks
Xres
screen resolution: leave it set to 1
A window is abbreviated x:[X_{\min},\ X_{\max},\ X_{scl}], y:[Y_{\min},\ Y_{\max},\ Y_{scl}]. The standard window is x:[-10,\ 10,\ 1], y:[-10,\ 10,\ 1].
2

Entering and Graphing a Relation

A relation must first be solved for y. Press Y= to open the equation editor, type the right-hand side into Y1 (or another empty row), and press GRAPH to draw the curve in the current window.

The running example for this lesson is y=0.75x-9. In the standard window x:[-10,\ 10,\ 1], y:[-10,\ 10,\ 1] (restored any time with ZOOM 6) the line is drawn, but its x-intercept is cut off, since 0.75x-9=0 gives x=12. The default window rarely shows every feature worth seeing.
3

Adjusting the Window

1Type in a custom window. Press WINDOW, edit the values with the cursor keys, press GRAPH. For y=0.75x-9, the window x:[-5,\ 20,\ 5], y:[-15,\ 10,\ 5] shows both intercepts comfortably.
2Zoom Out. Press ZOOM 3 (or select ZoomOut and press GRAPH) one or more times until the feature appears. Fast, but rarely ideal.
3ZoomFit. Press ZOOM 0 to let the calculator choose y-bounds that fit the curve over the current x-bounds.
Think ZoomFit on y=0.75x-9 (starting from the standard window) still doesn’t show the x-intercept. Why not?
ZoomFit only changes Y_{\min} and Y_{\max}. The x-values still run from -10 to 10, and the intercept is at x=12. (The new window, -16.5\le y\le-1.5, doesn’t even include the x-axis.)
4

Reading a Table of Values

Press 2nd WINDOW to open TBLSET and set TblStart (the first input shown) and ΔTbl (the spacing between inputs). Press 2nd GRAPH to view the table, scrolling with the cursor keys. With TblStart =-3 and ΔTbl =1, the table for y=0.75x-9 reads:

One far-away input. Switch Indpnt from Auto to Ask in TBLSET: a chosen x-value can then be typed straight into the table, and ENTER shows just that output. For y=0.75x-9, typing x=500 returns y=366.
Table and graph together. Press MODE, scroll to G-T, press ENTER, then GRAPH. Pressing TRACE and the left/right cursor keys moves along the curve while the table scrolls to match.
Check x=500 on the TI-30XIIS. .75×500−9ENTER gives 366 ✓

5

An Output From Trace, an Input From Intersect

Trace: given x. Press TRACE, type the input value, press ENTER. The matching output appears at the bottom of the screen.
Intersect: given y. Enter the relation in Y1 and the target output (a constant) in Y2. Press GRAPH, open CALC with 2nd TRACE and choose 5:intersect. The calculator prompts First curve?, Second curve? and Guess?: scroll near the crossing point each time and press ENTER. The intersection point displayed gives the input that produces that output. For y=0.75x-9 with Y_2=-3 the intersection point is (8,\ -3), so x=8 when y=-3.

Stay inside the window

!
TRACE and 1:value only accept an x-value between Xmin and Xmax; outside the window the calculator shows ERR:INVALID. Either widen the window, or use the table in Ask mode (Section 4), which works for any input. Likewise, intersect can only find a crossing point that is visible in the window.
Example 1

Window, trace, intersect, and the algebraic check

Consider the relation y=4x-30.

(a) Graph the relation on a calculator, sketch it below, and record a suitable window.
x:[\underline{\hspace{1.3cm}},\ \underline{\hspace{1.3cm}},\ \underline{\hspace{1.3cm}}]y:[\underline{\hspace{1.3cm}},\ \underline{\hspace{1.3cm}},\ \underline{\hspace{1.3cm}}]
x-intercept: 4x-30=0\Rightarrow x=7.5. y-intercept: -30.
A suitable window: \boldsymbol{x:[-2,\ 11,\ 1]}, \boldsymbol{y:[-35,\ 15,\ 5]}
Windows may vary: both intercepts must be visible.
(b) Use the trace or table feature to determine y when x=4.2.
Trace (or the table) at x=4.2 gives \boldsymbol{y=-13.2}.
(c) Algebraically verify the answer in (b).
y=4(4.2)-30=16.8-30=-13.2 ✓
(d) Use the intersect feature, with Y_2 set to the given output, to determine x when y=-5.4.
With Y_2=-5.4, 5:intersect gives the crossing point (6.15,\ -5.4), so \boldsymbol{x=6.15}.
(e) Algebraically verify the answer in (d).
-5.4=4x-30\Rightarrow 4x=24.6\Rightarrow x=6.15 ✓
The algebraic checks on the TI-30XIIS. (c) 4×4.2−30ENTER (e) ((−)5.4+30)÷4ENTER
6

Finding x-intercepts

Zero feature. With the relation in Y1, open CALC (2nd TRACE) and choose 2:zero. The calculator asks for a Left Bound? (scroll to a point left of the crossing, ENTER), a Right Bound? (scroll to a point right of the crossing, ENTER) and a Guess? (ENTER). The x-value shown is the x-intercept.
Intersect feature. Alternatively set Y_2=0 and use the intersect procedure of Section 5: the intersection with the x-axis is exactly the x-intercept.
7

Finding y-intercepts

The y-intercept is simply the output when x=0, so any of these works.

TTrace: press TRACE, type 0, press ENTER.
VValue: open CALC (2nd TRACE), choose 1:value, type 0, press ENTER.
≡Table: set TblStart =0 (or scroll the table to x=0) and read off y.

Trace and value need x=0 to be inside the window (see Section 5).

Example 2

Intercepts with the zero and value features

Use the features of a graphing calculator to determine the x-intercept(s) and the y-intercept of each relation.

(a) y=\dfrac{7x+21}{3}
2:zero: 7x+21=0\Rightarrow x=-3
1:value at x=0: y=\frac{21}{3}=7
x-int \boldsymbol{-3}, y-int \boldsymbol{7}
(b) y=-x^2+2x+8
2:zero twice: -x^2+2x+8=0
\Rightarrow x^2-2x-8=0
\Rightarrow (x-4)(x+2)=0
so \boldsymbol{x=4} and \boldsymbol{x=-2}.
1:value at x=0: \boldsymbol{y=8}
8

Converting a Decimal Intercept to a Fraction

When an intercept is not a whole number the calculator displays a decimal, which can be converted to an exact fraction. Find the intercept by any method above, then press 2nd QUIT (QUIT is above MODE) to return to the home screen.

For an x-intercept: press X,T,θ,n MATH ENTER ENTER. This recalls the most recent x-value the cursor sat on and converts it to a fraction.
For a y-intercept: press ALPHA 1 to type the letter Y, then MATH ENTER ENTER.

Multiplying the resulting fraction back out, or converting it back to a decimal, checks the conversion.

Example 3

Exact intercepts as fractions

Consider the relation y=\dfrac{3x-13}{8}.

(a) Determine the x-intercept, expressed as an exact fraction.
2:zero: 3x-13=0\Rightarrow x=\frac{13}{3}, shown as 4.333333333.
2nd QUIT, then X,T,θ,n MATH ENTER ENTER returns \boldsymbol{\frac{13}{3}}.
(b) Determine the y-intercept, expressed as an exact fraction.
1:value at x=0: y=\frac{-13}{8}=-1.625.
ALPHA 1 MATH ENTER ENTER returns \boldsymbol{-\frac{13}{8}}.
Check the fractions on the TI-30XIIS: back to decimals. 13÷3ENTER and (−)13÷8ENTER
9

Finding a Maximum or Minimum Value

A parabola’s vertex can also be located with the calculator. Open CALC (2nd TRACE) and choose 4:maximum for a downward-opening parabola or 3:minimum for an upward-opening one. As with the zero feature, the calculator asks for a Left Bound?, a Right Bound? and a Guess? on either side of the vertex.

!
The coordinates displayed may be slightly off because of the calculator’s internal approximation method. Round to a sensible exact value, check it by substituting into the equation, and use MATH ▸Frac on the coordinates to recover exact fractions if needed.
Example 4

The maximum feature, then the exact vertex

Consider the relation y=-x^2+7x+12.

(a) Use the maximum feature to estimate the coordinates of the maximum point.
4:maximum, answering Left Bound? and Right Bound? on either side of the turning point and Guess? near it, locates the maximum at \boldsymbol{x\approx3.5}, \boldsymbol{y\approx24.25}.
(b) State the exact coordinates of the maximum point as fractions.
The screen shows something like X=3.4999986, Y=24.25: round to x=3.5.
Check: -(3.5)^2+7(3.5)+12=-12.25+24.5+12=24.25 ✓, and nearby inputs give less (x=3.4 and x=3.6 both give 24.24).
MATH 1:▸Frac: maximum point \boldsymbol{\left(\frac{7}{2},\ \frac{97}{4}\right)}.
Substitute x=3.5 on the TI-30XIIS. 0−3.5x²+7×3.5+12ENTER gives 24.25, and 97÷4ENTER gives 24.25 too ✓
✓

Key ideas

Window: x:[X_{\min},X_{\max},X_{scl}], y:[Y_{\min},Y_{\max},Y_{scl}]. ZOOM 6 is standard, which rarely shows everything: type a custom window (Zoom Out is fast but rough; ZoomFit fits y only).
Given x, find y: TRACE or 1:value (only for X_{\min}\le x\le X_{\max}, else ERR:INVALID), or the table (Ask mode works for any x).
Given y, find x: Y_2= the output, then 5:intersect. Intercepts: 2:zero (or Y_2=0) for x; the output at x=0 for y.
Exact answers: X▸Frac, Y▸Frac. 4:maximum / 3:minimum may be slightly off: round, then check every answer by substituting into the equation.
