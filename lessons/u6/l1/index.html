Measuring the Length of a Line Segment

How long is a piece of a line? On a grid, a horizontal or vertical segment can be measured by counting squares or by subtracting coordinates. A slanted segment hides a right triangle underneath it, and the Pythagorean theorem does the rest.

I can…
  • name a line segment by its endpoints, write it as \overline{AB}, and write its length as AB
  • find the length of a horizontal segment by counting squares and by |x_2-x_1|, and explain why the absolute value is there
  • find the length of a vertical segment by counting squares and by |y_2-y_1|
  • find the length of a diagonal segment from the right triangle under it, c=\sqrt{a^2+b^2}, as an exact radical in simplest form and as a decimal

Warm-up

The Spider and the Fly

The spider wants the fly. It can only crawl: on walls, floor and ceiling. The room is 30 m long, 12 m wide and 12 m high, and each bug is in the middle of its end wall.

1
The obvious route: up, across the ceiling, down. How long is it?
1+30+11=\boldsymbol{42} m: a vertical piece, a horizontal piece and a vertical piece, each just counted or subtracted.
2
On the flat wall, how long is the straight crawl from corner to corner?
5 m. It is the hypotenuse of a right triangle with legs 4 and 3: \sqrt{4^2+3^2}=\sqrt{25}=5. It saves 2 m over the edges.
3
Open the room out flat, like a box. What does the shortest crawl look like?
A straight line on the flat net: the hypotenuse of a right triangle whose legs are a horizontal and a vertical segment.
!
The big one. Can the spider beat the obvious route? How long is the shortest possible crawl to the fly? Guess first.
A 42 m
B 40 m
C about 32 m
B: 40 m. Crawl up onto the ceiling, slant down across a side wall, then across the floor and onto the far end wall: 5 of the 6 faces. Opened out flat, that path is one straight segment with run 1+30+1=32 and rise 6+12+6=24, so it is \sqrt{32^2+24^2}=\sqrt{1600}=40 m.
A is the gut feeling: crossing more walls “must” be longer. C is the straight line through the air, \sqrt{30^2+10^2}\approx31.6 m: only if the spider could fly. (Henry Dudeney’s puzzle, about 1903.)
+
Early finishers. Try a 4-face route: up onto the ceiling, slant across it and down a side wall, then onto the far end wall. On that net the legs are 37 and 17. Does it still beat 42 m?
\sqrt{37^2+17^2}=\sqrt{1658}\approx40.7 m. Yes, it beats 42 m, but not 40 m.

Poll the room: A, B or C? Then test it with a shoebox and a piece of string.

1

What Is a Line Segment?

A line segment is the piece of a line lying between two specific points on that line, called its endpoints. If the endpoints are named A and B, the segment is called segment AB, and it may also be written with a bar on top as \overline{AB}. Unlike a full line, a segment has a definite length, since it stops at each endpoint.
2

Length of a Horizontal Line Segment

A line segment is horizontal when both of its endpoints share the same y-coordinate. Because the segment never rises or falls, its length can be found simply by counting grid squares, or, without a grid, by subtracting the x-coordinates of the endpoints and taking the positive value of the result, since a length can never be negative.

Absolute value. Bars around a number mean “the positive size of”: |a| is the size of a with its sign dropped, so |-9|=9 and |9|=9.
Horizontal segment:
length =|x_2-x_1|
Example 1

Horizontal segments on a grid

Segments AB, CD and EF are graphed below.

(a) Find the length of each segment by counting grid squares.
AB=6 units, CD=7 units, EF=8 units.
(b) State the coordinates of the endpoints of each segment.
A(-5,\ 5), B(1,\ 5); C(-4,\ 2), D(3,\ 2); E(-5,\ -4), F(3,\ -4).
(c) For each segment, subtract the x-coordinate of the left endpoint from the x-coordinate of the right endpoint.
x_B-x_A=1-(-5)=6; x_D-x_C=3-(-4)=7; x_F-x_E=3-(-5)=8. Each difference matches the length counted in (a).
(d) Based on parts (a)–(c), describe a rule for finding the length of a horizontal line segment directly from the coordinates of its endpoints.
The length is the positive difference of the x-coordinates of the endpoints: length =|x_2-x_1|. The y-coordinates play no part, since they are equal. The absolute value is needed because subtracting in the other order gives the same size with the opposite sign, and a length can never be negative.

Why the absolute value?

Subtract the x-coordinates in one order and you get a positive number; subtract them in the other order and you get its negative. The sign only tells you which way you walked. A length is just how far.

Example 2

Horizontal length from the coordinates

(a) Line segment PQ has endpoints P(5,\ -2) and Q(-4,\ -2). Determine the length of PQ.
Both endpoints have y=-2, so PQ is horizontal.
PQ=|x_Q-x_P|=|-4-5|=|-9|=\boldsymbol{9} units
(b) Determine the length of the line segment from M(a-3,\ b) to N(a+5,\ b).
Both endpoints have y-coordinate b, so MN is horizontal.
MN=|x_N-x_M|=|(a+5)-(a-3)|=|8|=\boldsymbol{8} units
The length does not depend on a or b.
Think In (a), does it matter which endpoint you call x_1 and which you call x_2?
No. |5-(-4)|=|9|=9 and |-4-5|=|-9|=9. The order only changes the sign, and the absolute value drops the sign.

Letters for coordinates

In Example 2(b) the endpoints are written with letters, so the segment could be anywhere on the grid. Try a few values of a and b: the segment slides around, but its length never changes.

3

Length of a Vertical Line Segment

A line segment is vertical when both endpoints share the same x-coordinate. The same idea applies, just turned 90^\circ: the length is found by subtracting the y-coordinates of the endpoints and taking the positive value of the result.

Vertical segment: length =|y_2-y_1|
Example 3

Vertical segments on a grid

Segments GH, IJ and KL are graphed below.

(a) Find the length of each segment by counting grid squares.
GH=8 units, IJ=6 units, KL=7 units.
(b) State the coordinates of the endpoints of each segment.
G(-4,\ 5), H(-4,\ -3); I(1,\ 4), J(1,\ -2); K(4,\ 3), L(4,\ -4).
(c) For each segment, subtract the y-coordinate of the lower endpoint from the y-coordinate of the upper endpoint.
y_G-y_H=5-(-3)=8; y_I-y_J=4-(-2)=6; y_K-y_L=3-(-4)=7. Each matches the count in (a). The other order gives -8, -6, -7: the same sizes with the opposite sign.
(d) Based on parts (a)–(c), describe a rule for finding the length of a vertical line segment directly from the coordinates of its endpoints.
The length is the positive difference of the y-coordinates of the endpoints: length =|y_2-y_1|. The x-coordinates play no part, since they are equal.
Example 4

Vertical length from the coordinates

(a) Line segment UV has endpoints U(3,\ -2) and V(3,\ -11). Determine the length of UV.
Both endpoints have x=3, so UV is vertical.
UV=|y_V-y_U|=|-11-(-2)|
=|-11+2|=|-9|=\boldsymbol{9} units
(b) Determine the length of the line segment from S(a,\ b) to T(a,\ b-12).
Both endpoints have x-coordinate a, so ST is vertical.
ST=|y_T-y_S|=|(b-12)-b|=|-12|=\boldsymbol{12} units
!
Brackets around a negative. Subtracting a negative coordinate is where most mistakes happen: -11-(-2)=-11+2=-9, not -13. Write the brackets first, then simplify.
4

Diagonal Segments and the Pythagorean Theorem

Most line segments on a grid are neither horizontal nor vertical: they run diagonally. To find the length of a diagonal segment, build a right triangle underneath it (or on top of it: either corner works): a horizontal leg (the run), a vertical leg (the rise), and the diagonal segment itself as the hypotenuse.

Pythagorean Theorem. In a right triangle, the square of the hypotenuse c equals the sum of the squares of the two legs a and b:
a^2+b^2=c^2\qquad\Longrightarrow\qquad c=\sqrt{a^2+b^2}
On a grid, a diagonal segment’s run and rise become the legs a and b, and solving for c gives its length.

Every slanted segment hides a right triangle

Slide across from one endpoint (the run), then up or down to the other (the rise). Count both legs, build a square on each side, and let a^2+b^2=c^2 do the rest.

!
Don’t add the run and the rise. Walking the two legs is the long way round (like the spider’s 7 m along the edges of the flat wall). The slanted segment is the short cut, so it is always shorter than run + rise and longer than either leg.
Example 5

Diagonal lengths by the Pythagorean theorem

Determine the length of each segment shown on the grid. Give each answer as (i) an exact value in simplest radical form, and (ii) a decimal approximation to the nearest hundredth.

(a) Segment AB
A(-5,\ -2), B(-2,\ 7). Run =|-2-(-5)|=3; rise =|7-(-2)|=9.
AB^2=3^2+9^2=9+81=90
(i) AB=\sqrt{90}=\sqrt{9\times10}=\boldsymbol{3\sqrt{10}} units
(ii) AB\approx\boldsymbol{9.49} units
(b) Segment CD
C(3,\ 6), D(7,\ -4). Run =|7-3|=4; rise =|-4-6|=10.
CD^2=4^2+10^2=16+100=116
(i) CD=\sqrt{116}=\sqrt{4\times29}=\boldsymbol{2\sqrt{29}} units
(ii) CD\approx\boldsymbol{10.77} units
Think Before calculating: which is longer, AB or CD? How can you tell from the triangles?
CD. Its run (4) and its rise (10) are both longer than AB’s (3 and 9), so its whole triangle is bigger: 10.77>9.49.
Decimals of the radicals. 2ndx² is \sqrt{\phantom{x}}: √90ENTER gives 9.486832981 → 9.49. Check the simplified form gives the same decimal: 3×√10ENTER.

Exact answers: simplest radical form

The exact length is a square root. To simplify it, look for the biggest perfect square that divides the number under the root, and take its square root out front. If no perfect square (other than 1) divides it, the root is already in simplest form.

✓

Key ideas

Segment: the piece of a line between two endpoints. Segment \overline{AB} has a definite length, written AB. A length is never negative.
Horizontal (same y): count squares, or length =|x_2-x_1|. The bars drop the sign, so the order of subtraction doesn’t matter.
Vertical (same x): count squares, or length =|y_2-y_1|. The same idea, turned 90^\circ.
Diagonal: the hidden right triangle. Run and rise are the legs: c=\sqrt{a^2+b^2}. Exact: simplest radical (\sqrt{90}=3\sqrt{10}); decimal: round at the end.
