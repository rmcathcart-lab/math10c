Rise Over Run: The Slope of a Line Segment

Steepness as a single signed number: counted off a grid, read as a rate of change, and run backward from a given slope.

Warm-up

Ramp Rules

A common rule: a ramp may rise at most 1 cm for every 12 cm across.

1
Which ramp climbs the highest?
Ramp C (25 cm). But highest is not steepest!
2
Which ramp is the steepest? (Hint: compare \tfrac{\text{rise}}{\text{run}} for each.)
Ramp B. A:\ \tfrac{10}{120}=\tfrac{1}{12}, B:\ \tfrac{20}{180}=\tfrac{1}{9}, C:\ \tfrac{25}{400}=\tfrac{1}{16}. Since \tfrac19>\tfrac1{12}>\tfrac1{16} (about 0.11, 0.08, 0.06), B is steepest and C is gentlest.
3
Which ramps follow the 1-in-12 rule?
A (exactly \tfrac{1}{12}, right at the limit) and C (gentler). B is too steep: it would need a run of 20\times12=240 cm.
!
The big one. A café door is up one 60 cm step. To follow the rule, at least how far out from the step must the ramp start? Guess first.
A 72 cm
B 5 m
C 7.2 m
C: 7.2 m. Every 1 cm of rise needs 12 cm of run, so run =60\times12=720 cm =7.2 m.
A added 60+12 instead of multiplying. B divided 60\div12=5, then guessed the unit.
Most people badly underestimate how long a gentle ramp is. (The sloped surface itself is \sqrt{720^2+60^2}\approx722 cm: still about 7.2 m.)
+
Early finishers. A school has only 3.6 m of sidewalk in front of a step. What is the tallest step a ramp there can climb and still follow the rule?
360\div12=30 cm.

Find a ramp at school: pace out its run and estimate its rise. Does it pass?

1

What Is Slope?

The slope of a line segment is a measure of its steepness. It compares the rise (the change in vertical position between the two endpoints) to the run (the change in horizontal position between the two endpoints).
\text{slope}=\dfrac{\color{#7b4fc4}\text{rise}}{\color{#c27a0e}\text{run}}
Rise
count UP: positive
count DOWN: negative
Run
count RIGHT: positive
count LEFT: negative

On a grid the rise and run are counted square by square, moving from one endpoint to the other. Counting from left to right keeps the run positive, so the sign of the slope then comes from the rise. A slope that is not a whole number is left as a fraction in lowest terms. (In Lesson 3 this counting is turned into a formula that works straight from the coordinates.)

!
Count squares, from one endpoint to the other. The run is how many squares across, the rise how many squares up or down. Rise goes on top: \tfrac{\text{rise}}{\text{run}}, not \tfrac{\text{run}}{\text{rise}}.

2

Slope Is the Same Everywhere Along a Line

The slope calculated from any two points on a straight line is always the same. That common value is called the slope of the line.

A slope triangle can slide anywhere along a line, and it can grow or shrink: every one is a scaled copy of the others, so \tfrac{\text{rise}}{\text{run}} never changes.

Example 2

The same line, four different pairs of points

Points A(-5,-4), B(-3,-3), C(1,-1) and D(5,1) all lie on the same line. Calculate the slope determined by each pair of points, and state what you notice.

(a) slope of AB
From A(-5,-4) to B(-3,-3): run 2 right, rise 1 up.
slope =\boldsymbol{\tfrac{1}{2}}
(b) slope of AC
From A(-5,-4) to C(1,-1): run 6 right, rise 3 up.
slope =\tfrac{3}{6}=\boldsymbol{\tfrac{1}{2}}
(c) slope of AD
A(-5,-4) to D(5,1): run 10, rise 5.
\tfrac{5}{10}=\boldsymbol{\tfrac{1}{2}}
(d) slope of BC
B(-3,-3) to C(1,-1): run 4, rise 2.
\tfrac{2}{4}=\boldsymbol{\tfrac{1}{2}}
(e) What do you notice?
All four pairs give the same slope, \tfrac{1}{2}. The slope is a property of the whole line, not of the particular pair of points chosen.

Horizontal and Vertical Line Segments

Two special cases deserve their own attention: segments that run straight across (horizontal) and segments that run straight up and down (vertical).

Example 3

Horizontal and vertical segments

Each segment below has endpoints with integer coordinates.
(a) Calculate the slope of each segment.

slope of MN
M(-4,3) to N(2,3) is horizontal: rise =0, run =6.
\tfrac{0}{6}=\boldsymbol{0}
slope of RS
R(1,4) down to S(1,-3) is vertical: rise =-7, run =0.
\tfrac{-7}{0} is undefined
slope of PQ
P(-3,-2) to Q(4,-2) is horizontal: rise =0, run =7.
\tfrac{0}{7}=\boldsymbol{0}
slope of TU
T(-2,4) down to U(-2,-4) is vertical: rise =-8, run =0.
\tfrac{-8}{0} is undefined
(b) Complete the statements.
Horizontal line segments have a slope of 0.
Vertical line segments have a(n) undefined slope.
Horizontal: rise =0, and 0\div\text{run}=0, so the slope is 0. Vertical: run =0, and we cannot divide by zero, so the slope is undefined.
Ask the calculator. 0÷6ENTER gives 0, but (−)7÷0ENTER gives DIVIDE BY 0 ERROR: there is no number to give.

Why 0, and why undefined?

Keep the run and flatten the rise to 0, or keep the rise and squeeze the run to 0, and watch what happens to \tfrac{\text{rise}}{\text{run}}.

Positive Slope, Negative Slope

Reading a graph from left to right, a line either climbs, falls, or stays flat. The sign of the slope tells us which.

Example 4

A line which rises from left to right has a positive slope.

Steepness: the Size of the Slope

The sign of a slope tells the direction of a line; the size of the slope tells how steep it is. To compare steepness, ignore the signs and compare the sizes.

The size of a slope m is written |m| (read “the absolute value of m”), so |-5|=5 and |2|=2. A line with slope -5 is steeper than a line with slope 2, because 5>2: it simply falls instead of rising.
Example 5

Ordering lines by steepness

(a) Four lines have slopes -5, 2, -\dfrac{1}{2} and \dfrac{1}{4}. List the slopes in order from the least steep line to the steepest line.
Compare the sizes: |-5|=5, |2|=2, \left|-\tfrac12\right|=\tfrac12, \left|\tfrac14\right|=\tfrac14.
Least steep to steepest: \boldsymbol{\tfrac14,\ -\tfrac12,\ 2,\ -5}.
The line with slope -5 is the steepest even though -5 is the smallest number.
(b) Which line is steeper: one with slope -\dfrac{3}{4}, or one with slope \dfrac{2}{3}? Explain.
\left|-\tfrac34\right|=\tfrac34=0.75 and \left|\tfrac23\right|\approx0.67.
Since 0.75>0.67, the line with slope \boldsymbol{-\tfrac34} is steeper (it falls, while the other rises).
(b) as decimals. Compare the sizes: 3÷4ENTER and 2÷3ENTER. Leave the negative sign out: steepness ignores it.
6

Slope in the Real World

Slope is how a builder describes a roof, how an engineer describes a road or a ramp, and how a graph shows how fast one quantity changes compared with another. A grid laid over a picture gives a slope estimate, provided two points on the sloped surface can be identified precisely.

Example 6

The slope of a roofline

A grid has been placed over a sketch of a roof. A chimney blocks part of the roofline to the left of the peak.

(a) Use the grid to determine the slope of the roofline to the right of the peak.
Count from the peak (4,6) to the visible eave (8,1): run =4 right, rise =-5 (down 5).
slope =\tfrac{-5}{4}=\boldsymbol{-\tfrac{5}{4}} (the roofline falls from left to right).
(b) Could this same grid reliably be used to find the slope of the roofline to the left of the peak, behind the chimney? Explain.
No. A slope read from a grid needs two points whose coordinates can be read exactly. The chimney covers the lower part of the left roofline, so the position where that roofline ends can only be guessed, and the visible part passes through no grid point except the peak; an error of even one unit changes the calculated slope.
(c) Builders describe a roof’s pitch as a positive rise for every 12 units of run: for example, a “6 in 12” roof rises 6 for every 12 across. State the pitch of the right-hand roofline this way.
Pitch ignores the sign: the roof drops 5 for every 4 across, and \tfrac{5}{4}=\tfrac{15}{12}.
The pitch is “15 in 12”: a steep roof.

Slope as a rate of change

When the axes measure real quantities, the rise and the run have units, so the slope does too: (units on the vertical axis) per (unit on the horizontal axis). A slope with units is called a rate of change. A positive rate means the quantity is increasing; a negative rate means it is decreasing. Two everyday rates of change:

A road’s grade is its slope written as a percent: an 8\% grade rises (or falls) 8 m for every 100 m of horizontal run, a slope of \tfrac{8}{100}.
Building codes require a wheelchair ramp to have a slope of no more than \tfrac{1}{12}: at least 12 cm of run for every 1 cm of rise.
Example 7

Slope as a rate of change

(a) The graph shows a car’s distance from home during a highway trip.
(i) Determine the slope of the graph, including its units. (ii) What does the slope tell you?
(i) From (0,0) to (3,240): rise =240 km, run =3 h.
slope =\dfrac{240\text{ km}}{3\text{ h}}=\boldsymbol{80\text{ km/h}}.
(ii) The car travels 80 km every hour: the slope is its speed.
(b) The graph shows the volume of water in a tank as it drains.
(i) Determine the slope of the graph, including its units. (ii) What does the negative sign mean?
(i) From (0,120) to (4,60): rise =-60 L, run =4 min.
slope =\dfrac{-60\text{ L}}{4\text{ min}}=\boldsymbol{-15\text{ L/min}}.
(ii) The volume is decreasing: the tank loses 15 L every minute.

Working Backward from a Given Slope

The relationship runs in reverse too. Given a point and a target slope, step off the rise and the run to plot the line; given a slope and one of rise or run, solve for the other.

!
A negative slope has one negative part. -\tfrac{2}{3} can be stepped as \tfrac{-2}{3} (down 2, right 3) or as \tfrac{2}{-3} (up 2, left 3). Both land on the same line.
Example 8

From a slope back to a line, and to a missing measure

(a) Draw a line segment on the grid which passes through the point (-3,-4) and has a slope of \dfrac{3}{2}. The segment must be long enough to cross both the x-axis and the y-axis. Then write the coordinates of three other points on the segment which have integer coordinates.
Tap the grid to plot points (tap a point again to remove it).
Three other points with integer coordinates:
Rise over run: from (-3,-4) each run of +2 pairs with a rise of +3.
(-3,-4)\to(-1,-1)\to(1,2)\to(3,5)
Three other integer points: \boldsymbol{(-1,-1)}, \boldsymbol{(1,2)}, \boldsymbol{(3,5)}.
(b) A line segment has a slope of -\dfrac{5}{6} and a rise of 20. Calculate the run as an exact value.
\text{slope}=\dfrac{\text{rise}}{\text{run}}, so \text{run}=\dfrac{\text{rise}}{\text{slope}}:
\text{run}=\dfrac{20}{-\frac{5}{6}}=20\times\left(-\dfrac{6}{5}\right)=-\dfrac{120}{5}=-24
Check: \dfrac{20}{-24}=-\dfrac{5}{6} ✓. The run is \boldsymbol{-24}.
(b) on the calculator: run = rise \div slope, with the fraction in brackets: 20÷((−)5÷6)ENTER gives -24. To check, 20÷(−)24ENTER2ndF◂▸D shows −5┘6, which means -\tfrac{5}{6}.

A missing rise or run: scale the slope

The slope is the small triangle; the segment is the big one. Find how many times bigger, and scale the other part by the same amount.

✓

Key ideas

Slope =\dfrac{\text{rise}}{\text{run}}. Rise: up +, down -. Run: right +, left -. Count squares from one endpoint to the other and write the fraction in lowest terms.
The same everywhere. Any two points on a line give the same slope: slope triangles slide and scale, and the fraction stays equal. That value is the slope of the line.
Four kinds (read left to right): rising positive, falling negative, horizontal 0, vertical undefined. Steepness is the size |m|: slope -5 is steeper than slope 2.
Real world and backward. Slope with units is a rate of change (80 km/h, -15 L/min); pitch is rise per 12 of run. Given a slope, step off rise and run to plot points, or scale it to find a missing rise or run.
