The whole unit in one lesson. Each question asks for a tool: slope, intercepts, domain and range, length or midpoint. Today: choose the right one, then combine them to classify a quadrilateral, find the point that is off a line, and classify a triangle.

The Distance Formula

Extension / Foundation
The distance between P(x_1,y_1) and Q(x_2,y_2) is d_{PQ}=\sqrt{(x_2-x_1)^2+(y_2-y_1)^2}.

A horizontal segment has equal y-coordinates and a vertical segment has equal x-coordinates; for either one the formula collapses to a single subtraction: the positive difference of the two x-coordinates, or of the two y-coordinates.

Exact or decimal? Leave lengths exact (\sqrt{40}=2\sqrt{10}) unless asked to round. To see the decimal, √40= shows 6.32455532, about 6.32.

JK=|9-3|=6   LM=|13-3|=10

The longest segment is \boldsymbol{LM}, with length 10.
(b) Determine the exact distance between the points P(2,7) and Q(7,-5).

The Midpoint Formula

Extension / Foundation
For a segment joining P(x_1,y_1) and Q(x_2,y_2), the midpoint is the average of the two x-coordinates paired with the average of the two y-coordinates: M\left(\dfrac{x_1+x_2}{2},\ \dfrac{y_1+y_2}{2}\right).

Run backwards (given the midpoint and one endpoint), each coordinate gives an equation that is solved for the missing endpoint.

!
Add, then halve. Subtracting the coordinates and halving gives half the run and half the rise: a step, not a point. That was Hunter B’s mistake in the warm-up.

Extension

M=\boldsymbol{(-1,-3)}
(b) The point M(2,5) is the midpoint of segment EF. If E(-3,1), determine the coordinates of F.

\dfrac{-3+x}{2}=2\ \Rightarrow\ -3+x=4\ \Rightarrow\ x=7
\dfrac{1+y}{2}=5\ \Rightarrow\ 1+y=10\ \Rightarrow\ y=9

Slope, Parallel Lines, and Perpendicular Lines

The slope of the segment joining P(x_1,y_1) and Q(x_2,y_2) is m=\frac{y_2-y_1}{x_2-x_1}.

A rising line has a positive slope and a falling line a negative slope; a horizontal segment has slope 0, and a vertical segment has an undefined slope, since its run is 0. The size of the slope, |m|, measures steepness, and when the axes carry units the slope is a rate of change (for example, km/h).

!
Same order, top and bottom. If the top is y_2-y_1, the bottom must be x_2-x_1. And it is rise over run, never run over rise.

Parallel and perpendicular

Two segments are parallel when m_1=m_2, and perpendicular when m_1\times m_2=-1, that is, when each slope is the negative reciprocal of the other. A horizontal line and a vertical line are also perpendicular, even though their slopes cannot be multiplied.
A slope as a fraction. Divide, then 2ndF◂▸D turns the decimal into a reduced fraction: (−)4÷10=2ndF◂▸D shows -\frac{2}{5}.
Think The perpendicular slope to -\frac35 is \frac53, not -\frac53 and not \frac35. Why do you need both the flip and the sign change?
Turning a slope triangle 90° swaps the rise and the run (the flip) and points one of them the other way (the sign change). Check with the product: \left(-\frac35\right)\left(\frac53\right)=-1, but \left(-\frac35\right)\left(-\frac53\right)=1 and \left(-\frac35\right)\left(\frac35\right)=-\frac{9}{25}.
Example 3

Slope, and the parallel and perpendicular tests

(a) Determine the slope of the segment joining A(-2,5) and B(4,-7).
m=\dfrac{y_2-y_1}{x_2-x_1}=\dfrac{-7-5}{4-(-2)}
=\dfrac{-12}{6}=\boldsymbol{-2}
(b) Segment GH joins G(1,2) and H(5,10). Segment JK joins J(-3,4) and K(1,-4). Determine whether GH and JK are parallel, perpendicular, or neither.
m_{GH}=\dfrac{10-2}{5-1}=\dfrac{8}{4}=2
m_{JK}=\dfrac{-4-4}{1-(-3)}=\dfrac{-8}{4}=-2
Not equal, so not parallel. The product is 2\times(-2)=-4\neq-1, so not perpendicular.
GH and JK are neither.
(c) Determine the slope of a line perpendicular to a segment whose slope is -\dfrac{3}{5}.
The perpendicular slope is the negative reciprocal: flip \dfrac{3}{5} and change the sign.
m=\boldsymbol{\dfrac{5}{3}}, since \left(-\dfrac{3}{5}\right)\left(\dfrac{5}{3}\right)=-1.

Characteristics of Linear Graphs

A table of values is linear when \dfrac{\text{change in }y}{\text{change in }x} is constant, and that constant is the slope.
The x-intercept is where y=0. A slanted line has domain \{x\mid x\in\mathbb{R}\}; a vertical line x=a has domain \{a\}.
The y-intercept is where x=0. A slanted line has range \{y\mid y\in\mathbb{R}\}; a horizontal line y=b has range \{b\}.

A segment or a real situation has a restricted domain and range. A line has one intercept (horizontal, vertical, or through the origin), two intercepts, or infinitely many (an axis).

!
Unequal steps in x? The table can still be linear. Divide each change in y by its change in x before you decide.
Example 4

A linear relation from a table

After a warm spell begins, the depth of snow on a deck, d (cm), is recorded every 2 hours.

(a) Show that the relation is linear, and state the slope with its units.
Equal steps of 2 h, and d drops 6 cm each time: linear.
slope =\dfrac{-6\text{ cm}}{2\text{ h}}=\boldsymbol{-3} cm/h: the snow melts 3 cm every hour.
(b) Determine both intercepts and explain what each means.
d-intercept 48: 48 cm of snow when the warm spell begins.
t-intercept: 48\div3=\boldsymbol{16}: the snow is gone after 16 hours.
(c) State the domain and range of this situation.
From t=0 until the snow is gone at t=16:
Domain \{t\mid 0\le t\le16,\ t\in\mathbb{R}\}, Range \{d\mid 0\le d\le48,\ d\in\mathbb{R}\}.
(d) The horizontal line through (5,-2) extends forever. State its slope, its intercept(s), and its domain and range.
Slope 0; one intercept, the y-intercept \boldsymbol{-2} (no x-intercept).
Domain \{x\mid x\in\mathbb{R}\}, Range \{-2\}.

Combining the Tools: Classifying a Quadrilateral

Compute the slope and the length of all four sides. If both pairs of opposite sides are parallel and equal in length, the quadrilateral is a parallelogram. If in addition one pair of adjacent sides is perpendicular, every angle is a right angle and the parallelogram is a rectangle. All four sides equal makes it a rhombus, and a rhombus with a right angle is a square.
!
The most complete description. A square is also a rectangle, a rhombus and a parallelogram. When asked what a shape is, name the most specific one that every test supports.

Combining the Tools: Testing for Collinearity

Points lying on the same straight line are collinear. If P, Q and R are collinear then m_{PQ}=m_{QR}=m_{PR}, and showing that any two of those slopes are equal is enough.

To find which of four points does not belong, compute the slope from one fixed point to each of the other three; the point producing the odd slope is the one off the line. If the fixed point is itself the odd one out, all three slopes will be different: then repeat with a different fixed point.

Think Why is showing m_{PQ}=m_{PR} enough? Couldn’t two segments have the same slope and still be on different lines?
Different segments with equal slopes are parallel, and parallel lines never meet. But PQ and PR share the point P, so they can’t be two separate parallel lines: they are the same line through P.
Example 6

Finding the point that does not belong

Consider the points A(-6,-4), B(-2,2), C(0,5), and D(4,-8). Three of these four points are collinear, and one point does not belong on that line.

(i) Explain, in words, an algebraic strategy for identifying which point is not collinear with the other three.
Pick one point as a fixed reference (say A) and compute the slope from A to each of the other three points. Collinear points all give the same slope from A, so two of the three slopes will be equal and one will differ.
The point producing the odd slope is the one that is not collinear with the other three. (If A itself is the odd point, all three slopes from A differ; then repeat from a different reference point.)
(ii) Carry out your strategy to determine which point is not collinear with the other three.
m_{AB}=\dfrac{2-(-4)}{-2-(-6)}=\dfrac{6}{4}=\dfrac32
m_{AC}=\dfrac{5-(-4)}{0-(-6)}=\dfrac{9}{6}=\dfrac32
m_{AD}=\dfrac{-8-(-4)}{4-(-6)}=\dfrac{-4}{10}=-\dfrac25
m_{AB}=m_{AC}=\dfrac32, so A, B and C lie on one line. m_{AD}\neq\dfrac32.
D is the point that is not collinear with the other three.

Combining the Tools: Classifying a Triangle

Side lengths classify a triangle: three equal sides is equilateral, exactly two equal sides is isosceles, and no equal sides is scalene.
The converse of the Pythagorean theorem then tests for a right angle: if the square of the longest side equals the sum of the squares of the other two sides, the angle opposite the longest side is 90^\circ. The perpendicular slope test m_1\times m_2=-1 on the two shorter sides gives the same verdict.
Example 7

Side lengths, and a right angle two ways

Triangle DEF has vertices D(-4,1), E(2,-1), and F(4,5), plotted below.

(i) Extension Determine the exact length of each of DE, EF and DF, and classify \triangle DEF by its side lengths.
DE=\sqrt{(2-(-4))^2+(-1-1)^2}=\sqrt{36+4}=\sqrt{40}=\boldsymbol{2\sqrt{10}}
EF=\sqrt{(4-2)^2+(5-(-1))^2}=\sqrt{4+36}=\sqrt{40}=\boldsymbol{2\sqrt{10}}
DF=\sqrt{(4-(-4))^2+(5-1)^2}=\sqrt{64+16}=\sqrt{80}=\boldsymbol{4\sqrt5}
Exactly two sides are equal (DE=EF=2\sqrt{10}), so \triangle DEF is isosceles.
(ii) Show, in two different ways, that \triangle DEF contains a right angle, and name the vertex at which it occurs.
Converse of the Pythagorean theorem. The longest side is DF=4\sqrt5, so test it against the other two: DE^2+EF^2=40+40=80 and DF^2=(4\sqrt5)^2=80. The two agree, so the angle opposite DF, the angle at E, is 90^\circ.
Perpendicular slopes. m_{DE}=\dfrac{-1-1}{2-(-4)}=\dfrac{-2}{6}=-\dfrac13 and m_{EF}=\dfrac{5-(-1)}{4-2}=\dfrac{6}{2}=3. Their product is \left(-\dfrac13\right)(3)=-1, so DE\perp EF at E.
\triangle DEF is an isosceles right triangle with the right angle at E.

(a) Three of these points lie on the same straight line and one does not. Explain how you could algebraically determine which point is not collinear with the other three.

16. (Multiple Choice) Consider AB joining A(7,-5) and B(-3,-5), and CD joining C(2,-10) and D(2,0). Which one of the following statements about these line segments is true?
AB and CD have the same slope and are equal in length.
AB and CD have the same slope and are unequal in length.
CD has a length of 10 units and a slope of zero.
AB and CD have the same midpoint and are equal in length.
AB is horizontal: m_{AB}=0, AB=|7-(-3)|=10. CD is vertical: m_{CD} undefined, CD=|0-(-10)|=10.
Midpoint of AB=(2,-5); midpoint of CD=(2,-5). Same midpoint, equal lengths. Option C is the trap: CD’s slope is undefined, not zero. Answer: D.
17. (Multiple Choice) Line segment AB joins A(-3,6) and B(3,-9); line segment PQ joins P(8,2) and Q(-4,-6). Which of the following statements is correct?
The midpoint of AB has an x-coordinate greater than the midpoint of PQ.
The midpoint of AB has a y-coordinate greater than the midpoint of PQ.
The midpoints of AB and PQ are the same point.
The line segment joining the midpoints is horizontal.
Midpoint of AB=\left(\dfrac{-3+3}{2},\dfrac{6+(-9)}{2}\right)=(0,-1.5); midpoint of PQ=\left(\dfrac{8+(-4)}{2},\dfrac{2+(-6)}{2}\right)=(2,-2).
-1.5>-2, so AB’s midpoint is higher. (The x-coordinates give 0<2; the midpoints differ; the joining segment is not horizontal.) Answer: B.
18. (Multiple Choice) Which of the following most completely describes quadrilateral PQRS from question 4?
rectangle
square
rhombus
parallelogram
From question 4, both pairs of opposite sides are parallel and adjacent sides are perpendicular, so every angle is a right angle. Lengths: PQ=\sqrt{2^2+6^2}=2\sqrt{10} and QR=\sqrt{9^2+(-3)^2}=3\sqrt{10} are unequal, so it is not a rhombus or a square. Answer: A.
19. (Multiple Choice) Triangle GHJ has vertices G(-5,2), H(1,-6), and J(7,2). Which of the following most completely describes \triangle GHJ?
scalene, with no right angle
isosceles, with no right angle
isosceles, with a right angle
equilateral
GH=\sqrt{36+64}=10, HJ=\sqrt{36+64}=10, GJ=\sqrt{144}=12: exactly two sides equal, so isosceles. GH^2+HJ^2=200\neq144=GJ^2, so there is no right angle (equivalently m_{GH}=-\dfrac43, m_{HJ}=\dfrac43, product -\dfrac{16}{9}\neq-1). Answer: B.
20. (Numerical Response) The points A(6,1) and B(-4,7) from question 15 lie on a circle, with AB passing through the centre. The area of the circle, to the nearest whole number, is ____.
AB is a diameter, so r=\dfrac{AB}{2}. AB=\sqrt{10^2+(-6)^2}=\sqrt{136}, so r^2=\dfrac{136}{4}=34.
Area =\pi r^2=34\pi\approx106.81, so 107.
21. (Numerical Response) PQ is the diameter of a circle whose centre is R. If Q(5.4,3.2) and R(9.1,7.9), then the x-coordinate of P is ____.
R is the midpoint of PQ: \dfrac{P_x+5.4}{2}=9.1\ \Rightarrow\ P_x+5.4=18.2\ \Rightarrow\ P_x=\boldsymbol{12.8}.
22. Using the points of the Written Response, determine algebraically, without using the midpoint formula, that B(1,1) is the midpoint of line segment AD.
A, B and D are collinear, so B lies on segment AD. On a straight line, the point that is the same distance from each endpoint is the midpoint, so it is enough to show AB=BD using the distance formula.
AB=\sqrt{(1-(-8))^2+(1-10)^2}=\sqrt{9^2+(-9)^2}=\sqrt{162}=9\sqrt2
BD=\sqrt{(10-1)^2+(-8-1)^2}=\sqrt{9^2+(-9)^2}=\sqrt{162}=9\sqrt2
AB=BD=9\sqrt2 and A, B, D are collinear, so B is the midpoint of AD.

Key ideas

Pick the tool from the question. How steep, which way, parallel, perpendicular, collinear: slope. Equal sides, how far: length. Where’s the middle: midpoint. Where does it cross: intercepts.
Slope m=\frac{y_2-y_1}{x_2-x_1}: horizontal 0, vertical undefined, steepness |m|. Parallel m_1=m_2; perpendicular m_1\times m_2=-1.
Linear graphs: constant \frac{\Delta y}{\Delta x}; x-intercept where y=0, y-intercept where x=0; domain and range are the shadows. One, two or infinitely many intercepts.
Combine: quadrilateral = slopes of all four sides (and lengths); collinear = slopes from one fixed point; right angle = slopes multiply to -1 or c^2=a^2+b^2.
