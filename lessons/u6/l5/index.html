Characteristics of Linear Graphs

A linear relation is a relation whose graph is a straight line. Today we read the key characteristics of such a graph (its slope, its intercepts, and its domain and range) and decide from a table of values whether a relation is linear at all.

I can…
  • decide from a table of values whether a relation is linear, and state its slope (with unequal x-steps, I divide first)
  • state the x- and y-intercepts of a line from its graph or from a table, and explain what they mean in a real situation
  • state the domain and range of a line, a horizontal or vertical line, a segment and a real situation, in set notation
  • explain why a line has one, two or infinitely many intercepts, and sketch a line with given characteristics
  • match graphs with descriptions using the sign of the slope and the intercepts

Warm-up

Low Battery

At noon, Mia and Leo forget their chargers. Each phone drains steadily. (tap a ? to fill it in)

1
What will each phone read at 3:00?
Mia 45% (down 15 each hour); Leo 25% (down 5 each hour).
2
How many percent does each phone lose every hour? Is it the same every hour?
Mia loses 15% per hour and Leo 5% per hour: the same drop every hour, so each pattern is linear.
3
At what time was Mia’s phone at 100%?
11:20 am. From 100% to 90% is 10 points, and at 15 points per hour that takes \tfrac{10}{15}=\tfrac23 h =40 min before noon.
!
The big one. Mia’s phone has more than twice the charge of Leo’s. If neither stops, whose phone hits 0% first? Guess first.
A Mia’s
B Leo’s
C They die at the same moment
A: Mia’s phone. Mia: 90\div15=6 hours, so it dies at 6:00 pm. Leo: 40\div5=8 hours, so 8:00 pm. The fuller phone dies first because it drains 3 times as fast.
B looks only at the starting value (“less charge dies first”) and ignores the rate. C guesses that the bigger charge and the faster drain cancel out. They don’t: 6 h for Mia, 8 h for Leo.
+
Early finishers. At what time do the two phones show the same charge?
5:00 pm, both at 15%: 90-15(5)=40-5(5)=15.

Check your own phone: how much battery has it lost since you woke up?

1

Is It Linear? Tables of Values

On a straight line the slope is the same everywhere (Lesson 3), so equal steps in x always produce equal changes in y. To test a table of values:

Equal x-steps
Find the change in y from each entry to the next.
Linear when all of these changes are the same.
Unequal x-steps
Divide each change in y by the matching change in x.
Linear when this quotient is always the same.
Either way, the constant value \dfrac{\text{change in }y}{\text{change in }x} is the slope of the line: its rate of change.
!
Check the x-row first. When the x-steps are unequal, the changes in y are unequal even on a straight line. Divide before you decide.
Example 1

Linear or not?

Decide whether each table of values represents a linear relation. If it does, state the slope.

(a)
x goes up in steps of 1. Changes in y: +2,\ +2,\ +2,\ +2: all equal.
Linear, slope =\dfrac{2}{1}=\boldsymbol{2}.
(b)
x goes up in steps of 1. Changes in y: +1,\ +3,\ +5,\ +7: not equal.
Not linear (the graph curves upward).
(c)
The x-steps are not equal (+2,\ +3,\ +4), so divide: \tfrac{-4}{2}=-2,\ \tfrac{-6}{3}=-2,\ \tfrac{-8}{4}=-2.
Linear, slope \boldsymbol{-2}. (The y-changes alone, -4,\ -6,\ -8, look unequal: a trap.)

The uneven-steps trap

Bigger steps in x make bigger changes in y, even on a straight line. Every step hides a slope triangle: what has to match is rise ÷ run, not the rise alone.

2

Intercepts

The x-intercept is the x-coordinate of the point where a graph crosses the x-axis; there, \boldsymbol{y=0}.
The y-intercept is the y-coordinate of the point where a graph crosses the y-axis; there, \boldsymbol{x=0}.
An intercept may be reported as a single number or as a point: “x-intercept 4” or “the point (4,\ 0)”. In a table of values, look for the entry where y=0 (the x-intercept) and the entry where x=0 (the y-intercept). In a real situation the intercepts usually have a meaning: the vertical intercept is often the starting value, and the horizontal intercept is often the moment the quantity reaches zero.
Example 2

Intercepts from a graph and from a table

(a) For the line graphed at the left, state the x-intercept, the y-intercept and the slope.
Crosses the x-axis at (-3,\ 0): x-intercept \boldsymbol{-3}.
Crosses the y-axis at (0,\ 2): y-intercept \boldsymbol{2}.
From (-3,\ 0) to (0,\ 2): run 3, rise 2, slope \boldsymbol{\tfrac23}.
(b) Show that this table represents a linear relation, then state its intercepts.
x steps by 2; y changes by -3 every time, so it is linear (slope -\tfrac32).
Entry with x=0: y-intercept \boldsymbol{3}. Entry with y=0: x-intercept \boldsymbol{2}.

Intercepts hiding in a table

A table shows the intercepts when it contains an entry with x=0 or y=0. When it doesn’t, a linear table can be continued: keep the same change going until you reach the zero.

3

Domain and Range of a Linear Relation

Recall from the relations unit: the domain is the set of all x-values of a relation and the range is the set of all y-values.

Slanted line
Continues forever in both directions, so every real number is used.
D \{x\mid x\in\mathbb{R}\}
R \{y\mid y\in\mathbb{R}\}
Horizontal, y=3
Every x, but only one y-value.
D \{x\mid x\in\mathbb{R}\}
R \{3\}
Vertical, x=-2
Only one x-value, but every y.
D \{-2\}
R \{y\mid y\in\mathbb{R}\}
Segment or real situation
Restricted domain and range, read from the endpoints, e.g.
\{x\mid 0\le x\le 6,\ x\in\mathbb{R}\}

(d) An office water cooler starts the week with a full 18 L jug. The graph shows the amount of water left as staff drink it at a steady rate.

(i) State the two intercepts and explain what each one means.
Vertical intercept \boldsymbol{18}: the jug holds 18 L at the start (day 0).
Horizontal intercept \boldsymbol{6}: the jug is empty after 6 days.
(ii) Determine the slope, with units, and explain what it means.
From (0,\ 18) to (6,\ 0): \dfrac{-18\text{ L}}{6\text{ days}}=\boldsymbol{-3} L per day: the staff drink 3 L each day.
(iii) State the domain and range.
The graph only runs from day 0 to day 6, and from 18 L down to 0 L. Let d be the day and V the litres of water left.
Domain \{d\mid 0\le d\le 6,\ d\in\mathbb{R}\}, Range \{V\mid 0\le V\le 18,\ V\in\mathbb{R}\}.

Reading a graph of a real situation

In a story the graph has a start and an end. The intercepts are where the story starts and where it runs out, the slope is the rate (with units), and the domain and range stop at the endpoints.

How Many Intercepts Can a Line Have?

Summary: the number of intercepts of a line
Two interceptsslanted, not through the origin: one x- and one y-intercept
One intercepthorizontal, not the x-axis: a y-intercept only
One interceptvertical, not the y-axis: an x-intercept only
One interceptslanted through the origin: both intercepts are 0, one point (0,\ 0)
Infinitely manythe x-axis (y=0) or the y-axis (x=0)
Every line crosses at least one axis, so every line has at least one intercept.

Rotate a line and slide it around the grid, and watch what its intercepts, slope, domain and range do.

Think Could a line have zero intercepts? Try to draw one.
No. To miss the x-axis a line must be horizontal, and every horizontal line crosses the y-axis. To miss the y-axis it must be vertical, and every vertical line crosses the x-axis. A line can’t be both, so it always meets at least one axis.

Counting intercepts: three questions

Ask the questions in this order and the count falls out. Each chip is one of the five summary lines.

Example 4

Sketching lines with a given number of intercepts

On each grid, sketch a line with the given characteristics.

(a) x-intercept 3 and y-intercept -2. State its slope.
Through (3,\ 0) and (0,\ -2): run 3, rise 2, slope \boldsymbol{\tfrac23}.
(b) Exactly one intercept, at the point (0,\ 4).
A line with a y-intercept but no x-intercept must be horizontal: y=4 (slope 0).
(c) Exactly one intercept, and a negative slope.
A slanted line with only one intercept must pass through the origin; e.g. through (0,\ 0) and (1,\ -2).
(d) Infinitely many intercepts.
The y-axis itself (shown), or the x-axis: every point on it is an intercept.

Example 4 check: try a line, then fix it

A first try often has one intercept too many. Rotate or slide it until it fits every condition.

✓

Key ideas

Is it linear? Equal x-steps: the changes in y must all be the same. Unequal steps: divide, \tfrac{\text{change in }y}{\text{change in }x} must be constant. That constant is the slope, the rate of change.
Intercepts. x-intercept where y=0; y-intercept where x=0. In a table, find (or continue to) the entry with a 0. In a story: the start and the moment it runs out.
Domain and range. Slanted line: \{x\mid x\in\mathbb{R}\}, \{y\mid y\in\mathbb{R}\}. Horizontal y=3: range \{3\}. Vertical x=-2: domain \{-2\}. Segments and stories: read the endpoints.
How many intercepts? Two (slanted, not through the origin), one (horizontal, vertical, or through the origin), or infinitely many (an axis). Never zero. Match graphs using the slope sign first, then the intercepts.
