About the course

Math 10C is the first course on two of Alberta’s high school math routes. It builds the algebra, number sense, graphing and measurement that every later course leans on: factoring, exponents, linear relations, functions, systems of equations and right-triangle trigonometry.

Most students come to Math 10C from Math 9; some take the optional Math 15 first. A high school diploma needs 100 credits, including a 20-level math.

Success in math is mostly persistence. Everyone in this room can do well in this course by doing the practice, asking questions early and learning from mistakes. Wrong answers are where the learning happens.

A typical class

  1. Arrive ready: laptop charged, calculator, pencil and paper on your desk.
  2. Warm-up on the board while attendance is taken.
  3. Lesson: I teach from the class website. Take notes on paper, or print the notes booklet for the unit.
  4. Practice on the website: new numbers every time, hints when you’re stuck, and your level on each outcome.
  5. Exit slip almost every day: a short check on today’s outcome, written on the website.

Classes run about 88 minutes, bell to bell. The class calendar on the website shows each day’s lesson and every test date, and it is always up to date.

What you will learn: the 18 outcomes

From Alberta Education’s Mathematics 10–12 Program of Studies (2008). These outcomes are what you are assessed on.

How you are assessed

Math 10C uses outcomes-based assessment. Your mark shows which outcomes you can do and how well, not how many points you collected. For each outcome you earn an indicator. On a unit test, it reads straight from three boxes of questions:

Beginning boxthe core skill
Progressing boxthe skill in typical questions
Mastery boxharder, multi-step and new situations

From outcomes to your report card

Part marks and unit grades

Questions can earn part marks: a small slip keeps ⅔ of the point, and a bigger but reasonable mistake keeps ⅓. Your unit grade is the average of the unit’s outcomes. Exit slips check one or two outcomes from the day’s lesson, at the highest level you answer correctly.

Your final mark

Your report card mark comes from your grades on the 18 outcomes. Everything you do in class adds evidence for the outcomes it covers: unit tests, exit slips, practice in class, the game Lorebound played in class, and the three cumulative exams that every Math 10C class at Croxford writes. When I make a judgment call on an outcome, I use what I’ve seen you do in class.

Tests and exit slips

Unit tests and exit slips are written on the class website, in class, on your laptop. They pop up full screen when I launch them, and every student gets their own numbers. Tests end at the end of class. Leaving the test window, switching tabs or leaving full screen submits your work and locks the test; only I can unlock it.

Once I release the results, you see your answers, the correct answers and a worked solution for each question.

Missed a test?

Make it up at the Test Centre in the Learning Commons, Fridays 2:15–3:45. Fill out the missed-assessment form first. If you miss your Test Centre time, you lose that chance.

Practice and due dates

Each lesson’s practice is on the website, with a due date on the class calendar. Due dates set the pace, so you’re ready for the exit slip and the unit test. Practice isn’t marked down for being late, since marks come from work in class, but unfinished practice matters for retests.

Retests: earn another try

A retest is earned with practice, and your best result counts, so a retest can only help.

  1. Do the practice. Finish every required question in the unit without seeing the solution. (Saw one? Redo a new version without it.)
  2. Request a retest. The button appears on that unit on your home page.
  3. Write it in class. I open a retest just for you, with new numbers, while the class works.

Want another retest after that? Finish all the extra practice in the unit first.

Medals. Every outcome has a medal beside your name on the website, from Rust (Not Meeting) up to Diamond (Mastery). It shows the best level you have reached from practice in class or a unit test.

What to bring every day

  • Laptop or Chromebook, charged. Croxford is a bring-your-own-device school; ask the office about borrowing a Chromebook for the year.
  • Scientific calculator with SIN, COS and TAN keys, like the TI-30XIIS (about $15–20). Not your phone. A few loaners are available in class.
  • Pencil, eraser and paper or a notebook for notes (or your printed notes booklet).

Calculators

Math 10C follows Alberta Education’s calculator rules. Graphing calculators are not used. A calculator is not allowed if it gives exact answers such as 3√2 instead of decimals, stores programs or notes, or has anything wireless (for example the Casio fx-991 or TI-30XS MultiView). Not sure about yours? Bring it in and we’ll check it.

Expectations

  • Be on time and ready to start when the bell goes.
  • Phones off and away during class (the provincial rule). If you leave the room, your phone stays with me; take the hall pass, one person at a time.
  • Compassionate Cavs: be kind, encourage each other, and listen when someone is teaching. This is a room where you can be wrong out loud.
  • Own your learning. Missed a class? The lesson, notes and practice are on the website: catch up before the next class and ask me about anything unclear.

Getting help

Asking early is the biggest predictor of doing well. Ask during work time in class (that’s what it’s for), use the hints in the practice, or come see me at lunch or before or after school. The only time I won’t help is during a test.

Academic integrity

Every assessment must show your own thinking. On tests and exit slips, any of these is cheating:

  • talking to or communicating with anyone, by any means;
  • using notes, devices, websites or apps that weren’t allowed;
  • using AI or photo-solver apps for any part of the work;
  • sharing questions or answers with students who haven’t written yet.

On practice, copying someone’s work costs you the practice. AI and solver apps are fine for checking your work, not for producing it.

When academic dishonesty happens, I will talk with you and contact home. It can mean a 0 on that assessment, or an alternate assessment of the outcome at my discretion. Serious or repeated cases are referred to administration.

For parents and guardians

Marks are posted in PowerSchool by outcome: an indicator for each outcome a unit test or exit slip has covered, plus each unit grade.

For the day-to-day picture, ask your student to show you their home page on the class website: their level on every outcome, their medals, what’s due and the next test date.
