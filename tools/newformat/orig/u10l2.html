Converting Units Within and Between SI and Imperial

SI prefixes are powers of ten, so a metric conversion is a shift along a number line, a proportion, or a chain of unit-analysis fractions. Imperial units each carry their own multiplier (12, 3, 5280, 1760), and crossing to SI needs a fact like 1 in = 2.54 cm. Same methods, new facts: estimate first with a benchmark, then convert exactly.

I can…
  • convert within SI by counting steps on the metric number line, with a proportion (including 1:n map scales) and with unit analysis, even for rates like m/s
  • use the imperial facts (12 in = 1 ft, 3 ft = 1 yd, 5280 ft = 1760 yd = 1 mi) and the imperial–SI facts, choosing an exact factor when there is one
  • convert a mixed measure (like 8 ft 5 in) to a single unit, and back again with a division and a remainder
  • chain conversions with proportions and with unit analysis, and build a fact such as 1\text{ m}\approx39.37\text{ in}
  • convert between imperial and SI in both directions, estimate first with a benchmark, and check the direction of my answer
M1 Solve problems that involve linear measurement, using: SI and imperial units of measure; estimation strategies; measurement strategies.
M2 Apply proportional reasoning to problems that involve conversions between SI and imperial units of measure.

SI Review: the Prefix Chart and the Metric Number Line

Every SI measurement is a base unit (the metre for length, the gram for mass, or the litre for volume) carrying a prefix that scales it by a power of ten.

The core prefixes for this unit are kilo through milli. Giga, mega, micro and nano are Extension: notice that each of their steps is a factor of 1000, not 10.

!
Two different “m”s. On its own, m is the metre. In front of another unit, m means milli (a thousandth): mm, mg, mL. And capital M is mega, a million.

The metric number line

Arranging the prefixes from largest to smallest produces a metric number line on which each single step is one power of ten. Moving right (toward smaller prefixes) multiplies by 10 per step; moving left (toward larger prefixes) divides by 10 per step.

Kilometres to millimetres is 6 positions right (k is 3 steps left of the unit and m is 3 steps right of it), so 1\text{ km}=10^{6}\text{ mm}=1\,000\,000\text{ mm}; the reverse trip is 6 positions left, so 1\text{ mm}=10^{-6}\text{ km}.

Count steps, move the point. Each step on the line moves the decimal point one place, in the same direction you moved on the line. Right: the number gets bigger (smaller units, so you need more of them). Left: the number gets smaller.
A whole number’s decimal point is hiding at its end: 58\,400=58\,400. Fill any empty places with zeros.
Example 1

Counting steps on the metric number line

Use the metric number line to convert each measure.

(a) 4.6\text{ m} to cm
m \to cm is 2 steps right: \times10^{2}.
4.6\times100=\boldsymbol{460}\text{ cm}
(b) 58\,400\text{ mg} to kg
mg \to kg is 6 steps left: \div10^{6}.
58\,400\div1\,000\,000=\boldsymbol{0.0584}\text{ kg}
(c) 36\text{ mm} to m
mm \to m (the unit) is 3 steps left: \div10^{3}.
36\div1000=\boldsymbol{0.036}\text{ m}
Check the point you moved. The number line tells you what to multiply or divide by; the calculator confirms it. (b): 58400÷12ndEE6ENTER gives 0.0584. EE types “\times10^{\square}” for you.
!
Count every tick, even the unlabelled ones. The unit itself is a position too: mg to kg is 6 steps, and mm to m is 3. (Between M and k, and between m and \mu, there are empty positions as well: that is why each Extension step is \times1000.)

Proportional Reasoning and Map Scales

A proportion is a statement that two ratios are equal. Given either a known conversion (such as 1\text{ m}=100\text{ cm}) or a ratio supplied by the question, set up

\dfrac{a}{b}=\dfrac{c}{d}

with matching units in matching positions, then cross-multiply to isolate the unknown. Scale drawings, map scales, recipes and currency exchange rates are all ratios, so each can be solved with a proportion.

Map scales. A map scale written as a ratio, 1:n, means 1 unit on the map stands for n of the same unit on the ground: on a 1:50\,000 map, 1 cm on the map is 50\,000\text{ cm}=500\text{ m} on the ground. Convert to a convenient unit only at the end.
Example 2

A map scale written as a ratio, 1:n

A map of central Alberta has a scale of 1:250\,000.

(a) Two towns are 18.4 cm apart on the map. How far apart are they on the ground, in kilometres?
\dfrac{1\text{ cm (map)}}{250\,000\text{ cm (ground)}}=\dfrac{18.4\text{ cm}}{x}
x=18.4\times250\,000=4\,600\,000\text{ cm}
4\,600\,000\text{ cm}\times\dfrac{1\text{ m}}{100\text{ cm}}\times\dfrac{1\text{ km}}{1000\text{ m}}=\boldsymbol{46}\text{ km}
(b) Two lakes are 32 km apart. How far apart are they on the map, in centimetres?
Same unit on both sides first: 32\text{ km}=32\times1000\times100=3\,200\,000\text{ cm}.
\dfrac{1}{250\,000}=\dfrac{x}{3\,200\,000}
x=\dfrac{3\,200\,000}{250\,000}=\boldsymbol{12.8}\text{ cm}
Big zeros: count them as you type. (a): 18.4×250000ENTER gives 4 600 000 cm, then ÷ 100 ÷ 1000 gives 46 km.

Unit Analysis (Dimensional Analysis)

Proportional reasoning suits a single conversion. When several conversions must be chained, unit analysis (also called dimensional analysis) is usually faster, and it is the method used in science courses. The set-up is

\text{required}=(\text{given})\times(\text{conversion}),

with each conversion written as a fraction arranged so the unwanted unit cancels. A conversion fraction such as \dfrac{1000\text{ m}}{1\text{ km}} has the same length on top and bottom, so it is just a fancy way to write 1.

For example, to convert 640 mL to L using 1\text{ L}=1000\text{ mL},

\dfrac{640\text{ mL}}{1}\times\dfrac{1\text{ L}}{1000\text{ mL}}=\dfrac{640}{1000}\text{ L}=0.64\text{ L}\qquad(\text{mL cancels, leaving L}).
!
The unit you don’t want goes on the bottom. If a unit appears on top and on the bottom it cancels. If your answer’s units come out as something strange, like \dfrac{\text{km}^2}{\text{m}}, a conversion fraction is upside down: flip it.

Converting a rate: two units change at once

A rate like 64 m/s already is a fraction: metres on top, seconds on the bottom. Each unit that changes needs its own conversion fraction, and each one is arranged to cancel where that unit sits.

Example 3

Chaining two conversion factors, and converting a rate

(a) Complete the unit analysis below to convert 5.3 km to mm. Show the cancellation.
\dfrac{5.3\text{ km}}{1}\times\dfrac{1000\text{ m}}{1\text{ km}}\times\dfrac{1000\text{ mm}}{1\text{ m}}=
km cancels against km; m cancels against m; only mm is left.
=5.3\times1000\times1000=\boldsymbol{5\,300\,000}\text{ mm}
(b) Priya needs to convert 64 m/s to km/min. State the two conversions she needs, then complete her work, showing the cancellation of units.
\dfrac{64\text{ m}}{\text{s}}\times\dfrac{\phantom{00}\text{km}}{\phantom{00}\text{m}}\times\dfrac{\phantom{00}\text{s}}{\phantom{00}\text{min}}=
1\text{ km}=1000\text{ m} and 1\text{ min}=60\text{ s}.
\dfrac{64\text{ m}}{\text{s}}\times\dfrac{1\text{ km}}{1000\text{ m}}\times\dfrac{60\text{ s}}{1\text{ min}}: m and s cancel.
=\dfrac{64\times60}{1000}=\boldsymbol{3.84}\text{ km/min}

Key Imperial and Imperial–SI Conversion Facts

In Sections 1–3 every SI conversion was a power of ten. Imperial units don’t work that way, and moving between the two systems needs a conversion fact such as 1 in = 2.54 cm. These are the facts used in the rest of the lesson and in the assignment. Facts marked (exact) are true by definition; the others are rounded.

Imperial to Imperial
1 foot (ft) = 12 inches (in) exact
1 yard (yd) = 3 feet (ft) exact
1 mile (mi) = 5280 ft exact
1 mile (mi) = 1760 yards (yd) exact

Tip. Use an exact factor when you can (for example, divide by 2.54 rather than multiply by 0.3937), so your answer does not differ from the key by one unit in the last place.

Benchmarks for checking (mental math)

Before converting, estimate with a round benchmark; after converting, compare. If the two are far apart, look for a multiply/divide mix-up.
1 in ≈ 2.5 cm1 ft ≈ 30 cm1 m ≈ 1.1 yd1 mi ≈ 1.6 km (so 5 mi ≈ 8 km)1 kg ≈ 2.2 lb
Also check the direction: converting to a smaller unit gives a bigger number (1 ft → about 30 cm), and converting to a bigger unit gives a smaller number.

Converting Within the Imperial System

Imperial conversions chain the imperial-to-imperial facts exactly the way 1 m = 100 cm is used in SI, except that there are four different multipliers to keep straight (12, 3, 5280, 1760), so write down which fact is being used at each step.

A mixed measure such as 8 ft 5 in uses two units at once.
Mixed → single unit: multiply the big unit by its multiplier, then add the extra small units. Single unit → mixed: it is a division with a remainder: the quotient is the big units, the remainder is what’s left over.
Example 4

Mixed imperial measures, both directions

Convert each of the following.

(a) 8 ft 5 in to inches
8\times12=96 in, plus the extra 5 in:
96+5=\boldsymbol{101} in
(b) 6 yd 1 ft to feet
6\times3=18 ft, plus the extra 1 ft:
18+1=\boldsymbol{19} ft
(c) 2 mi 410 yd to yards
2\times1760=3520 yd, plus 410 yd:
3520+410=\boldsymbol{3930} yd
(d) 68 in to feet and inches
68\div12=5 remainder 8.
5 ft 8 in
(e) 97 ft to yards and feet
97\div3=32 remainder 1.
32 yd 1 ft
(f) 5555 yd to miles and yards
3\times1760=5280 and 5555-5280=275.
3 mi 275 yd
A remainder on the calculator. 5555÷1760ENTER gives 3.156…: 3 whole miles. The remainder is what’s left after those 3 miles: 5555−3×1760ENTER gives 275. (Don’t turn the decimal 0.156… into yards by guessing: it is a fraction of a mile, not 156 yd.)

Chaining Conversions with Proportional Reasoning and Unit Analysis

Both methods from Sections 2–3 still work when more than one multiplier is needed. Unit analysis is usually faster, because the units themselves show which factors to multiply by.

Proportional reasoning. One proportion per fact, solved for the unknown:
\dfrac{1\text{ ft}}{12\text{ in}}=\dfrac{3\text{ ft}}{x}\ \Rightarrow\ x=36\text{ in}
Unit analysis. Multiply by conversion factors (each worth 1) so the unwanted units cancel:
\dfrac{3\text{ ft}}{1}\times\dfrac{12\text{ in}}{1\text{ ft}}=36\text{ in}
Example 5

The same chained conversion, two ways

Convert 0.6 miles to inches using each method.

Proportional Reasoning
Miles to feet, using 1 mi = 5280 ft:
\dfrac{1\text{ mi}}{5280\text{ ft}}=\dfrac{0.6\text{ mi}}{x}\Rightarrow x=0.6\times5280=3168\text{ ft}
Feet to inches, using 1 ft = 12 in:
\dfrac{1\text{ ft}}{12\text{ in}}=\dfrac{3168\text{ ft}}{y}\Rightarrow y=3168\times12
y=\boldsymbol{38\,016}\text{ in}
Unit Analysis
Chain both factors so the unwanted units cancel:
\dfrac{0.6\text{ mi}}{1}\times\dfrac{5280\text{ ft}}{1\text{ mi}}\times\dfrac{12\text{ in}}{1\text{ ft}}
mi cancels, then ft cancels, leaving in.
=0.6\times5280\times12=\boldsymbol{38\,016}\text{ in}
Both methods agree: 0.6 mi = 38 016 in.
The whole chain in one line. Multiply by every number on top and divide by every number on the bottom (here the bottoms are all 1): 0.6×5280×12ENTER

Watch the units cancel

A conversion factor is a fraction equal to 1, such as \dfrac{2.54\text{ cm}}{1\text{ in}} or \dfrac{1\text{ in}}{2.54\text{ cm}}. Pick the one whose bottom unit matches the unit you want to get rid of; it cancels like a common factor. When only the unit you want is left, multiply the tops and divide by the bottoms.

Example 6

Building the conversion fact 1\text{ m}\approx\ ?\text{ in}

A student uses unit analysis to find how many inches are in one metre. The required unit is inches per metre. Part of her work is shown.

\dfrac{100\text{ cm}}{1\text{ m}}\cdot\dfrac{1\text{ in}}{2.54\text{ cm}}=
(a) State which units cancel, and what unit is left.
cm appears once on top and once on the bottom, so cm cancels:
\dfrac{100}{2.54}\cdot\dfrac{\text{in}}{\text{m}}: inches per metre, the required unit.
(b) Calculate the answer to two decimal places.
\dfrac{100}{2.54}=39.370078\ldots
\approx\boldsymbol{39.37}
(c) Write the answer as a completed conversion fact: 1 m ≈   in.
1\text{ m}\approx\boldsymbol{39.37}\text{ in}
Tops over bottoms. 100\times1 on top, 1\times2.54 on the bottom: 100÷2.54ENTER

Converting Between the Imperial and SI Systems

The imperial–SI facts move a measure directly between systems.

Mixed imperial measure? Single unit first. When a measurement mixes imperial units (feet and inches), convert it to a single imperial unit first, then apply the imperial–SI fact.
Going to a mixed answer (feet and inches), work the other way round: convert to inches first, round to the nearest inch, then split into feet and inches with a remainder.

Estimate first, then convert

A benchmark gives a quick mental answer before you touch the calculator. If the exact answer lands far from the estimate, something went wrong, usually a multiply/divide mix-up. Ask: is the new unit bigger or smaller? Should the number go up or down?

Use a benchmark to estimate each conversion mentally. Then convert exactly and compare.

(a) 5 ft 9 in to metres (two decimal places)
Estimate: 5 ft 9 in = 69 in; 69\times2.5\approx172 cm ≈ 1.7 m.
Exact: 69\times2.54=175.26 cm = 1.75 m.
Close to the estimate, so the answer is reasonable.

Math HistoryAir Canada 143 · 23 July 1983

The Gimli Glider

Flight 143, Montreal to Edmonton, needed 22 300 kg of fuel. The fuel gauges were broken, so the crew converted litres of fuel by hand, multiplying by 1.77. But 1.77 is pounds per litre, not kilograms.

The jet left with 22 300 lb, about 10 100 kg: less than half. Both engines stopped at 41 000 ft. The pilots glided 17 minutes to an old air force base at Gimli, Manitoba, and all 69 on board survived.

What should the factor have been, in kilograms per litre? Use 1 kg ≈ 2.2 lb.
A kilogram is heavier, so the number gets smaller: 1.77\div2.2\approx\boldsymbol{0.80} kg per litre (the real value: 0.803).

Example 10

A conversion that must be rounded up

Before digital storage, industrial and educational films were shot on 35 mm film stock, which is now being transferred onto digital media. If 1200 feet of film fit on one archival DVD, determine how many DVDs are needed to store 1650 metres of film footage.

Convert 1650 m to feet using 1 ft = 0.3048 m:
1650\div0.3048=5413.3858\ldots\approx5413.39\text{ ft}
Divide by the capacity of one DVD: 5413.39\div1200=4.511\ldots
Four DVDs leave footage with nowhere to go, so the count must be rounded up: 5 DVDs are needed.
!
Count things? Think before you round. You can’t buy 4.511 DVDs, and 4 isn’t enough. When the answer counts containers, trips or boxes that must hold everything, round up, even if the decimal is small.
Exact factor, then divide by the capacity. 1650÷.3048ENTER then ÷1200ENTER.

Key ideas

SI: powers of ten. Number line: count the steps, move the decimal point one place per step (right \times10, left \div10). Map scale 1:n: same unit on both sides, convert at the end.
Every imperial step has its own multiplier. 12 in = 1 ft, 3 ft = 1 yd, 5280 ft = 1760 yd = 1 mi. Mixed → single: multiply and add. Single → mixed: divide; the remainder is the small unit.
Unit analysis, in any system. Required = given \times conversion fractions, each with the unwanted unit on the bottom so it cancels; rates change two units. Across systems: single imperial unit first, and prefer the exact factors (1 in = 2.54 cm, 1 ft = 0.3048 m). Containers? Round up.
Estimate first, then check. 1 in ≈ 2.5 cm, 1 ft ≈ 30 cm, 1 m ≈ 1.1 yd, 5 mi ≈ 8 km, 1 kg ≈ 2.2 lb. Smaller unit ⇒ bigger number. Far from the estimate? Look for a multiply/divide mix-up.
